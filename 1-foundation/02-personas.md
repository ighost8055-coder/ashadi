# Kapruka — Personas Document

> **Purpose:** This is the avatar layer. Personas are built on *behaviour, situation, and emotion* — NOT demographics. We do not write "32-year-old woman in Colombo." We write the situation that produces a specific pain and the emotion that produces a specific desire, because that is what writes the ad.
>
> **The model — broad persona, sharp ad.** A persona is a **broad relationship situation** (Romantic, Parents, Kids, Siblings, Friends, Social/Obligation, Corporate — each in two geographies, plus Self-Purchaser). Keep it broad: it's the *exploration unit*, and over-narrowing it would choke research and idea generation. **You sharpen at the ad, not at the persona** — by layering optional modifiers (Occasion · Emotion). One ad picks one sharp combination; the next ad picks another. The strategy stays broad; the ads get sharp.
>
> **The angle menu.** The detailed sub-situations below (the former A1.1–B1.3) are no longer a fixed taxonomy you must pick from — they are the **angle menu**: a palette of specific pains, desires, fears, and situations to draw from when you sharpen a broad persona into a single ad. Pull what fits; ignore the rest.
>
> **Not every ad names a pain.** Pain-led ("name the fear, disarm it") is *one* angle type. An ad's job can also be positive/desire-led (joy, love, pride), broad-brand, or occasion-led. Every ad needs **one clear job** — pain is one option, not the rule. (See `02b-fears-objections.md`.)
>
> **Status:** v1 draft — `[CONFIRM]` fields need Kapruka input. Pain/desire/fear are written as hypotheses to be validated against reviews, support tickets, and real customer language.
>
> **The foundation model — five dimensions.** Each persona is understood through **persona (the situation) · fear · objection · desire · why-they-buy.** Objections live in `02b-fears-objections.md`; the buying-driver lens in `02d-why-people-buy.md`; the desire/pain/fear/misconception/belief bank in `02e-desire-engine.md`. *(The old behavioural sub-avatar layer was retired 2026-07-05 — archived under `_archive/`.)*

# LINE A — GIFTING

The buyer is not the consumer. Every persona below is anchored to Kapruka's authority: **reliable, dated, island-wide delivery — from anywhere in the world.** The recipient relationship determines the emotional weight.

---

## A1 — INTERNATIONAL → LOCAL (Diaspora Sender)

**Who:** Sri Lankans living abroad (top markets Australia · USA · Canada · UAE) sending to people back home. Highest value, highest moat, highest emotional intensity. They cannot verify delivery themselves, so trust is the entire game.

> **⚖️ But weight it right (confirmed 2026-07-05): diaspora is only ~30% of gifting volume; Local→Local (A2) is ~70%.** Diaspora is the highest-*value* segment, not the highest-*volume* one. Generate ~70% Local / ~30% Diaspora for gifting — don't let the emotional pull of the diaspora story crowd out the local majority.

**Universal driver across all A1 recipients:** *I can't physically be there. The gift is my stand-in. If it fails, I've failed the person — from a distance, where I can't fix it.*

### A1 → Family

#### A1.1 — Sending to Parents (Mother / Father)
- **Situation:** Working abroad to support the family; misses parents' birthdays, anniversaries, Mother's/Father's Day, religious occasions. Often the parents are ageing.
- **Reason behind purchase:** Emotional — guilt, duty, love, "showing I haven't forgotten where I came from."
- **Pain:** Being absent for the people who raised them. Watching parents age from far away. Not being able to do the small in-person things (bring cake, sit with them).
- **Desire:** To feel present in their parents' day. To be the child who "still takes care of them" despite distance.
- **Fear:** The gift arrives late, broken, or looks cheap → reads as neglect. Or worse: sending money to a relative to "handle it" and it never happening properly.
- **Specific note:** Mothers and Fathers differ — Mother gifts skew warmth/sentiment (flowers, cake, sarees `[CONFIRM]`), Father gifts skew respect/practical (`[CONFIRM]` what sells). Mother's Day and parents' birthdays are peak triggers.

#### A1.2 — Sending to Own Children (in SL, raised by relatives/spouse)
- **Situation:** Parent working abroad while child is raised back home by spouse/grandparents. Extremely emotionally loaded — common in Gulf migration.
- **Reason:** Emotional — guilt of absence, wanting the child to feel loved and not forgotten.
- **Pain:** Missing the child growing up. Birthdays spent on a video call. Fear the child feels abandoned.
- **Desire:** To make the child's day special *from afar* — to be the parent who "showed up" even when they physically couldn't.
- **Fear:** A late or wrong gift on a child's birthday is devastating — there's no second chance on the day. This avatar's fear is the most acute of all.
- **Specific note:** Toys, cakes, birthday surprises. The "delivered exactly on the day" promise is everything here.

#### A1.3 — Sending to Siblings (Brother / Sister)
- **Situation:** Lateral family bond, often less obligation-heavy, more "thinking of you."
- **Reason:** Mix of emotional and habitual.
- **Pain:** Drifting apart due to distance; missing the casual closeness.
- **Desire:** To stay connected, mark their occasions, be a present sibling.
- **Fear:** Lower intensity than parents/kids, but still "looking like I forgot."

### A1 → Romantic Partner

#### A1.4 — Diaspora Romantic Gifter (to spouse/partner in SL)
- **Situation:** Married or in a relationship split across countries (one abroad for work). Common and emotionally intense.
- **Reason:** Emotional — love, reassurance, maintaining intimacy across distance.
- **Pain:** Loneliness of long-distance; fear the relationship cools; missing anniversaries and "couple moments."
- **Desire:** To keep the romance alive, to surprise, to make the partner feel chosen despite distance.
- **Fear:** Gift fails on the anniversary → partner feels neglected → distance feels bigger. High stakes.
- **Specific note:** Anniversaries, Valentine's, "just because" surprises. Flowers, cake, jewellery `[CONFIRM]`.

### A1 → Other Recipients

#### A1.5 — Occasion-Obligation Gifter Abroad
- **Situation:** Can't attend a wedding, funeral, almsgiving, new baby, housewarming back home. Social/cultural obligation to "send something appropriate."
- **Reason:** Practical + social duty (more than personal emotion).
- **Pain:** Social embarrassment of not marking the occasion; cultural pressure; not knowing what's appropriate from afar.
- **Desire:** To fulfil the obligation correctly and be seen to have done the right thing.
- **Fear:** Sending something inappropriate, late, or to the wrong place — public/family embarrassment.
- **Specific note:** Strong for funerals/almsgivings (**confirmed 2026-07-04 — Kapruka serves this; handle sensitively**), weddings, new baby. Highly occasion-triggered, low ongoing.

### A1 → Friends

#### A1.6 — Diaspora Friend Gifter (to a friend back home)
- **Situation:** Abroad, marking a friend's birthday, congratulations (new job, baby, graduation), or "thinking of you" across distance. Lower obligation than family, more affection-and-habit.
- **Reason:** Emotional — staying connected, not letting the friendship fade with distance.
- **Pain:** Friendships quietly drifting once you've left; missing the in-person moments (being at the birthday, the celebration).
- **Desire:** To stay present in a friend's life, surprise them, prove the distance hasn't changed anything.
- **Fear:** Lower stakes than family — but still "looking like I forgot" or letting the friendship cool.
- **Specific note:** Birthdays, congratulations, "just because." Strong fit for Friendship Day. Pairs well with positive/desire-led angles (joy, surprise) rather than fear-led.

> **Local mirror (A2 → Friends):** the same friend-gifting in-country, driven by convenience rather than distance — "celebrate them without the drive across town." Pull the same desires; swap distance-guilt for ease.

## A2 — LOCAL → LOCAL (In-Country Sender)

**Who:** Sri Lanka-based senders arranging delivery to someone else in SL. Lower emotional intensity than diaspora, but high *convenience and occasion-pressure* drivers. Trust still matters but they *could* do it in person — so the ad must sell why they shouldn't have to.

**Universal driver across A2:** *I want this handled well — on time, looking good — without me having to drive across town/country to do it myself.*

### A2 → Romantic Partner

#### A2.1 — Romantic Gifter (girlfriend/boyfriend, wife/husband)
- **Situation:** Marking anniversary, birthday, Valentine's, apology, or "just because" for a partner in SL.
- **Reason:** Emotional — love, romance, making an impression, sometimes guilt/apology.
- **Pain:** "I never know what to get them." Pressure to not be boring/predictable. Time-poor but wants to seem thoughtful.
- **Desire:** To impress, to be seen as romantic and thoughtful, to nail the occasion.
- **Fear:** Giving something boring/lazy → partner unimpressed. Forgetting the date. Looking like they didn't put effort in.
- **Angle goldmine:** "the gift that looks like you spent hours" / "what they *say* they want vs what they *actually* want." (The earlier worked example lives here.)
- **Specific note:** This is where "girlfriend gifting to boyfriend" sits. Recipients split further: gifting *to him* (he's hard to shop for, "don't get me anything") vs gifting *to her* (expectation is higher, stakes feel bigger).

### A2 → Family

#### A2.2 — Family Gifter (parent / child / sibling, local)
- **Situation:** Birthday/occasion for a family member in another part of SL, or simply wanting it delivered rather than handed over.
- **Reason:** Emotional + practical (love + convenience).
- **Pain:** Busy life, distance within the country, can't always visit.
- **Desire:** To show care, mark the occasion, stay close.
- **Fear:** Being the family member who forgot / sent something thoughtless.
- **Specific note:** Mother/Father/child/sibling each carry the same sentiment splits as A1 but with lower distance-guilt and more convenience-driver.

### A2 → Convenience / Situational

#### A2.3 — Last-Minute / Convenience Gifter
- **Situation:** Remembered late, busy at work, can't get to a shop or travel to deliver.
- **Reason:** Practical — speed and reliability dominate.
- **Pain:** Panic of having forgotten; no time; fear of showing up empty-handed or late.
- **Desire:** To make the problem disappear fast and still look thoughtful.
- **Fear:** It won't arrive in time → the whole point is missed.
- **Angle goldmine:** "Forgot? Still arrives tomorrow." Speed + reliability as the hero.

> **NOTE:** Corporate gifting (the former A2.4) is now its own line — **Line B — Corporate**, below. It is no longer a gifting persona.

# LINE B — CORPORATE (B2B / bulk / branded)  ✅ CONFIRMED KEY LINE (2026-06-20)

The buyer is a **company gifting at scale, within Sri Lanka (Local only)** — to its own **staff** or to its **customers/clients** — often many at once, branded/customised. Driver is **professional obligation and reputation**, not personal warmth. Own packs, own economics. **Two personas** below; bulk/branded/invoiced delivery is the line's *characteristic* (how), not a persona.

**Universal driver:** *I have to give something appropriate — often many of them — that reflects well on us, handled reliably at scale, and be done.*

**Universal fear:** *An inappropriate, cheap-looking, late, or inconsistent bulk order is a professional embarrassment.*

**The line's characteristic (proof needs on every corporate ad):** volume/bulk pricing, branding/customisation, invoicing, and Kapruka's dated reliable delivery *at scale*. Competitor signal: FNP runs a dedicated advertised "Corporate" lane.

#### B.1 — Customers / clients (company → customers)
- **Situation:** Thanking/retaining clients/customers (festive, deal-close, year-end). Reputation-bearing — the gift represents the company.
- **Desire:** Tasteful, on-brand, reliably delivered to many addresses on a date.
- **Fear:** A client gets something cheap/late → reflects on the relationship.

#### B.2 — Staff / employees (company → staff)
- **Situation:** Festive hampers, employee appreciation, onboarding, milestones — at volume.
- **Desire:** Consistent, fair, morale-building gifts delivered across the workforce.
- **Fear:** Inconsistent or low-effort gifts read as the company not valuing staff.

> **Bulk / branded / customised** is a *characteristic* of the whole Corporate line (logos, custom packaging, volume pricing, invoicing), applied to either persona — **not** a separate persona.
> **Boss / colleague / in-law** (an *individual* discharging a personal-professional obligation) is **not Corporate** — it lives in **Gifting · Social / Obligation**.

# LINE C — ECOM (SELF-PURCHASE)

The buyer is the consumer. Driver is **practical**. Emotion is minor; the ad wins on value, trust, selection, and ease. Kapruka's delivery moat still matters but now as *convenience/speed*, not *emotional reliability*.

## C1 — Local Self-Purchase

> **One persona, three angle facets.** B1.1–B1.3 are **not three personas** — they're three angles inside the single **Self-Purchaser** grid persona. Pull whichever fits the ad. *No product/offer advertising (SKUs, prices, deals) until the human flags it* — until then the Self-Purchaser runs on broad **value, trust, selection, and ease**, not specific offers.

#### B1.1 — The Value / Deal Shopper
- **Reason:** Practical — price-led.
- **Pain:** Worried about overpaying; comparison-shops; distrusts online prices.
- **Desire:** A clearly good deal, value for money, no buyer's remorse.
- **Fear:** Finding it cheaper elsewhere right after buying; getting ripped off.
- **What wins:** Price clarity, comparison, offers, "lowest price / best value" with proof.

#### B1.2 — The Convenience Shopper
- **Reason:** Practical — wants it without leaving home.
- **Pain:** No time/desire to go to shops; traffic; hassle.
- **Desire:** Order, pay, receive — frictionless.
- **Fear:** It won't arrive, slow delivery, complicated returns.
- **What wins:** Fast/reliable delivery, ease of ordering, island-wide coverage.

#### B1.3 — The Specific-Need Shopper
- **Reason:** Practical — came for one category (electronics, groceries, appliances, books — `[CONFIRM]` top categories).
- **Pain:** Wants the right item, in stock, trustworthy, delivered.
- **Desire:** Selection + availability + confidence it's genuine.
- **Fear:** Out of stock, fake/wrong product, no after-sales support.
- **What wins:** Selection breadth, in-stock, authenticity/warranty signals, ratings.

# OCCASIONS & TRIGGERS (cuts across all gifting avatars)

Occasions are not avatars — they are *triggers* that activate avatars and drive seasonal/product-first campaigns. Map campaigns to the calendar.

| Occasion | Avatars activated | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Birthdays | All gifting | Year-round baseline; "delivered on the day" hero |
| Anniversaries | Romantic (A1.4, A2.1) | High emotional stakes |
| Avurudu (Sinhala/Tamil New Year) | All, esp. diaspora & family | Tier-2 revenue peak (broadest all-persona) |
| Valentine's Day | Romantic | Peak romantic window |
| Mother's Day / Father's Day | A1.1, A2.2 | Strong diaspora-to-parent window |
| Christmas / religious festivals | All | Tier-2 revenue peak; Valentine's & Mother's Day are Tier-1 |
| Weddings | A1.5, A2.4 | Obligation gifting |
| New baby / housewarming | A1.5, A2.2 | Occasion-obligation |
| Funerals / almsgiving | A1.5 | **Served (confirmed 2026-07-04)** — sensitive, handle carefully |
| "Just because" / apology | Romantic | Non-occasion emotional gifting |

**General (non-occasion) gifts:** "thinking of you," surprise, reconnecting — these run year-round and aren't tied to the calendar. Useful for evergreen always-on packs.

# HOW TO USE THIS DOC (for the Strategist)

Start from one of the **four entry points** (see CLAUDE.md): persona-led, broad-brand, occasion/seasonal-led, or competitor-library. For a **persona-led** ad:

1. Pick a **broad grid persona** + geography (e.g., Kids — International → Local).
2. Open its **angle menu** entry above; pull the specific pain/desire/fear/situation that fits.
3. **Sharpen with optional modifiers** — layer an occasion (`03-occasion-calendar.md`) and an emotion. Use as many or as few as the ad needs; zero modifiers = a broader ad.
4. Decide the **job**: pain-led (name + disarm a fear), desire-led (joy/love/pride), or broad. *Not every ad names a pain.*
5. Anchor to Kapruka's **authority** (dated, reliable, island-wide, from-anywhere delivery) where it earns its place.

The **sharpened combination** is the ad — not the broad persona. "Diaspora parent → own child, birthday, terrified it won't arrive on the day, last-second panic" writes a completely different ad than "local boyfriend who thinks his girlfriend is hard to shop for." The persona stays broad so you can explore widely; the *ad* is where that specificity lives. **No product/offer ads** (SKU + price + deal) until the human flags one.
