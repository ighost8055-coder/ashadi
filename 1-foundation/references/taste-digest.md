## ⭐ THE VIBE RULE (applies to every ad — picking refs AND making creatives)

Kapruka ads must feel **social-media-worthy, fun, exciting, engaging, vibrant, aesthetic** — the kind of thing a real person stops scrolling for. The energy of the **iPhone-shot / social-native / editorial-design** look is wanted; the **sterile, generic, boring STOCK-photo** look is the #1 thing to avoid. Premium ≠ stiff — premium *and* alive at once is the bar (the brand's "premium + playful" fingerprint, pushed further toward scroll-stopping).

- **Lead with an idea / a hook of energy** — a clever concept, a surprising scale, a bold colour, a candid real moment. Flat catalogue shots are a fail even if "clean."
- **Vibrant, confident colour and composition** — alive, not muted-and-safe. (Conceptual product-hero surrealism and bold editorial typography are squarely in lane — see the swipe file's graphic-design bucket.)
- **Aesthetic-candid is allowed and encouraged** — a well-composed real/iPhone moment can beat a glossy studio set. Judge *vibe*, not polish.
- **Still honest, still on-brand** — the purple-and-gold world, the moat, true claims (below) all hold. This rule raises the *energy*, it doesn't loosen brand or honesty.
- The human's **Style Board** (`style-board/_style-board.md`) holds hand-picked preferred-style examples — read it to match the *vibe/energy* (taste only; never copy 1:1, never the original brand's marks, never an on-ad ref link).
- Full curation criteria + the scan bar live in `pinterest-curation.md` (this is the same standard, stated for the ad-making side).

## The observed patterns (grounded in the 3 references)

- **Background purple — flat, deep, saturated.** A rich royal/indigo purple ≈ Retail Purple `#340A5C`, used two ways: (a) a **solid flat fill** edge-to-edge (ref 3), or (b) a **flat purple band up top** sitting above a premium photographic scene (ref 1). No gradients in the graphic zones. The only depth is *inside* photography (warm gold lighting on ref 1's counter), never in the flat color.

- **A second background mode: soft cream.** For clean product comparison, Kapruka drops the purple and uses an off-white/cream `#F5F4EB`-type field (ref 2) with dark-purple type. So: **flat purple = bold/witty/emotional; cream = clean product showcase.**

- **Negative space — generous and deliberate.** Ref 2 is mostly empty cream around a centered headline and two products. Ref 3 gives the headline the whole top half. Even ref 1, which is prop-rich, keeps the props tidily organized on one counter plane rather than filling the frame. Breathing room is protected.

- **Headline — top, huge, ExtraBold, tight.** Poppins-style ExtraBold, very large, stacked 2–3 lines with tight line-height. **White on purple** (refs 1, 3); **dark purple/near-black on cream** (ref 2). Alignment varies — left (ref 3), centered (refs 1, 2). Short staccato phrases with periods for punch ("Grocery Shopping. Evolved.").

- **Yellow — surgical, never dominant.** Kapruka Yellow/gold `#FFC107` appears as exactly one of: a **single highlighted word** in the subhead ("**delivered**", ref 3), a **whole short subhead line** in gold ("Where every order ends with a smile", ref 1), a **thin underline divider** (ref 1), and always the **smile in the logo**. One yellow idea per creative. Gold also shows up naturally in props/ribbons/lighting.

- **Product styling — real photography, colour-matched to the brand.** Products are shot real and premium, then **tinted into the purple world**: purple macarons + purple-wrapped bouquet (ref 2), purple shopping bags and gold-ribboned gift boxes (ref 1). Cakes/flowers/gifts look fresh, abundant, giftable. Cleanly composited, never 3D/plastic.

- **People — used, and central.** Real, warm, relatable Sri Lankan people in 2 of 3. A friendly uniformed **service person** for trust (cashier ref 1; delivery rep ref 3) — white shirt + tartan tie + name tag, or purple branded uniform + cap. Premium but everyday, not glamour models. People carry both *trust* and *humour*.

- **What's deliberately absent:** gradients, heavy texture, neon or off-brand colours, clutter, cheap stock-photo feel, maximized type. Restraint is visible even in the busy one.

- **The "feels like Kapruka" tell (the signature):**
  1. **Royal purple world + a single yellow smile/highlight.**
  2. **The wordmark with the yellow "u"-smile**, top-center (white on purple, purple on cream), or bottom on a full-bleed photo.
  3. **Wit.** A clever, slightly cheeky concept — flowers as the "0 CAL treat," evolution gag — delivered with *premium* polish. Premium **and** playful at once is the brand's fingerprint.
  4. **Everything pulled into the purple-and-gold palette**, even the food and props.

## Extra notes for the Art Director
- **Playful hand-drawn accents** (small white sketch/motion doodles around figures, ref 3) are used *sparingly* for energy — fine in moderation, never clutter.
- **Two-mode decision:** witty/emotional/announcement → flat `#340A5C`; clean product showcase/comparison → cream. Pick the mode the concept calls for.
- **Humour is on-brand, not a risk.** Kapruka clearly leans into clever comparisons and gags. A witty hook backed by premium craft is squarely in their lane.

## How this maps to our current W25 concepts
- The text-led purple layouts (A2.3-01/02/04, A2.1-01/03) match **ref 3's flat-purple + big-white-headline + one-yellow-word** mode exactly. We're on-taste.
- The product-hero (A2.1-02 "Looks like you spent hours") could run **ref 2's cream-background clean-product mode** as easily as purple — worth offering both to the Designer.

## THE STYLE-BOARD TASTE (scanned from the 48-image board, 2026-07-05)

The full Style-Board (`style-board/_style-board.md` + `format-library.md`) expands the taste beyond the 3 old refs. The consistent fingerprint across all 48:

1. **One flat, saturated colour ground** for any graphic/conceptual ad — bold and ownable (the board uses red/blue/terracotta/yellow; **for Kapruka that ground is Retail Purple `#340A5C`**, cream for product grids). No gradients in flat zones.
2. **Huge, confident condensed/display type** — cream or white, stacked 2–3 lines, one word emphasised. The type *is* the design.
3. **Real photography only, two registers:** (a) **iPhone-candid** — handheld product, real light, a plain ground isolating the subject (bouquet over tarmac); (b) **editorial/cinematic food** — either **dark-moody chiaroscuro** (one spotlight, hand into the light) or **clean-minimal on white** with huge negative space. Never 3D/glossy/stock.
4. **One frozen detail carries the craving** — a caramel drip, a glaze pour, a glisten. Restraint over clutter.
5. **Native-UI devices read as content, not ads** — CAPTCHA, iOS alert, calendar block, faux-IG post, Photoshop layers, quiz. A major scroll-stopping lever; use the familiar chrome to make the ad feel native.
6. **Conceptual metaphors built from the product's own world** — phone-as-portal (delivery!), magnifier, torn-paper burst, landmark/doorway-as-frame. The idea carries the ad, not a slogan.
7. **Grids** — one product many ways, or a curated numbered set: cohesive palette, hairline rules, generous space, exactly ONE accent colour pop.
8. **Human hands / small human details** for warmth (many-hands abundance, a small fist gripping the product); **handwritten annotations** for a personal, curated-by-a-real-person feel.

> **The through-line:** *bold single colour + huge type + real (candid or cinematic) photography + one clever device/detail + disciplined restraint.* Premium **and** alive — never sterile, never cluttered. This is the bar for every Kapruka creative; the `format-library.md` turns each of these into a named, buildable format.

## ✅ A proven pattern (an option, not a rule) — "Style-Board Conceptual Poster" (human-picked, 2026-07-18)

Two human-approved concepts (`cannes-01` gifts-abroad, Prompts 3 & 7) landed strongly. The formula behind them:

**flat solid ground (Retail Purple `#340A5C` for bold/emotional, Soft Cream `#F5F4EB` for warm/light) · flat gold *conceptual shorthand* · ONE real Kapruka hero cut-out (attached asset, composited photoreal) · a one-line Ogilvy headline + exactly one yellow word · a one-second, ownable metaphor.**

- **The "conceptual shorthand" is the reusable part, and it changes per scenario** — a **landmark** silhouette for *abroad/geography* (Burj, Opera House, CN Tower); an **occasion** motif for *seasonal* (Avurudu lamp, Christmas star, Valentine's heart); a **category icon** for *everyday Ecom* (Kapruka already owns a set — the line-icon grid on the delivery van, "A world of choices"); a bulk/branded motif for *Corporate*. Landmarks are **abroad-only** — don't bolt them onto local/everyday concepts.
- **Loose, not strict.** This is one option in the kit, available to the Art Director, Ideator and Kelly to reach for **when a clean conceptual metaphor can be explained wordlessly by a flat icon** — NOT a mandate. The treatment-mix still governs (photoreal + graphic, varied per batch); most creatives won't be this. Don't force it; don't over-use it.
- Reference set: `3-output/reference-recreations/cannes-01b-…styleboard-set` (Prompts 11–16).

## ✅ Winning-ingredient checklist (from 4 human-confirmed winners, 2026-08-22)

> **Read this as a toolbox, not a template.** None of these four examples should get literally repeated (no future prompt needs a dog, a costume, or a gas station) — what matters is the *underlying ingredient* each one proves works. Pull the ingredient that fits a given concept; don't force all four into one ad, and don't treat any single example as a formula to clone.

**The four winners and what each one proves:**

1. **"You're allowed to send yourself a cake"** (handwritten note, real bakery, gloved hand) → proves **permission-giving copy beats benefit-stating copy.** The line doesn't describe the cake or the delivery — it removes a psychological barrier (self-purchase guilt). Ingredient: find the *permission* or *reframe* hiding behind the persona's fear/objection, not just the feature.

2. **"Nothing stops the delivery. Not even this."** (dog chasing the courier) → proves **a plain, simple sight gag directly illustrating the actual claim outperforms a clever metaphor for it.** No device needed — the joke and the reliability promise are the same object. Ingredient: when the real thing that happens on the ground (traffic, dogs, weather) is already funny, just show it — don't invent a metaphor to replace a true story.

3. **"Don't disappoint your girl on Aug 1"** (bride pumping gas in a wedding dress, sign held to camera) → proves **borrowing a recognizable internet-native genre (the protest-sign meme) earns attention the ad itself hasn't paid for.** Ingredient: absurd formality colliding with mundane task (wedding dress + gas pump) is a reliable comedy device — the gap between the two registers is where the laugh lives. **Caution:** this example's typo ("DISSAPOINT") works because it's a photographed real object — an imperfection in a real handwritten sign reads as authentic. That same typo generated by an AI image model reads as a rendering error, not charm — **never intentionally write a misspelling into an AI-generation prompt** hoping for this effect; it only works when the "imperfection" is genuinely real.

4. **Spider-Man costume delivery guy loading the van** → proves **zero text can be the strongest choice when the image is funny enough on its own** — this is the live, real-world proof of the Visual Comedy rule (rulebook §0). Ingredient: **a specific, local, slightly-imperfect detail** (the ordinary slippers, not proper shoes) is what makes an absurd costume gag feel real instead of staged — the specificity sells the authenticity, not the joke itself.

**The distilled ingredients list (pull from these, don't force all of them):**
- **A true insight beats a constructed metaphor.** If something real and already-true is funny or moving on its own (a dog chasing a courier, a permission-giving reframe), tell that story plainly before reaching for a device.
- **Borrow a recognizable genre when it fits.** Meme formats (protest signs, group chats, screen recordings) come pre-loaded with attention people already pay to that format — use this sparingly and only when it genuinely fits, not as a crutch.
- **Real imperfection reads as authentic; AI-generated imperfection reads as an error.** A genuine typo on a real photographed object works. The same instruction inside an AI-generation prompt does not — never intentionally engineer a "flaw" into generated text.
- **Local, specific, slightly-mundane detail sells authenticity harder than polish does** — the wrong footwear, the real street, the ordinary background clutter. Specificity is what makes a bold concept feel true rather than staged.
- **Silence is a valid, sometimes stronger choice.** Confirmed live in example 4 and codified as the Visual Comedy rule — don't add text to a joke that's already landing without it.
- **A reframe/permission-giving insight is a distinct, powerful copy move**, separate from a benefit claim or a twist — worth the Strategist and Copywriter actively reaching for it as its own tool, not just as a fallback.
