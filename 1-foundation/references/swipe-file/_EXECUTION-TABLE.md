> ## ⛔ WHAT THIS FILE IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT
>
> **THIS FILE ANSWERS: *how do I build the frame?*** Visual treatment · composition · text treatment · copy register.
>
> **⛔ IT DOES NOT SUPPLY IDEAS.** Ideas come from the **Recognition Brief** (`3-output/relatability/`, role 15) — real relatable events, jokes and situations the audience already lives. **Never mine these images for what an ad should be ABOUT.** They are borrowed execution for *our* idea, not borrowed ideas.
>
> *(The previous `_MECHANISM-REFERENCE-INDEX.md` conflated the two — it taught "mechanisms," which are idea-shaped. **That file is superseded by this one for execution purposes.**)*
>
> ## ⭐ THE COPY LESSON — the single most important thing in the whole set
>
> Look at what these headlines actually are: *"This is trash."* · *"They're jeans. Technically."* · *"We didn't invent anything."* · *"Same Day Delivery."* · *"___ happens"* · *"Uncomfortable / Comfortable"* · *"Because mixtapes still work."* · *"St Helens, your nearest Uber driver is 6 mins away."*
>
> **Every one is a plain, flat, spoken sentence. Not one is clever.** No puns, no wordplay, no double meanings, no poetry. **The cleverness is in the PICTURE; the words just say what is true.** Several are deliberately anti-dramatic — *"Technically."*, *"Either."*, *"Anything."*
>
> **This is Core Rule 15 proven by 38 award-winning examples: CLARITY OVER CLEVER.** If a line sounds like advertising, rewrite it until it sounds like a person talking.

# ⛔⭐ TABLE 0 · WHO IS IN THE FRAME, AND WHAT ARE THEY DOING

> **Added 2026-10-03.** The first version of this file catalogued **how the pictures are built** and never asked **what is happening in them.** That omission is what produced five batches of lifeless frames. **This table is the first thing to read.**

| | Count | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Two or more people, one acting on the other** | **~15 of 38** | the strongest images in the set, without exception |
| **One person, absurdly out of place** | ~8 | works when the absurdity carries the whole frame *(a racing driver in an armchair; a beekeeper eating a burger on a roof)* |
| **One person, composed, nothing happening** | ~4 | **the weakest images in the set** — including the one that carries an *"AI-GENERATED PERSON"* label |
| **No people at all** | ~11 | almost all are type-led posters or pure product/graphic pieces — **not emotional or comedic work** |

### ⛔ WHAT EVERY STRONG IMAGE HAS

**1 · A VISIBLE FACE, MID-EXPRESSION.** A girl **mid-scream** at a board game · a man **mid-lick** over someone else's pizza · an old couple **kissing badly** · a sunburnt man who looks **furious on holiday**. **Not one is composed.**

**2 · CONTACT AND CONSEQUENCE.** Two people collide and **the drink flies out of the bottle**. A tailor **feeds** a man mid-fitting. A man **drags** furniture while a baby wakes. A child **lunges** at a lunchbox while an arm enters from off-frame.

**3 · SOMEONE SLIGHTLY RIDICULOUS.** **⛔ Not one person in the 38 is dignified.** A man photographed from behind in denim hot pants. A dad meditating while his child destroys the attic. **In the strongest ones the brand's own customer is the butt of the joke** — VW mocks the man, KFC mocks the eater, Monopoly mocks its player.

**4 · THE PRODUCT IS ALLOWED TO BE A MESS.** Coca-Cola's *"Worth every drop"* runs over a bottle whose contents are **airborne**. A pizza slice is **half-eaten**. **Pristine reads as catalogue.**

> ### ⛔ THE TEST
> **Mid-event, never aftermath.** *Could this photograph have been taken one second earlier or later and been the same picture?* **If yes, nothing is happening** — and dust, empty rooms and objects-left-behind are the commonest way this goes wrong.

# TABLE 1 · VISUAL TREATMENT — how the picture is made

| Treatment | What it is | References | Build it like this |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V1 · FIELD** | Subject cut out on a flat or single-hue-gradient brand colour, edge to edge. **60–75% empty colour.** | **2** talabat · **11** B&Q · **15** Spotify turntable · **23** Burger King · **31**+**32** Fabrikant · **18** tiger (gradient) · **27** app | Flat field, no environment. Subject gets a **real contact/cast shadow on the field** — a floating cut-out is the clearest failure. Declare **flat** (graphic) or **gradient** (premium). |
| **V2 · OBJECT** | One hero object, studio-lit, plain neutral ground (not a brand colour). | **13** Nike · **16** Ghee | **Hard directional key**, crisp-edged shadows, high contrast. The idea lives in *what the object is* or *what's done to it*. Macro when material is the message. |
| **V3 · STREET** | A real moment, real place, real people. | **1** Coca-Cola · **3** Domino's · **4** KFC · **12** VW · **24**+**30** Spotify · **25** Road Lodge · **28** Monopoly · **29** Uber · **33**+**36** nib · **34** ESPN · **35** ParentSmile · **37** Uber Eats · **38** VW | **The light is a CHOICE** (Table 4), never a default. Candid or direct address. Environment must read as a real place someone was. |
| **V4 · CONSTRUCT** | Openly built image that never pretends to be a photograph — map, diagram, exploded view. | **14** McDonald's | **No camera line. No 8K.** Its power is that it is obviously designed. |
| **V5 · STAGE** | Photoreal subject in a deliberately unreal space; construction not hidden. | **7**+**26** talabat · **18** tiger · **22** Spotify macro | Subject lit photographically, situation impossible. **The funnier the idea, the straighter the craft.** |

# TABLE 2 · OVERALL COMPOSITION — how the frame is arranged

| Composition | What it is | References | Build it like this |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 · THE VOID** | One hero, **60–75% of the frame deliberately empty.** | **17** Aircel · **13** Nike · **34** ESPN · **2** talabat · **11** B&Q | Emptiness is **the primary construction method**, not restraint applied later. One hero, one headline, nothing else. If you can name three focal objects, cut two. |
| **C2 · CORNER LOCKUP + OPEN FRAME** | Type parked in one corner; the rest of the frame breathes. | **12** VW · **24**+**30** Spotify · **8** Target · **37** Uber Eats · **38** VW · **7** talabat | Type flush-left in **one** corner. ⛔ Never centre it and never spread it across two corners. |
| **C3 · THE SPLIT** | Frame divided; same subject, one variable changed. | **31**+**32** Fabrikant · **19** Singapore Kindness | **Identical scale, angle, light and pose on both halves.** Any drift and the comparison stops being a comparison. One plain label per side. |
| **C4 · THE GAP** | Headline carries a **literal blank with a drawn underline**; the subject sits **IN** the blank. | **33**+**36** nib · **11** B&Q | ⭐ **The subject must sit BETWEEN the quote marks, directly above the rule.** Rule is **short and thick**, on the type's baseline. Subject is **modest in a large calm frame.** |
| **C5 · TYPE IN DEPTH** | Giant word **behind the subject, in front of the background**, occluded by them. | **5** Ah! · **9** Superads · **20** Midea | Occlude **≤25%** of the word, **never its identifying letters.** Correct edge behaviour, no halo. ⛔ No scrim to rescue it. |
| **C6 · THE GRID / SERIES** | Repeated cells or panels; the jump between them is the structure. | **6** Make It Local · **11** B&Q ×3 · **23** Burger King | **Identical angle, light, scale and ground in every cell.** Mixed lighting is an instant reject. |
| **C7 · THE SHAPED CROP** | Brand colour field meets photo along **one confident curve or diagonal.** | **7**+**26** talabat · **23** Burger King | One edge, confidently placed. ⛔ Never shaped as the Kapruka mark itself. |
| **C8 · FULL-BLEED TEXTURE** | A single surface fills the entire frame; the type floats on it. | **22** Spotify macro | Macro scale means **the material realism floor becomes the entire ad.** |

# TABLE 3 · TEXT TREATMENT — how the type sits

| Text treatment | What it is | References | Build it like this |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T0 · NO TEXT** | Logo only. | — | Mandatory consideration on any comedic frame: **a joke needing a caption isn't landing yet.** |
| **T1 · CORNER STACK** | Heavy sans, **2–4 very short stacked lines, flush-left**, in one corner, lots of space around it. Lines often **end in full stops.** | **12** VW · **13** Nike · **8** Target · **2** talabat · **38** VW · **7** talabat | ⭐ **The full stop after a two-word line makes a fragment sound final.** This is the dominant pattern in the set. |
| **T1-P · + PRICE CHIP** | Corner stack plus product descriptor and a **real verified price** in a yellow chip. | *(human's denim reference)* | Price must be **real, current, verified** or the chip is dropped, not guessed. ⛔ No support sentence on top. |
| **T2 · TYPE IS THE IMAGE** | The words **are** the composition; a small object acts on them. | **11** B&Q · **14** McDonald's | ⭐ The twist is performed **by a physical object**, not a clever word — which is why it clears the no-wordplay rule. |
| **T3 · ONE HUGE WORD IN DEPTH** | Single short word at enormous scale, interleaved with the subject. | **5** Ah! · **9** Superads | Cap height ~25–30% of frame. |
| **T4 · SEQUENTIAL** | **One word per panel** across a stack; the sentence completes as the eye travels. | **6** Make It Local | 3-panel vertical stack or carousel. |
| **T5 · INLINE BRAND** | Logo sits **mid-sentence, as a word.** | **4** KFC | Composited attached asset at text scale. Still the frame's **one** logo. |
| **T6 · THE QUIET LINE** | One small plain informational string, flush, **deliberately under-scaled.** | **29** Uber · **3** Domino's | ⭐ It whispers on purpose — that's why it doesn't sound like advertising. **Minimum ~58px**, or it dies at thumbnail. |
| **T7 · PAIRED LABELS** | One plain word or phrase per half of a split. | **31**+**32** Fabrikant · **19** Singapore Kindness | **Typographically identical** on both sides. No claim, no benefit, no joke in the words. |

> ## ⛔ THE ABSOLUTE TEXT RULE — 38 out of 38
> **Not one reference uses a scrim, gradient wash, dark box, glow or drop shadow behind type.** **Legibility is ALWAYS solved by composition** — placing type on genuinely empty, genuinely contrasting ground, or interleaving it in depth. **If the type isn't readable, the composition is wrong — never add a panel.**

# TABLE 4 · LIGHT — a declared choice, never a default

| Light | Look | References |
|---|---|---|
| **HARD KEY** | One hard source, **crisp-edged shadows**, high contrast | **13** Nike · **16** Ghee |
| **DIRECT SUN** | Raking/overhead sun, **hard shadows, blown highlights**, real glare | **3** Domino's |
| **FLAT DAYLIGHT** | Overcast, even, **documentary plainness** | **12** VW · **34** ESPN |
| **SOFT WINDOW** | One large soft side source, low contrast | **35** ParentSmile · **24** Spotify |
| **FIELD LIGHT** | Lit to cut cleanly off a colour field, real contact shadow | **2** · **31** · **18** |
| **NIGHT / PRACTICAL** | Street and interior practicals, wet surfaces | **29** Uber · **26** talabat |

**⛔ The one surviving ban: flat frontal three-point ad lighting with a rim kicker.** **Hard light is not flat light** — hard light has direction and makes shadows; flat ad light kills them.

# TABLE 5 · THE COPY REGISTER — what to actually write

**Every headline in the set is one of five shapes. All five are plain speech.**

| Shape | Examples from the set | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| **R1 · THE FLAT STATEMENT** | *"This is trash."* · *"We didn't invent anything."* · *"Same Day Delivery."* | Say the thing. Full stop. **The picture supplies the surprise.** |
| **R2 · THE ONE-WORD HINGE** | *"They're jeans. **Technically**."* | Write the flattest literal version, then ask **which single word the picture could make land harder.** Change nothing else. One meaning only — **a hinge is not a pun.** |
| **R3 · THE GAP** | *"___ happens"* · *"You can't ___ if you don't run."* | A literal blank. **Only one word may plausibly fit**, and it is **never printed.** |
| **R4 · THE PLAIN LABEL** | *"Uncomfortable / Comfortable"* | One word per side. No claim, no benefit, no joke in the words. |
| **R5 · THE INFORMATION LINE** | *"St Helens, your nearest Uber driver is 6 mins away"* | Write it as a **system notification**, not a boast. ⛔ Every value must be **separately verified** — this register reads as fact, so an unverified one is worse than ordinary copy. |

> ## ⭐ THE TEST BEFORE YOU WRITE ANY LINE
> **Read it aloud. Does it sound like a person talking, or like an ad?**
> If it sounds like an ad — if it has a rhythm, a pun, a rhyme, a "journey," a "moment," a "celebrate the ones who..." — **rewrite it flatter.**
>
> **⛔ Banned outright:** puns · wordplay · double meanings · rhyme · alliteration for its own sake · "celebrate/honour/cherish the ones who…" · any line a greeting card could carry.

# HOW TO USE THIS FILE

1. **The IDEA comes from the Recognition Brief** (role 15) — a real, relatable, evidenced situation. **Never from these images.**
2. **Check TABLE 0 FIRST** — who is in frame, what they are doing to whom, and who comes off badly. **A frame with nobody in it is a declared exception, not a default.**
3. **Then decide HOW to build it:** pick one **V** (Table 1), one **C** (Table 2), one **T** (Table 3), one **light** (Table 4), one **copy shape** (Table 5).
3. **Open the named reference images and look at them.** Proportion, weight and timing do not survive into prose — *(the nib underline is short and thick; I specced it long and thin from memory and the render failed).*
4. **Declare all five in the prompt.** A prompt that cannot name its V, C, T, light and copy shape is incomplete.

> **⛔ The division of labour, in one line:** **the Recognition Brief decides WHAT the ad is about. This file decides HOW it is built. Neither does the other's job.**
