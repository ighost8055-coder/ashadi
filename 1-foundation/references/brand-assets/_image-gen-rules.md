# §A0. ⭐⭐ THE LIVING FRAME TEST — run this BEFORE the Redundancy Test *(human rule, 2026-10-03)*

> **Human feedback that produced this section:** *"Something is missing — it's concept that's missing. The concept isn't very relatable. It looks very fake, like AI generated, no human involvement kinda thing. A human will have better ideas for execution."*
>
> **The diagnosis, found by going back through all 38 references.** Five batches of Teachers' Day frames were rule-compliant, well-composed and lifeless. The cause was mechanical and it was in this rulebook:
>
> **Every realism rule here was written as a BAN** — *no posed smile · no performed expression · no camera-facing grin · no product that isn't hero-lit.* **A ban is perfectly satisfied by putting NOBODY in the frame.** So the system drifted, batch by batch, to empty rooms and still lifes, because an object can never break a rule about faces. **Three of five frames in one batch were `Product: none` with no human in them at all.**
>
> **⛔ The proof is inside the reference set itself.** The one image in it that IS AI-generated — the Superads ad, which carries an *"AI-GENERATED PERSON"* disclosure down its edge — fails in **exactly the way the human described**: one composed person, no second person, no event, nobody at risk of looking foolish. **The two weakest other references (a phone floating on orange; a disembodied hand and a laptop) fail the same way.** That is what "looks AI-generated" actually means, and it is a **concept** failure, not a render failure.

## ⛔ THE FOUR REQUIREMENTS — every frame, unless a stated exception is declared

**1 · A PERSON IS IN FRAME, WITH A VISIBLE FACE.**
⛔ `Product: none` is still allowed (Core Rule 17) — **but "no people" is NOT.** A still life is now a **declared exception** that must be argued in writing, not a default. *(Across the 38, the still lifes are the weakest images in the set; every memorable one has a human in it.)*

**2 · SOMETHING IS HAPPENING, AND IT IS HAPPENING NOW.**
The frame is **mid-event, never aftermath.** Coca-Cola: two people collide and the drink is **airborne**. Monopoly: a girl **mid-scream**. KFC: a man **mid-lick**. Target: a child **lunging**. Spotify: a girl **leaning in for a kiss he isn't ready for**.
⛔ **The test:** *could this photograph have been taken one second earlier or later and been the same picture?* **If yes, nothing is happening.** Dust, empty rooms, objects left behind and "the morning after" are all **aftermath** — they are the single most common form this failure takes.

**3 · TWO PEOPLE, AND ONE IS DOING SOMETHING TO THE OTHER.**
Relationship, contact, consequence. A tailor **feeding** a man mid-fitting. A man **dragging** furniture while a baby wakes. An old couple **kissing badly** in a doorway. An arm entering frame **from off-camera**.
*A single-person frame is permitted when that person is **absurdly out of place*** (talabat's racing driver in an armchair; the beekeeper eating a burger on a roof) — **but then the absurdity must carry the whole frame.**

**4 · SOMEONE COMES OFF SLIGHTLY BADLY.**
⛔ **Reverence is the enemy of the laugh, and it is the single biggest failure in this project's output.** Not one person in the 38 is dignified: a man in denim hot pants shot from behind · a man licking his fingers over **a competitor's pizza** · a sunburnt man who looks furious on holiday · a dad meditating while his child destroys the attic · a child screaming at losing a board game.
**⭐ In the strongest references the brand's own customer is the butt of the joke.** VW mocks the man. KFC mocks the eater. Monopoly mocks its player. **Treating the subject as sacred is why a frame has no joke in it.**

> ### ⛔ AND THE RULE THIS OVERTURNS: THE PRODUCT MAY BE A MESS
> This rulebook said the hero must be the brightest, cleanest, most composed thing in frame. **Coca-Cola's headline is *"Worth every drop"* over a bottle whose contents are spilling through the air.** The product is mid-disaster and the ad is better for it.
> **A product that is spilling, half-eaten, dropped, overflowing or being fought over reads as REAL. A product sitting immaculate on a surface reads as a catalogue.** The figure/ground rule (§0a Q4) still binds — the hero must still be findable — **but "findable" is not "pristine."**

## ⛔ THE DECLARED EXCEPTION

A frame with **no person** is still legitimate — for a **declared functional/offer piece**, a pure product hero, or a graphic style-board. **It must be declared in writing as `Living frame: exception — [reason]`, and a batch may not be mostly exceptions.** **⛔ At least half of any batch carries a human doing something.**

## ⭐ WHAT TO WRITE IN THE PROMPT

Every prompt names, in one line each:

```
Who:        the person, as a specific human — not a casting note
            ⛔ "a teacher in her fifties, face partly turned away" is a casting note.
               "a woman who has taught Grade 5 for 22 years and is being made to
               stand on a chair by her own students" is a person.
Doing:      the physical action, mid-event
To whom:    the second person and what they are doing back
Who looks bad: who is slightly ridiculous, and how
```

**⛔ A prompt that cannot fill all four lines has not found its concept yet — it has found a composition.**

# §A0b. ⛔⭐ THE TWO-PASS RULE — the type is COMPOSITED, never rendered *(human rule, 2026-10-05)*

> **Human rule:** *"Make sure the human-edited feel is there — especially the text. Make sure the image gives a layer-by-layer edit feel, not all-at-once like AI. Because customers now can tell what's human and what's AI."*

## THE TELL, AND IT IS MEASURABLE

**A human-made ad is two separate jobs done on two different days by two different people.** A photographer shot a photograph. **Later, a designer opened it in another application and put type on top.** The type never visited the place the photo was taken.

**An AI image is one job.** The model renders type and photograph **in the same pass, into the same tonal world** — so the letters pick up the scene's grain, haze, falloff and colour cast. **They look like they were present at the shoot.** That is the thing customers now read as synthetic, and it is **not the face, it is the INTEGRATION.**

**The proof is inside the reference set:**
- **12 VW** — the headline is **pin-sharp, perfectly flat white, with zero grain**, sitting over a photograph that has visible grain and soft falloff. **The type does not participate in the photograph at all.**
- **29 Uber** — a plain line sits over **heavy rain**, and **the letters are not wet.** Nothing in the weather touches them.
- **9 Superads** — the one reference carrying an **"AI-GENERATED PERSON"** label. Its type sits in the **same tonal world as the photo**, slightly soft, lit as though it belongs in the room. **Baked in. One pass.**

> ## ⛔ THE RULE
> **THE TYPE LAYER AND THE PHOTO LAYER MUST LOOK LIKE THEY WERE MADE SEPARATELY.** State it in every prompt, in these words or equivalent:
>
> > *"The headline is a flat vector type layer composited on top of the finished photograph in a separate pass — like a designer opened the photo in another application and set type over it. **The type carries NO film grain, NO noise, NO atmospheric haze, NO depth-of-field blur, NO lens distortion, NO colour cast and NO light from the scene.** Its edges are perfectly crisp and mathematically clean at 100%, with pure flat fill and no texture. **The photograph underneath keeps all of its grain, falloff and atmosphere. The two layers must look like they were made on different days by different people.** The type is never lit by the scene, never shaded by it, never wet, never dusty, never warmed by it."*

## ⛔ THE SIX THINGS THAT BREAK IT — all are instant rejects

1. **Grain or noise in the letterforms.** The photo has grain; **the type has none.** If the type is grainy, it was rendered, not composited.
2. **The type picking up the scene's colour cast.** A warm golden-hour photo must not produce warm-tinted white type. **White stays `#FFFFFF`; cream stays `#F5F4EB`.** Flat, unmixed, uncontaminated.
3. **Soft, blurred or anti-aliased-into-the-photo edges.** Letter edges are **mathematically crisp.** No glow, no bloom, no softness, no feather, no depth-of-field falloff across a word.
4. **Type that is lit by the scene.** No highlight on a letter's top edge, no shadow under it from the scene's key light, **no shading that suggests the type is a physical object in the room.** *(⚠ The ONE exception is a declared `T3`/`L9` type-in-depth frame, where occlusion is the device — and even there the type stays flat and ungrained; only the OCCLUSION is real.)*
5. **Weather or atmosphere touching the type.** Not wet in rain. Not dusty in dust. Not hazed by distance. **Nothing in the air reaches the type layer.**
6. **Perspective or warping on the type** to match a surface. The type is **parallel to the picture plane, always** — it lives on the poster, not in the room.

## ⭐ WHAT TO ADD TO PUSH IT FURTHER TOWARD HUMAN

- **Optical, not mathematical, placement.** A designer's margins are **felt, not computed** — state the type is aligned by eye to the subject, not centred by formula. **A hair off-centre reads human; dead-centre reads generated.**
- **One deliberate asymmetry.** Flush-left type with a ragged right edge, ending where the sentence ends — **never justified, never auto-balanced.**
- **The photograph keeps its flaws.** Grain, a blown highlight, a slightly soft corner, a crooked horizon. **A perfect photograph under perfect type is the most synthetic combination there is.**
- **The logo is composited the same way** — flat, crisp, unlit by the scene, **never shaded to sit in the room.**

> **⛔ This rule binds in EVERY treatment, V1 through V5, and on every text treatment T0 through T7.** A flat-field graphic frame is already two layers; a street photograph must be **made** into two. **The separation is the point.**

# §A. THE CORE IDEA OF THE NEW SYSTEM

> **One sentence:** **the picture and the line are two halves of ONE sentence, and neither works alone.**

In every one of the strong references, the headline and the image are **mutually incomplete.** Neither repeats the other.

| Reference | The line alone | The picture alone | Together |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FedEx** | *"Now you know which one came first."* — meaningless | a chicken leaving a box — just odd | **we deliver faster than nature** |
| **Aircel** | *"Tigers are irreplaceable."* — a platitude | a goat in tiger stripes — a stunt | **this is what a replacement looks like** |
| **Nike** | *"This is trash."* — an insult | a beautiful sneaker — a product shot | **the trash IS the shoe; that's the point** |
| **VW** | *"They're jeans. Technically."* — confusing | denim shorts that don't fit | **non-genuine parts work. Technically.** |

> ### ⛔ THE REDUNDANCY TEST — run on every prompt, before anything else
> Write the headline in one sentence. Describe the image in one sentence.
> **If either one still makes complete sense on its own, the ad is redundant and not at this bar.**
>
> **The failure it catches is this project's most common one by far: a correct headline sitting above a picture that illustrates it.** Illustration is not advertising. The picture must make the line *necessary*; the line must make the picture *make sense*.
>
> **The one legitimate exception:** a **declared functional/offer frame** (a price, a delivery fact, a category push). Target's *"Same Day Delivery. Easy as a PB&J."* is exactly this — warm, clean, and its picture does illustrate its line. That is a **good ad**, and it is **not the bar.** Declare it as `Register: functional` and move on.

# §B. THE SIXTEEN MECHANISMS — how great frames are actually built

**A frame needs ONE of these working hard, not sixteen working weakly.** Every prompt declares `Mechanism:`. "None" is valid only on a declared functional frame.

### M1 · THE HINGE WORD
The strongest lines are **short, flat and conversational**, and turn on **one word the picture reframes**.

| Line | Hinge | What the picture does to it |
|---|---|---|
| *"They're jeans. **Technically**."* | technically | turns a true statement into a devastating admission |
| *"This is **trash**."* | trash | insult → material specification |
| *"Tigers are **irreplaceable**."* | irreplaceable | a dead word made literal by a goat |
| *"Now you know which one came **first**."* | first | resolves an ancient joke as a delivery claim |

**⭐ Compatible with Core Rule 15 (no wordplay):** a hinge word has **one** meaning, is not a pun, and is not "wrong on purpose." It is the one honest word whose meaning the *picture* changes.
**Method:** write the flattest literal version of the claim, then ask *which single word could the picture make land harder?* Change nothing else.

### M2 · ONE IDEA, ONE OBJECT, EMPTY EVERYWHERE ELSE
Glovo is **~70% empty yellow**. Aircel is ~60% empty white. B&Q is flat orange and five words. **None fills the frame; all are unmissable.**
> **⛔ RULE: one hero object, one headline, everything else empty.** If you can name three competing focal objects, cut two. **This is not restraint applied when a frame gets busy — it is the primary construction method of award work.**

### M3 · THE HYBRID — and the shared-geometry test
The most-used device in the set: goat+tiger · rider+cheetah · straightener+pasta · turntable+running track · flight paths+arches.

> **⭐ THE TEST THAT SEPARATES THIS FROM AI SLOP — name the shared geometry first.** *"These two things are both ___-shaped."* Record grooves are concentric; so are running lanes. Flight arcs curve; so do the arches. Pasta falls in strands; so does hair. **The join is FOUND, not forced.** **If you cannot name the shared geometry in one short phrase, do not write the prompt** — it will render as a Photoshop job, not an idea. This is the single most common way a hybrid fails.

### M4 · TYPE AS A PHYSICAL OBJECT IN THE SCENE
The *"Ah!"* piece sets a colossal white word **behind the subject and in front of the background**, so his head and raised glass **occlude the letterform**. Superads passes *"win"* behind a head. KFC sets its logo **inline, mid-sentence**, as a word.
**Why:** type merely *on* a photo always reads as an overlay added later; type **interleaved in depth** reads as part of the picture — and it solves legibility **without a scrim**.
→ Registered as **`L9 · TYPE IN DEPTH`** (§F).

### M5 · THE BRAND BUILT OUT OF THE WORLD, NOT STAMPED ON IT
McDonald's arches are **drawn by flight paths**. Spotify's mark is the **grass infield** of the track. talabat's brand shapes **become the kitchen**.
**The insight:** ordinary work *applies* the logo at the end; great work makes the brand **the material the picture is made of.**
> **⛔ HARD LIMIT (unchanged):** **exactly one Kapruka logo, composited from the attached asset, never redrawn or reconstructed (§H).** So: build a **brand-shaped composition** (a route, an arc, a colour field, a shape made of real products) and place the real logo once. **Building the mark itself out of flowers is banned.**

### M6 · PUT IT IN THE WRONG WORLD, KEEP THE RULES OF BOTH
Pasta in a hair-styling world. A sneaker in a waste world. A chicken in a logistics world. A tiger in a wedding-portrait world, with correct zari embroidery and real fabric drape.
> **⛔ THE RULE THAT PROTECTS THE PREMIUM BAR: the funnier the idea, the straighter the craft.** The absurdity lives in the **concept**; the execution is **dead serious** — same material floor, same lighting discipline, same grade. The moment the rendering gets jokey too, it collapses into a meme.

### M7 · LET THE BRAND BE THE BUTT OF THE JOKE
B&Q's tool **physically breaks the apostrophe out of "can't"**, converting the line to *"You can do it."* Nike calls its own shoe trash. KFC admits you're eating a competitor's pizza.
**Mechanism:** saying something mildly against your own interest earns credibility, because advertising is assumed to flatter. **Self-deprecation is a trust device.**
> **⚠️ HIGHEST-RISK MECHANISM — scope for Kapruka.** Core Rule 9 means the self-criticism must be **literally true**; Core Rule 14 means the frame must land on the **warm, happy beat**.
> **✅ Safe form: joke about the CUSTOMER'S situation** (forgot the date, panic-bought, the cousin who always sends better) — universally recognised.
> **⛔ Never joke about Kapruka's service.** *"Our delivery is slow"* is not a joke, it is a false claim, and it spends the exact asset the business runs on.
> **Default to the B&Q typographic variant, not the self-criticism variant.**

### M8 · THE FOUR TYPE PATTERNS *(the full text system is §E)*
Across 21 references the type does only four things. **8a** flat-corner stack · **8b** type as the entire image · **8c** one huge graphic word in depth · **8d** the sequential word across panels.
> **⛔ WHAT NONE OF THEM DO: not one reference uses a scrim, gradient wash or dark box behind type. Legibility is ALWAYS solved by composition.** 21 out of 21.

### M9 · THE FLAT SATURATED FIELD AS THE ENTIRE BACKGROUND
Glovo yellow · B&Q orange · talabat orange/blue · Target red · Spotify green · the tiger piece's green gradient. The brand colour is **not an accent — it is the whole background**, edge to edge, subject cut out on top.
**Why it stops a thumb:** feeds are full of photographs and almost empty of **pure colour**. It also makes figure/ground automatic.
> **⭐ Directly applicable and currently unused by Kapruka:** Retail Purple `#340A5C` edge-to-edge with one cut-out product. **Note the tiger reference's vertical gradient** — a light-to-dark gradient in one hue reads *premium* where a flat field reads *graphic*. Both available; **declare which**.

### M10 · THE CAMPAIGN LINE — one sentence stem, infinite frames
*Spotify's “Because …” · Monopoly's “FOR LEARNING TO LET GO” · Midea's “WINDOW ___ / ___ ON” · talabat's “When/Whatever/Whenever you want — just talabat it.”*

**The strongest references are not single ads — they are a STEM plus a variable.** *“Because music doesn't judge.” · “Because mixtapes still work.”* Same four-word opening, infinite endings. Midea runs *“WINDOW UP / MATCH ON”* and *“WINDOW DOWN / MUSIC ON”* — identical grammatical skeleton, opposite scenario.

> **⭐ Why this matters more to Kapruka than any other mechanism here.** We generate **batches**, and a batch of unrelated frames reads as noise however good each one is. A stem makes eight frames **one campaign**: the viewer learns the pattern on frame one and gets the pleasure of recognition on every frame after. **It also solves our range problem for free** — the stem stays fixed while the department changes, so breadth is argued by repetition instead of by cramming eight products into one grid.
> **How to use it:** declare a `Stem:` for the batch, then one variable per frame. **⛔ The stem must be literal and direct** (Core Rule 15) — *“Because …”* works; a punning stem does not. **⛔ The variable must change the PICTURE, not just the words** — if only the headline changes, that is a one-word headline change, which Core Rule 5 already calls not-a-real-iteration.

### M11 · THE PRODUCT IS ABSENT; THE EFFECT IS THE WHOLE FRAME
*Spotify's goosebumps · Road Lodge's one star · the Singapore Kindness split.*

The Spotify goosebump ad is **a macro photograph of skin**, filling the entire frame, with one green line: *“When the music hit the right spot.”* **No product, no phone, no headphones, no person's face.** Just the physical evidence that the product worked.

> **⭐ This is the purest form of our existing Core Rule 17 (“a product is not mandatory”), and we have never once executed it.** Rule 17 currently reads as permission to omit a product; this reference shows it as **a device**: shoot the *consequence*, not the thing. For Kapruka the consequences are strong and completely unused — **the face of the person receiving**, the emptied box, the table with one more plate on it, the phone screen at 6am with a photo of what arrived.
> **⛔ Honesty guard:** the effect shown must be one the product genuinely produces (Core Rule 9). **⛔ Craft guard:** at macro scale the Material Realism Floor becomes the entire ad — skin at full-frame must have real pores, real fine hair, real subsurface warmth, or the frame is worthless.

### M12 · THE LINE IS LIVE DATA, NOT A SLOGAN
*Uber: “St Helens, your nearest Uber driver is 6 mins away.”*

The headline is **a UI string**, set small, with the location named and a real number in it — placed over a photograph of four people sheltering from rain. It does not sound like advertising at all, which is exactly why it lands.

> **⭐ Directly applicable to Kapruka's moat, and the sharpest unused idea in either reference set.** Our entire business is **a place, a date and a distance**. *“Melbourne to Nugegoda. Delivered Thursday.”* — that is a Three-Yes line (visualizable, falsifiable, ownable) that reads as information rather than persuasion.
> **⛔ The hard guard:** a data-shaped line makes a **specific, checkable promise**. Only name a time, a date or a route **that is separately confirmed** — and because a UI-styled string reads as a system fact rather than a claim, an unverified one is worse here than in ordinary copy. **⛔ It must not become e-commerce chrome** (§K): a typographic line, never a widget, button or notification card.

### M13 · THE BRAND SHAPE AS THE CROP ITSELF
*talabat's swoosh · the Burger King check mark · Glovo's corner curve.*

talabat does not place its logo on a photo — **the brand's curve cuts the photograph**, so the orange field and the image meet along a brand-shaped edge. The Burger King piece makes **the check mark the only graphic element**, oversized, behind the type.

> **⭐ This is M5 (brand as material) made cheap and repeatable** — no conceptual stunt required, just a shaped crop. **Kapruka has no proprietary swoosh**, so the honest version is: **a full-bleed brand colour field meeting the photograph along one confident curve or diagonal**, with the real logo placed once. **⛔ Never construct a Kapruka-mark-shaped crop** — that is reconstructing the logo, banned under Invariant 4.

### M14 · ⭐ THE GAP — the picture completes the sentence
*nib “__ happens” ×2 · Marathon Sports “YOU CAN'T ___ IF YOU DON'T RUN” · B&Q's apostrophe.*

**The single strongest device in all 42 references, and the most directly usable.** The headline has **a literal blank in it**, drawn as an underline, and **the picture sits in the blank.**

- **nib:** *“* ___ *happens”* — quote marks, a green underline, and **a man falling through a ceiling occupying the gap.** The reader supplies the word; the brand never prints it.
- **Marathon Sports:** *“YOU CAN'T ___ IF YOU DON'T RUN.”* — and a tennis player **mid-serve** fills the blank. The missing verb is the thing she is doing.

> **⭐ Why this is the best fit for Kapruka in the whole set.** It is the §A redundancy test made **structural and unfakeable**: the line **physically cannot** be understood without the picture, because a word is literally missing from it. There is no way to write a redundant ad in this format.
> **⛔ And it clears Core Rule 15 cleanly.** The reader completes **one literal word** — that is **not a pun, not a double meaning, not a wrong-on-purpose verb.** Comprehension is instant because the gap tells the eye exactly where to look. *(Compare the IndiGo line we rejected: that asked the reader to hold two meanings at once. This asks them to read one picture.)*
> **Construction:** a short all-caps line with a **drawn underline** marking the blank; the subject sits **in** the gap, cleanly separated from the ground; the completing word is **never printed anywhere.** **⛔ The gap word must be unmissable from the picture alone** — if two different words would fit, the device has failed.

### M15 · THE ANTI-BOAST — claim less than you have earned
*VW: “We didn't invent anything.” over a crowd at a stadium, under a 360° camera product name.*

The headline **denies the brand any credit at all**, over a warm photograph of a father with a child on his shoulders seeing over the crowd. The product name sits plainly beside it. **The understatement is the entire tone.**

> **⭐ This is M7 (self-deprecation) made safe**, and it resolves the risk I flagged there. M7's danger was joking about the service; **the anti-boast instead under-claims a true thing** — *we didn't invent the shoulder-ride; we just gave you the view.* It is humble, warm, **and fully honest (Core Rule 9)**.
> **⭐ Kapruka's version writes itself and is unused:** *“We didn't invent the family.”* · *“We don't make the moment. We just make sure you're in it.”* — under-claiming while the moat does the work.
> **⛔ Guard:** under-claim, never **mis**-claim. The sentence must stay literally true, and the frame must still land on the **warm, happy beat** (Core Rule 14).

### M16 · THE CONTROLLED A/B — same subject, one variable, side by side
*Fabrikant “Uncomfortable / Comfortable” ×2 · Singapore Kindness “Your thoughtlessness / Their sleeplessness.”*

Two near-identical halves on one flat field. **Same man, same pose, same lighting, same everything — and one half has the furniture.** The label under each is a single plain word.

> **⭐ Why it beats an ordinary before/after:** nothing is being *transformed*, so there is **no implied claim to over-promise** — it is a controlled comparison, which is honest by construction. The Kindness execution shows the premium version: **one continuous room split down the middle**, two consequences of one act, with the two labels meeting at the seam.
> **⛔ The craft is the whole thing (and it is exactly our L8 grid lesson):** the two halves must be **identical in scale, angle, light and pose.** Any drift and the comparison stops being a comparison. **⛔ Honesty guard:** the difference shown must be caused **only** by the variable named.

# §C. THE NEW VISUAL TREATMENT SYSTEM *(replaces the old A/B1/B2/B3/C axes)*

> **Why the old system was retired.** It was organised around **how the image was made** (flat graphic vs Canon vs iPhone vs documentary). That is a production question, not a creative one, and it produced a house style — *soft side light, candid in-between moment, muted palette* — that appears in **none** of the 21 references. The new axes are organised around **what the frame is doing to the viewer.**

**Every prompt declares one `V` (what the picture is) × one `T` (what the type does).**

### V1 · FIELD — subject cut out on a flat or gradient colour field
*Glovo · B&Q · Spotify · talabat · the tiger piece.* The ground is **one brand colour, edge to edge**, flat or a single-hue vertical gradient. The subject is a clean cut-out with **real contact shadow**. **60–75% of the frame is empty colour.**
- **Camera:** none named — this is not a photograph of a place. **The SUBJECT is still photographic** and carries its own lighting description (see §D).
- **Use for:** the hybrid (M3), the flat field (M9), range/graphic work, anything that must survive a thumbnail.
- **⛔ Watch:** the cut-out must have **a real contact shadow or a cast shadow on the field.** A floating subject with no shadow is the clearest composite failure.

### V2 · OBJECT — one object, studio-lit, on a plain ground
*Nike · Ghee · FedEx.* A single hero object or small assembly, photographed as a **serious product shot**, on a plain neutral ground (not a brand colour field). The idea lives in **what the object is** or **what is being done to it**.
- **Camera:** named, with a **hard directional key** (see §D). This is the one treatment where studio light is correct.
- **Use for:** the wrong-world (M6), substitution, material-as-message, product heroes.

### V3 · STREET — a real moment, real place, real people
*Coca-Cola · VW · KFC · Domino's · talabat's kitchen.* A photograph of something happening in a real environment.
- **Camera:** named, **and the new default is NOT soft side light** — see §D.
- **Use for:** emotional work, the moment, Sri Lankan authenticity, delivery/handoff scenes.

### V4 · CONSTRUCT — an openly built image that never pretends to be a photograph
*McDonald's flight-path arches · Spotify's turntable.* Diagram, map, exploded view, impossible assembly. **Declared as constructed** — its power is that it is obviously designed.
- **Camera:** none. **⛔ No `8K`** — a diagram is not a photograph.
- **Use for:** brand-as-material (M5), data/geography, the moat (international delivery is a *map* idea and we have never once drawn it).

### V5 · STAGE — a real subject in a deliberately unreal space
*The tiger in the sherwani · Glovo's rider on a cheetah · talabat's helmeted rider in an armchair.* Photoreal subject, impossible situation, **no attempt to hide the construction.**
- **Camera:** named for the subject.
- **Use for:** the hybrid (M3), comedy (M6), surreal concepts.
- **⛔ This is where the "funnier idea, straighter craft" rule (M6) bites hardest.**

> **⛔ THE MATERIAL REALISM FLOOR — binds in ALL FIVE treatments, no exceptions.**
> **A scene may be impossible; a surface may never be fake.**
> - **Real material behaviour.** Fur as fur (strands, direction, sheen breaking over form), fabric as fabric (weave, drape, real wrinkles), skin as skin (pores, fine lines, subsurface warmth), metal/glass/ceramic each behaving correctly. **No plastic, waxy, over-smoothed or CG-putty surfaces** — the fastest tell there is.
> - **One coherent light logic.** Every element lit from the same direction, shadows matching in softness, correct light wrap, **real contact shadows wherever anything touches anything.**
> - **Correct anatomy and construction.** Right number of limbs and fingers; joints bending the way they bend; **no melted, warped, fused or duplicated detail.**
> - **Real edges.** No cut-out halo, no eroded fringe, no visible masking line.
> - **Type rendered, never faked.** Correct spelling, real letterforms, no garbled or invented sub-text.
> - **The test:** *the idea may be impossible; nothing may look cheaply made.* A bad composite is **broken, not stylised** — regenerate it.

# §D. THE NEW CAMERA SYSTEM *(fully replaces the old camera rules)*

> ## ⛔ WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY — read this, it is the biggest single change in the rebuild
>
> The old rulebook mandated, on **every** photographic prompt: *one large soft directional side light, low contrast, gentle falloff, eye-level, shallow depth of field, candid in-between moment, subject not posing and not looking at the lens, muted cohesive palette.*
>
> **Every one of those clauses is now a CHOICE, not a default.** They were written to fix a real problem (glossy fake-looking ad renders) and they fixed it — but they also hard-coded **one single look** into every frame the system produced. **Not one of the 21 references uses that look.** Look at what they actually do:
> - **Nike / Ghee / FedEx** — **hard directional studio light** with crisp shadows. The opposite of soft and low-contrast.
> - **Domino's** — **brutal direct sun**, blown highlights, a sunburnt face shot head-on at close range.
> - **Coca-Cola** — subject in motion, **shot from behind**, faces barely visible.
> - **VW** — **flat daylight**, shot from directly behind at hip height.
> - **KFC** — long-lens compression, **motion blur across the foreground**, subject looking straight down the barrel.
> - **The tiger / Glovo / talabat** — **cut-outs on colour**, lit to be cut out.
>
> **The honest summary: the old camera rules were a cure that became a cage.** What stays is the *realism*; what goes is the *single look*.

### D1 · THE REALISM FLOOR *(kept — this is what the old rules got right)*
Every photographic element, in every treatment, states:
- **Visible texture and imperfection** — skin pores and fine lines, real fabric wrinkles, handled/worn surfaces, small marks. **No airbrushing, no skin smoothing.** Perfect = fake.
- **Fine natural film grain + real lens character** — mild vignetting, gentle highlight roll-off. Sterile digital-perfect rendering is an AI tell.
- **Genuine optical depth of field** from a named aperture — not software-faked bokeh with cut-out edges.
- **The explicit anti-ad line:** *"looks like a real photograph, not a rendered or AI image."*
- **⛔ Banned always:** waxy/plastic skin · airbrushing · melted or warped detail · HDR glow · CG/3D-render look.

### D2 · THE LIGHT IS NOW A DECLARED CHOICE — pick ONE, name it
| Light | What it looks like | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **HARD KEY** *(new default for V2 OBJECT)* | one hard directional source, **crisp-edged shadows**, high contrast, strong falloff | product heroes, material-as-message — *Nike, Ghee, FedEx* |
| **DIRECT SUN** *(new)* | overhead or raking tropical sun, **hard shadows, blown highlights**, real glare | Sri Lankan exteriors, heat, energy — *Domino's* |
| **FLAT DAYLIGHT** *(new)* | open shade or overcast, even, **low drama, documentary plainness** | street candour where the *idea* carries it — *VW* |
| **SOFT WINDOW** *(the old default, now one option of five)* | one large soft side source, low contrast, gentle falloff | intimate interiors, warmth, the gift being opened |
| **FIELD LIGHT** *(for V1 cut-outs)* | lighting that lets the subject cut cleanly off a colour field, with a real contact/cast shadow on the field | *Glovo, talabat, the tiger piece* |

> **⛔ The one surviving ban:** **flat frontal three-point ad lighting with a rim-light kicker.** That is still the fastest "this is an ad" tell. Hard light is not the same thing as flat light — **hard light has direction and makes shadows; flat ad light kills them.**

### D3 · CAMERA HEIGHT AND ANGLE ARE NOW A CHOICE
The old rule was *eye-level, always, stated explicitly*. **Retired as a mandate, kept as an option.** The references use: **behind the subject at hip height** (VW) · **low and close, looking up** (Domino's) · **straight overhead** (Spotify) · **profile, dead side-on** (Aircel) · **eye-level** (Coca-Cola, KFC).
**Declare the height and angle in every photographic prompt. Eye-level is now a decision you make, not a default you inherit.**

### D4 · LENS — pick for the job, state it
- **24–35mm** — environment matters, slight exaggeration of near/far, energy
- **50mm** — natural human perspective, the honest middle
- **85–135mm** — **compression**, background collapses into a wall of context *(KFC uses this and we have never once used it)*
- **Macro** — material as the subject *(Nike's trash texture)*

### D5 · THE MOMENT IS A CHOICE
Old rule: *candid in-between moment, subject absorbed, NOT posing, NOT looking at the lens.* **Now one of four:**
**Candid** (absorbed, mid-action) · **Direct address** (looking straight down the lens — *KFC, Domino's*) · **Action** (mid-movement, real motion blur — *Coca-Cola, Glovo*) · **Still life** (no person at all).
> **⛔ The one surviving ban: the held, presented, camera-facing *advertising* smile.** Direct address is now allowed; **performing happiness at the lens is not.**
>
> **⚠ READ THIS BAN CORRECTLY — a loose reading of it wrecked five batches.** It bans the **frozen catalogue grin** — **not emotion, not expression, and not performance.** A girl **mid-scream** at a board game, a man **mid-lick**, a couple **kissing badly** are all enormous performed expressions, and all are correct. **⛔ The two most alive images in the reference set would FAIL a literal reading of this ban.** What is banned is a face **holding a pose for the camera**; what is required is a face **in the middle of doing something** — **and it is never satisfied by removing the person (§A0).**

### D6 · WHAT A PHOTOGRAPHIC PROMPT MUST STILL NAME
Non-negotiable, in the Format line: **the device/camera · the lens · the aperture · the light choice from D2 · the camera height and angle · the moment type from D5 · `SUBJECT SCALE` (D7) · `8K` · `1080×1350 (4:5)`.**
**⛔ `8K` and the camera line do NOT appear on V4 CONSTRUCT, and do not apply to the flat field itself in V1** — only to the photographic subject sitting on it.

### D7 · SUBJECT SCALE IS A DECLARED CHOICE — default slightly zoomed out *(human rule, 2026-10-12)*
> *"Slightly zooming out the subject will bring in more premium. So mention the scale of the subject."*

A subject that fills the frame reads as a catalogue crop or a sticker; **a subject given air reads as expensive.** Surrounding space is what signals premium.
- **Every photographic and cut-out prompt declares `Subject scale:`** — the subject's share of frame in plain numbers (e.g. *"hero occupies ~40% of frame height, centred-low, ~30% clear space above"*) and its margin to the frame edges.
- **Default: slightly pulled back** — hero roughly **35–55% of frame height**, with breathing room on every side, never touching or cropping at an edge unless the crop is the declared device. Tight/full-bleed is the exception and must be declared as such with its reason.
- **Zoom out by distance/framing, not by shrinking into clutter** — the extra space stays calm and uncluttered (Rule 18: fewer, bigger, calmer still binds). Type sits in the cleared space (Two-Pass Rule intact).
- **Limits:** the subject must still survive the ~250px thumbnail test (§0a) — if zooming out makes the hero illegible at feed size, step back in. Zoom-out never licenses adding props to fill the space.
- **Does not apply** to declared macro/material-as-subject frames (D4) where the close crop is the idea.

# §E. THE NEW TEXT SYSTEM *(replaces T0–T3)*

**Declare one `T` per prompt.**

### T1 · CORNER STACK *(the dominant pattern in the reference set)*
Heavy Inter, **2–4 very short stacked lines, flush-left**, parked in one corner with **large empty space around it**. Lines often **end in full stops**.
> **⭐ The tonal trick, worth stating: a full stop after a two-word line makes a fragment sound final.** *"This Is Trash."* · *"Same Denim. New Angles."*
- **Sub-variant `T1-P` — WITH PRICE CHIP:** add a light descriptor + a **real verified price** in `#340A5C` inside a solid `#FFC107` rounded rectangle. *(Formerly L7. Price is product/offer advertising — permitted here by the human's 2026-10-03 flag, and the price must be **real, current and verified** or the chip is dropped, not guessed.)*

### T2 · TYPE IS THE IMAGE
*B&Q, McDonald's.* The words **are** the composition; the object is small and **acts on them**.
> **⭐ This is where this project's old "twist" tension finally resolves.** In B&Q the twist is performed **by a physical object in the picture** (a saw cutting out the apostrophe), not by a clever word. **That is a text-hero idea Core Rule 15 fully permits** — the cleverness is in the *picture*, and the line stays literally direct.

### T3 · ONE HUGE WORD, SET IN DEPTH
*"Ah!", Superads.* A single short exclamation or verb at **enormous scale**, interleaved with the subject. → device `L9`.

### T4 · SEQUENTIAL WORD
*"Make It Local."* **One word per panel** across a 3-panel vertical stack or carousel; the sentence completes only as the eye travels. → device `L10`.

### T5 · INLINE BRAND
*KFC.* The logo sits **mid-sentence as a word**: *"Everything reminds you of [KFC]"*.
> **⛔ Kapruka constraint:** this uses the **attached logo asset, composited inline, at text scale** — it is still **the frame's one logo**, and it is **never redrawn or retyped** as text (§H).

### T0 · NO TEXT
Nothing in frame but the logo. **Mandatory consideration on every comedic frame** — a joke that needs a caption isn't landing yet.

> ## ⛔ THE ABSOLUTE TEXT RULES — all five, every frame
> 1. **NO SCRIM, EVER.** No gradient wash, no dark box, no blur panel, no glow, no drop shadow behind type. **Legibility comes from composition.** *(21/21 references.)*
> 2. **Inter only** — ExtraBold/Bold headlines, Medium/SemiBold support. No serif, script, slab, display, decorative or handwritten face **in any element.** Write it as **description + name**: *"Inter (a clean geometric grotesque sans-serif) — tall x-height, open apertures on c/e/a, straight-cut single-story 'a', near-square dots on i/j."* **A render that comes back as a generic system sans is a reject, not a close-enough pass.**
> 3. **One headline block + at most one support line.** *(T1-P's descriptor + price chip are a declared narrow exception — they are factual product identifiers, never extra selling lines. No support sentence may be added on top of a T1-P lockup.)*
> 4. **No em dash** in any on-image copy.
> 5. **Stated px sizes are a FLOOR, not a target.** Headline cap height 90–125px; support 32–46px. Smaller than its own prompt said = reject.

# §G. COLOUR — the one thing that did not change *(application rebuilt)*

**LOCKED, unchanged:**
- **Real attached assets keep their true colours:** Kapruka Purple `#4B2386`, Kapruka Yellow `#FFD21E` — **never recolour** the van, bag, flower box, cake box or packaging.
- **The brand hexes are fixed:** Retail Purple `#340A5C` · Kapruka Yellow `#FFC107` · Soft Cream `#F5F4EB` · Luxury Gold `#D9A441` · Deep Purple `#241044`. **Never invent a new brand colour or drift a hex.**

**REBUILT — how they're applied:**
- **⭐ The full-bleed field is now a first-class choice (M9).** Retail Purple edge to edge with one cut-out subject is **encouraged and under-used.** Flat field = graphic; single-hue vertical gradient = premium. **Declare which.**
- **The ground is a per-ad decision.** Purple is the anchor, not a requirement. Cream, editorial white, near-black, a real environment, a product-colour environment — all legitimate. **Judge the ratio across the body of work, never within one frame.** A batch where every ad is 70% purple is the failure, not the target.
- **⛔ GOLD ≠ YELLOW.** Every time gold appears, say it: *"Luxury Gold `#D9A441` — MUTED, DESATURATED, ANTIQUE gold, NOT bright yellow; do not let it collapse into `#FFC107`."*
- **⛔ Yellow is an accent, never a field** *(except as a declared M9 full-bleed ground)*. One or two words, or one chip. Keep it off support lines, rules, dots, icons and footers unless it is doing real work.
- **⛔ NO OUTLINE OR STROKE OF ANY COLOUR ON YELLOW TYPE, EVER.** Yellow type is solid `#FFC107` with no outline, or it is not used on that ground. **If the ground is too light to hold it: move the word to a darker patch, or drop the yellow and set the headline in `#340A5C`.** Never add a stroke — it reads as a cheap decal.
- **⭐ SCROLL-STOPPING CONTRAST — state the pairing explicitly in every prompt.** Name which element is darkest, which is brightest, and that the gap is large. **The hero must separate from its ground on at least TWO of: size, value, saturation, shape.** ⛔ **The product must never be the quietest element in its own ad.**

# §I. THE HONESTY, SAFETY AND AUTHENTICITY FLOOR *(unchanged — a new agency inherits this)*

- **Real products.** Pull from kapruka.com. **⛔ Never describe a product from its name — open the listing photo and look at it.** Verify in stock. One distinct product per prompt. **Attach the real photo.**
- **⛔ Honest claims only (Core Rule 9).** 23+ years (since 2002), island-wide delivery, millions delivered. Prices real and current. **A metaphor may not imply a capability Kapruka lacks.**
- **⛔ No real identifiable person** — living or dead, famous or private. Not a face, not a caricature, not a lookalike, not a prompt that names them. **Deceased and historical figures are not an exception.** Build archetypes **from props, not features**, and say the character **must not resemble any real or identifiable person.**
- **⛔ No invented sub-text on ANY object** — packaging, boxes, bags, labels, tags, signage, vehicle panels, aprons, cups. A surface carries text in exactly two cases: **it is the attached real asset**, or **the prompt names the exact string.** Otherwise **blank**. *(This is stricter than "no garbled text": garbled text is obviously broken and gets rejected, while cleanly-rendered invented brand copy **looks legitimate and ships** — a false claim in Kapruka's own voice.)*
- **⛔ No Kapruka branding on third-party products. No counterfeit brands.** A real listing keeps its own maker's branding — that is honest.
- **⛔ No alcohol.** Tea, coffee, king coconut or juice instead.
- **⛔ Sri Lankan authenticity** wherever a real setting appears — terrazzo/tiled floors, painted plaster, grille windows, tropical foliage, equatorial light, local dress. **And when a frame is comedic, the JOKE itself must be locally true**, not a generic joke wearing local scenery.
- **⛔ Gifts are handed over, never left on the ground. ⛔ A box opens toward the person, never angled at the camera. ⛔ The van is the only delivery vehicle — no motorbike, scooter or bicycle.**
- **⛔ Category breadth (Core Rule 12).** Any frame with more than one product spans **more than one department** — count departments, not objects. Cakes/flowers/chocolates are **one department wearing three hats.** The exception is a **declared category ad**, which then carries no breadth claim.
- **⛔ Gifting is happy and vibrant (Core Rule 14)** — but **vibrant is a mood, not a colour-grade licence.** Premium editorial colour; never candy-bright or app-onboarding gloss.
- **⛔ Copy is direct (Core Rule 15)** — no puns, no double meanings, no wordplay. **M1's hinge word is not an exception: it has one meaning.**
- **⛔ Basic English.** Plain everyday register, no US idioms, no fussy formal British.

# §L. THE SHIP CHECK — five questions, before any prompt is written

1. **Does the line still make sense without the picture?** → illustrating. Rebuild. *(Unless `Register: functional`.)*
2. **Which ONE mechanism (M1-M16) is this running on?** → **several weakly = none.**
3. **What is the hero, and is everything else empty?** → three competing focal objects means cut two.
4. **If I removed the logo, whose ad would a stranger say this is?** → if not "an online gift shop," the framing is fighting you. *(Certain compositions are owned by a category: a rear-seat car cabin is a car ad; a plated dish overhead on marble is a restaurant ad.)*
5. **Does it survive a ~250px thumbnail?** → no counting, no reading fine detail, no inference. **If the device's payload is small text, the device is broken.**
6. **⭐ THE STRANGER READ — does the poster say its message PLAINLY?** *(Human rule, 2026-10-12, caught live on the Boss's Day graphic batch: "it's not very clear".)* **⭐ THE FIVE-YEAR-OLD STANDARD (same day, human): "a 5 year old should understand, with a creative image."** Show the picture and read the headline to a five-year-old: they must be able to say what it is about (a boss being thanked, a day for the boss). **Simple everyday words, one idea, a picture of one obvious thing happening. Clear is the floor; creative is how the picture does it** (a bold, surprising use of a real object, scale or colour), never a riddle, never inference, never insider knowledge. Clarity is not allowed to cost the creative image, and a creative image is not allowed to cost clarity. A viewer who knows nothing of the brief sees the poster for one second and must be able to say, in everyday words, **what it wants them to DO or CELEBRATE** (e.g. "thank my boss on Boss's Day"). Write that sentence as `Stranger read:`. It fails if the viewer could say anything else: *a chore, a prank, a name-learning tip, a threat, "a quirky photo".*
   - **For occasion/brand promotions the headline carries a PLAIN ACTION toward the person (thank / wish / appreciate / remember) plus the day, in direct speech.** The picture makes that action specific, human and local. **A headline that is only a setup ("Find out her first name", "Hand him the knife") makes the viewer infer the purpose, and fails.** Direct copy (Core Rule 15) means *direct about the purpose*, not just free of puns.
   - **No insider-only reads as the whole message.** A local-culture detail (Sir/Madam, the first boss, the leave stamp) may flavour the frame but cannot be what makes it make sense. **Never rest an ad on an UNCONFIRMED ritual or custom** (flagged INFERRED/ASSUMED in the Recognition Brief) as the sole way to understand it.
   - **Tone: appreciation, never a chore, dare, prank or threat.** Light affection only.
   - **Direction and safety are stated twice and checked against the picture.** Any knife, blade or pointed object is held **handle toward the receiver**, said in both the scene and the negatives; every left/right or toward/away instruction is re-read after writing, because the image model reverses them (caught live: a knife came back blade-first at the receiver's palm, reading as a threat).

7. **⭐ THE "NORMAL" TEST — would this survive being one of five in a festive template pack?** *(Human feedback, 2026-11-03, on five rendered Diwali covers: "all looks very normal".)* Clear and rule-compliant is the floor, not the bar. **Fail if** any of these is true: the five frames share one layout recipe (same ground, same garland corners, same headline-top / people-bottom stack, same footer bar); the idea is a small gag in a pleasant scene with no single bold visual device; the people could be any brand's festive stock set; the reference's signature move (e.g. low-angle hero + gelled saturated light + type interacting with the subject) was dropped in the translation. **Pass requires:** (a) every frame in a batch has a **visibly different construction** (not just different people in the same stack); (b) each names ONE bold device taken from the reference or the mechanism list and executes it at full strength; (c) ornament is a **real photographed object with a job** (a lamp, a garland held, a doorway), never a decorative border or corner garlands; (d) it declares `Not-template:` — one sentence on what makes it unmistakably not a template. **(e) KAPRUKA'S ROLE IS READABLE IN ONE SECOND** *(human, 2026-11-03: "no gifting, no 'celebrate Diwali with Kapruka'")*: an occasion creative that only shows a happy festival scene is a greeting card for nobody. Every occasion ad shows a **real Kapruka gifting element doing a job** (bag, box, van, or real listed gifts handed over / opened / fought over) AND a headline or support line that names the idea plainly (e.g. "Celebrate Deepavali with Kapruka." / "Gift Deepavali."); declare `Gifting + Kapruka read:` (what a five-year-old would say). A name-the-person greeting alone does not satisfy this. **Render-side bans repeated in the prompt:** no tagline under the logo, no footer bar or date lockup beyond the one allowed support line, no glow or gradient on type, no baked-in ghost text unless declared and readable.
