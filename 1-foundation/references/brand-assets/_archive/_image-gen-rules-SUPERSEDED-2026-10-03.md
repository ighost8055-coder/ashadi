# Image-Generation Rulebook — PROJECT LEVEL (applies to every image prompt, every batch)

> **Status: source of truth (human-set, 2026-07-18).** Any prompt that produces a Kapruka image — the Reference Recreator track, or any role that ever writes an image-gen prompt — MUST follow every rule here. This consolidates rules scattered across the role files + brand guidelines into one place. If a batch's STANDING SPEC and this file ever disagree, **this file wins** and the batch is fixed.

---

## 0. THE CREATIVE BAR — Cannes-grade or it doesn't ship (the overarching standard)
Every concept is judged against the **Cannes Lions** benchmark — the global standard for creative excellence (Grand Prix / Gold / Silver / Bronze). Non-negotiables:
- **One-second comprehension.** A viewer must grasp what the ad is about in **one second** — the idea reads instantly, before any reading.
- **Metaphorical / conceptual, not literal.** Build on a **visual metaphor or conceptual idea**, not a product description. The idea lives in a real object, a seam, a landmark, a twist — never a decoration.
- **Ogilvy headline discipline.** Copy is **thought-through and minimal** — a headline that earns its place (Ogilvy: the headline is 80% of the ad), plus at most a short support line. **Not too much copy.** Copy only when the image needs it; if the visual carries it, let it.
- **⛔ MAXIMUM TWO COPY BLOCKS PER FRAME** *(human rule, added 2026-08-16 — caught live on a sympathy-flower prompt that stacked a headline, a footer proof line, a footer CTA-adjacent line and the tagline as four separate messages).* Every frame gets **one headline block and, at most, one support/proof line** — never more. If the concept wants a proof stat AND a delivery claim AND a CTA, **fold them into that single support line** (e.g. *"Because you can't be here. Same-day flower delivery in Sri Lanka."* is one support line, not two) — don't invent a second footer block to hold the overflow. The logo lockup and its tagline are brand attribution, not a third copy block, and don't count against the cap — but don't let them carry a second sentence of messaging either. **If a prompt has more to say than one headline + one support line can hold, the idea is overloaded — cut the message, don't add a block.**
- **⭐ THE TWIST — when the TYPE IS THE HERO** *(human rule, 2026-07-25)*. In any **text-hero** creative (text-led poster, broad-brand line, style-board where the headline carries the ad), a plain claim isn't enough — the line needs a twist. Reference: *"No one grows ketchup like Heinz"* — you don't *grow* ketchup, and that "wrong" verb collapses the whole chain so Heinz claims all of it. **Reads wrong in one second, right on the second read.** Mechanics: **impossible verb** (*"No one delivers Sri Lanka like Kapruka"*) · **owned inversion** (*"You'll miss the party. Your gift won't."*) · **specificity as the twist** · **literalised idiom**. Guardrails: **twist the words, never the facts** (the claim underneath stays literally true — Rule 9); **a pun is not a twist** and is exactly what the anti-slop gate kills; it must be **ownable** (swap in a competitor's name — if the line still works, kill it); and **functional / occasion-direct / offer lines stay plainly direct** — don't force it. **⛔ NEVER stack a twist headline on top of an already-conceptual device (added 2026-08-21, caught live on "The ribbon doesn't stop at the bow" — a ribbon/route fusion paired with an abstract twist headline, which forced a two-step decode: work out the visual metaphor, THEN work out the wordplay, and neither explained the other. Failed the One-Second Test's third question outright.)** When the picture is already carrying a device — seam/join, substitution, absence, scale distortion, impossible object, transformation, before/after, remix/fusion (§0a) — **the headline pairs with it PLAINLY LITERAL, describing what the image shows, not adding a second layer of cleverness on top of it.** One of the two carries the cleverness; the other confirms it in plain words. Only pair a twist headline with a plain/product-hero visual (`Visual idea: none`) or a visual whose device is simple enough to read instantly on its own (e.g. absence/negative space) — never with a device that itself requires a beat to parse. Full method: `2-roles/04-copywriter.md` § THE TWIST.
- **⭐ VISUAL COMEDY — LESS TEXT, NOT MORE** *(added 2026-08-21, human rule)*. When the concept is a comedic/sight-gag idea (a physical joke, an absurd juxtaposition, an exaggerated moment — anything from the Comedian Angle Generator or any other humor-led angle), **the joke in the frame carries the laugh, not a headline stacked on top of it.** A funny image with a headline AND a support line explaining it reads as an ad narrating its own joke, and comedy that has to be explained isn't landing. **Default to T0 (no in-frame text at all) or a single short caption-length line** — never a full headline + support pair on a comedic visual. **Test:** if covering all the text still gets a laugh, the text was decoration and should be cut or shrunk to a bare caption; if the text is doing the comedic work, the *visual* isn't funny yet — fix the image, don't prop it up with more copy. **Honesty (Core Rule 9) still applies** — if the piece makes a claim, state it in as few words as the joke can absorb (T0's claim restriction is unchanged); don't use "it's a joke" as an excuse to skip an honest claim that's actually being made. **⛔ Every prompt declares `Comedy: yes` or `Comedy: no` as the very first line, before "Visual idea:"** *(added 2026-08-22)* — this makes it instantly traceable whether this rule was checked against a given prompt, rather than left implicit. **When `Comedy: yes`, the joke must be grounded in something specifically, recognizably Sri Lankan** *(added 2026-08-22)* — a real local social dynamic, family/neighbourhood pattern, or everyday Sri Lankan moment (per the examples already in this project: the tuk-tuk/rider dynamic, the grandmother out-ordering her grandkids, the office pantry snack-grab) — not a generic, could-be-anywhere joke translated into a Sri Lankan setting after the fact. This ties directly to §1a (Sri Lankan Authenticity) — the *comedy itself*, not just the visual backdrop, has to be locally true. **Test:** if the same joke would work exactly as-is set in any other country with the location details swapped, it isn't Sri Lankan-relatable yet — it's a generic joke wearing local scenery.
- **⭐ CREATIVITY IS REMIX** *(added 2026-08-20, human rule)*. The strongest concepts rarely invent from nothing — they **connect two unrelated things**, or take something completely ordinary and ask *how would a genuinely creative person redesign this?* Before finalizing any concept, run this check: is this idea actually a remix/connection/redesign of something, or is it just the expected version of a known ad format wearing a new coat of paint? A concept that only rearranges the furniture of a cliché (same layout, same device, different product) hasn't cleared this bar. This bar applies to every treatment and every role (Ideator, Strategist, Creative Director, Kelly, the Reference Recreator alike) — its visual-execution form is the **REMIX/FUSION device**, added to the device menu below.
- **Breakthrough Advertising thinking.** Enter the conversation in the reader's head; sharpen the one desire/tension the concept owns.
- **Highly detailed, specific prompts.** Every prompt is exhaustively specified (composition, light, materials, exact on-image text, palette, negatives) — never vague.
- **⛔ The anti-slop reject gate (do this BEFORE finalizing each concept).** Ask: *is this AI slop? Is this the usual AI design / the usual AI prompt / the first idea every ordinary art director would reach for?* If yes, **reject it and go again.** Each document lists the specific clichés it threw out (globe-with-bow, boxes-from-sky, overflowing cart, etc.) as proof the gate ran. A concept ships only after it survives this gate.

This bar is **project-level** and sits above every rule below.

## 0a. THE VISUAL IDEA — the PICTURE must carry one *(added 2026-07-25, human rule)*

> **The failure this exists to stop:** a batch of "graphic" ads that are all the same picture — **product cut-out, centred on flat purple, headline above it.** That is a *layout*, not a *concept*. It happened because Treatment A was defined by its rendering style ("product as a clean cut-out"), the prompt checklist had no slot for an idea, and the **twist rule let the image off the hook** — once the headline carries the concept, the picture only has to show what's for sale. *(Caught live 2026-07-25 after five graphic ads came back with no visual idea in any of them.)*

### ⭐ THE BLANK HEADLINE TEST — run it before writing the prompt
**Cover the headline. Does the image still say something?**
- If what's left is *"here is the product, on a colour"* → it is a **product poster**, not a concept. The copy is doing 100% of the thinking.
- If what's left still carries a tension, a claim or a joke → you have a visual idea.

A product poster is a **legitimate choice** for functional/offer/occasion-direct work (Creative Standard: those may be plainly direct). It is **not** acceptable as the unexamined default for brand and emotional work — which is most of what this system makes.

### Every prompt DECLARES its visual idea (see the new item 0 in Rule 5c)
Before "Background," each prompt states one of:
- **`Visual idea:` [the device, in one sentence]** — what the picture is *doing* beyond displaying the product, and
- **`Blank-headline read:` [what a viewer takes from the image alone]**

…or, deliberately and in writing: **`Visual idea: none — product hero (functional/offer piece).`** The point is that a product poster becomes a **choice you defend**, never a default you slid into.

### ⭐ THE ONE-SECOND TEST — the counterweight, run it right after the blank-headline test *(added 2026-07-26)*

> **Why this exists.** The blank-headline test only pushes one way. It catches *"the image says nothing"* and has **no opinion on "the image says too much."* On 2026-07-25 that produced the opposite failure: devices too demanding to read — an empty-place-setting ad that required **counting six settings** to find the idea, and a range ad with **40+ items in small type** that read as grey noise at feed size. Both had a genuine visual idea. Both failed anyway. **A test that only rewards cleverness manufactures unreadable cleverness.**

Three questions. **Any "no" means rebuild the device, not the render.**

1. **Does it need counting, reading or inference?** If understanding the idea requires **counting objects, comparing quantities, reading a list, or working out what's missing by elimination**, it is not a one-second image. Reach instead for a convention the viewer already holds — *the empty chair at a full table* is read instantly; *five plates and a box* has to be worked out.
2. **Does every meaning-carrying element survive the thumbnail?** Picture it **~250px wide in feed**. Any element the idea *depends on* — list items, labels, small props, fine detail — must still be legible there. **If the device's payload is small text, the device is broken.** Imply scale by letting the object **exit the frame**; never by enumerating it.
3. **Does the headline explain the picture, or does the picture have to be finished first?** If the line only lands **after** the viewer has decoded the image, that's a **three-step read** (see it → decode it → get the joke) and most of the feed stops at step one. The line and the image should land **together**.
4. **⭐ Can the eye SEPARATE the hero from its background?** *(Added 2026-09-29 — caught live on the peacock render.)* Figure/ground is the thing the eye does first, before it recognises anything. It fails when **the hero and the background share the same visual language** — same size, same contrast, same saturation, same shape vocabulary. The peacock ad put small bright circular products inside a tail already covered in small bright circular eye-spots: every element individually passed the thumbnail test, yet the frame read as **one busy texture** because nothing told the eye which shapes were the point. **The rule: the hero must differ from its surroundings on at least two of — size, value (light/dark), saturation, and shape.** Differing on one is not enough. If the background is the most saturated, most detailed or most contrasty thing in frame, **the background has become the subject** — calm it down (darken, desaturate, blur, simplify) so the hero can win. ⛔ **The products you are selling must never be the quietest elements in their own ad.**
5. **⭐ Does the eye know where to go FIRST, and in what order?** *(Added 2026-09-29, same cause.)* A frame with several equal elements gives the eye **no entry point**, so it ping-pongs instead of travelling — which reads as *busy*, the thing that makes an image tiring rather than instant. **Every frame needs one clear first stop, then a path.** Build the hierarchy deliberately: **one element is biggest / brightest / most central**, the rest are demonstrably secondary. **⛔ Never place a set of items at equal size with equal emphasis in a scattered arc** — if several products must appear, either give one clear primacy, or place them on a structure the eye already knows how to read (a row, a clean grid, a single arc with even spacing). **A good check: describe, in one sentence, the path you intend the eye to take. If you can't, the frame has no hierarchy and needs rebuilding — not re-rendering.**

> **⛔ FEWER, BIGGER, CALMER — the standing fix.** When a frame fails Q4 or Q5, the answer is almost always the same three moves: **fewer elements**, each one **bigger**, on a **calmer ground.** Six small products fighting a detailed background is weaker than three large ones on a quiet one — and it argues *range* just as well, because breadth is read from the **variety** of what's shown, not the **count**. Cutting items is not weakening the idea; it is what makes the idea legible.

**And check the object means the right thing.** A device fails quietly when the object's everyday meaning fights the idea — a **receipt** says *"what I spent,"* so it argues cost when the brief was *range*. Pick the object whose ordinary meaning already points where the idea is going.

**And check the COMPOSITION isn't borrowed from another category's advertising.** *(Added 2026-07-26.)* Certain framings are so owned by one industry that a viewer reads the industry before they read the ad: **a car cabin shot from the rear seat is a car-manufacturer frame** · a plated dish overhead on marble is a restaurant frame · a model walking toward camera on a plain seamless is a fashion frame · a phone held in a hand at 45° is a telco frame. Put anything inside that composition and it is read as *that* category's ad first. **Ask: if I removed the logo, whose ad would a stranger say this is?** If the answer isn't "an online shop," the frame is fighting you — change the framing, not the products. *(Caught live 2026-07-26: a Kapruka car-accessories ad in the rear-seat cabin frame read straight through as an automotive ad.)*

### The device menu — mechanics for the picture *(visual metaphor devices — separate from copy, which stays plainly direct per §1g, no wordplay/twist)*

| Device | The move | Kapruka example |
|---|---|---|
| **The seam / join** | One frame contains two places or times, meeting at a visible edge — the join *is* the idea. | One dining table, split down the middle: Colombo side and Melbourne side, same meal, same gift. *(This mechanic produced a previous human-picked winner — "a table away.")* |
| **Substitution** | An expected object is replaced by an unexpected one that means the same thing. | A parcel's shipping label is a family photograph. A doormat woven as a boarding pass. |
| **Absence / negative space** | The subject is the thing that *isn't* there. | A laid dinner table with one empty chair — and the gift sitting on it. Presence by proxy. |
| **Scale distortion** | Something is deliberately the wrong size to make the point. | A wall calendar the size of a door, one date circled; the gift is human-scale beside it. |
| **The impossible object** | A physically impossible thing whose logic resolves on the second look. | A gift box casting a **person-shaped shadow** — you're there, even though you aren't. |
| **Transformation** | The product becomes, or grows into, something else. | Bouquet stems becoming flight paths; foil chocolates growing as fruit on a tree. |
| **Before / after in one frame** | The change is shown, not described. | Left half: a phone screen showing her. Right half: the same table, the gift physically on it. |
| **Remix / fusion** *(added 2026-08-20)* | Two unrelated real things are physically fused into ONE continuous object — not standing side by side (that's before/after), not one meaning the other (that's substitution) — genuinely both at once. | A delivery van's headlight beam IS a sunrise, one continuous light source. A courier's motion-blur trail turning into flower petals mid-air. |

**Guardrails:** the device must live in a **real object, seam or landmark** — never a floating symbol or a decorative flourish (§0's anti-slop gate). It must still read in **one second**. And it must survive the same honesty test as copy — a metaphor may not imply a capability Kapruka doesn't have.

## 1. SUPER-REAL — no "AI-generated" look, ever
Every product/scene must read as **real professional photography, indistinguishable from a genuine campaign shot.** Instruct explicitly for: genuine textures, true material imperfections, natural light falloff, real shallow depth of field. **Ban the AI tells outright** in every negative list: no waxy/plastic surfaces, no over-smoothed or over-symmetrical texture, no uncanny-valley faces or hands, no melted/warped/artifacted detail, no airbrushed sheen, no CG/3D-render look. **A generated product that looks generated is a reject** — regenerate it.

> **The rule is anti-AI-look, NOT pro-polish — two declared exceptions:**
> - **Treatment A (GRAPHIC / STYLE-BOARD)** is *deliberately* flat — a chosen graphic look, not an AI-photo failure.
> - **Treatment B2 (iPHONE / CANDID)** is *deliberately* not a campaign shot — handheld imperfection, phone-lens distortion and no cinematic grading are the **specified** look, not sloppiness. B2 still obeys the real target of this rule: **no AI tells** (no waxy/plastic surfaces, no melted detail, no uncanny hands/faces, no CG render). *Read "indistinguishable from a genuine campaign shot" as "indistinguishable from a genuine photograph" — for B2 the genuine photograph is a phone snap.*

## 1a. ⛔ SRI LANKAN AUTHENTICITY — the setting must read as Sri Lanka, not generic/Western stock *(added 2026-08-22, caught live on a "doorstep delivery" prompt that rendered a North American-style suburban house — grey vinyl siding, an interior wood staircase, a Western porch light fixture — nothing in the frame read as Sri Lanka)*
Every prompt with a real-world environment (a home, a street, an office, a doorstep) must **explicitly describe Sri Lankan architectural and environmental detail** — don't let the model default to its most common training-data association (which skews North American suburban). State this in the prompt itself, don't assume it:
- **Architecture:** Sri Lankan homes commonly show tiled or terrazzo floors (not carpet), painted plaster/masonry walls (not vinyl/wood siding), grille/security-barred windows and doors, a covered *"pola"*-style front step or small verandah, gates and compound walls around houses rather than open North American-style front yards. Name these explicitly rather than leaving the architecture unspecified.
- **Vegetation & climate:** tropical foliage (coconut palms, areca palms, hibiscus, croton, banana plants), bright equatorial daylight (not the cool/overcast light common in temperate-climate stock photography), visible heat/humidity cues (nothing bundled in heavy layers/coats).
- **Street-level detail, where relevant:** tuk-tuks (three-wheelers), local vehicle plates, Sri Lankan road/utility-pole styling, local signage conventions — see the batch's own examples for how this reads when done well (e.g. the tuk-tuk/delivery-rider street scene).
- **People:** Sri Lankan/South Asian skin tones, features and dress appropriate to the scene (casual home clothing, not Western-coded wardrobe defaults) unless the concept is explicitly an overseas-diaspora scene (Gifting International), where the *sending* side may authentically be set abroad — but the **receiving** side in Sri Lanka must still read as Sri Lanka.
- **The test:** before finalizing, ask "if the logo were removed, would this read as a random Western stock-photo house, or unmistakably Sri Lanka?" If the former, the environment description is too generic and must be rewritten with the specifics above.

## 1b. ⛔ GIFTS ARE HANDED OVER, NEVER LEFT ON THE GROUND *(added 2026-08-22, caught live on the same "doorstep delivery" prompt — the gift box was rendered sitting on the ground/doorstep rather than being handed to the recipient)*
**A cake, bouquet, hamper or any gift must never be shown resting on the floor, ground, doorstep or pavement.** This is both a real-world courtesy failure (a cake or flowers set down on a dirty step reads as careless, not premium) and a brand-quality failure (Kapruka's whole positioning is white-glove, dated, careful delivery — a gift on the ground contradicts that in one glance). **Every delivery/handoff scene must show the gift physically being handed to, held by, or already in the hands of the recipient** — mid-handoff (rider extending the box toward an outstretched hand), just received (recipient holding it, arms wrapped around it), or set on a proper surface (a table, a counter) if a hands-free moment is needed — **never on the ground.** State this explicitly in the prompt whenever a delivery/handoff moment is depicted: *"the [gift] is held in [person]'s hands / mid-handoff between the rider's hand and the recipient's — never set down on the floor or step."* Add **"gift resting on the ground/floor/doorstep"** to the negative list of every prompt depicting a delivery or handoff moment.

## 1c. ⛔ NATURAL ORIENTATION — a box opens toward the person, not toward the camera *(added 2026-08-22, caught live on an office gift-reveal prompt where the recipient tilted the open box outward to face the lens like a product-shot reveal, instead of opening it naturally toward herself)*
**When someone opens a gift/box in a candid or lifestyle shot, the box must face and tilt toward THE PERSON opening it — the way anyone actually opens something to see what's inside — never angled out toward the camera like a staged product reveal.** A box tilted toward the lens is the single fastest tell that a "candid" moment was actually staged for the shot, and it breaks B1/B2/B3's whole premise of a real, unposed moment. **State the natural orientation explicitly in the prompt:** the recipient looks down/into the box from their own natural angle, their body reads as genuinely curious about the contents rather than performing the reveal for an audience; the camera captures this natural angle **from the side or a three-quarter angle**, not from a position that requires the subject to twist the box toward the lens. This applies to any opening/unboxing moment — a gift box, a hamper lid, a jewellery box, a delivery package. Add **"box/gift tilted or angled toward the camera for a staged reveal"** to the negative list of every prompt depicting an opening moment.

## 1d. ⭐ COPY REGISTER — BASIC ENGLISH, TALKING TO DESI FAMILIES *(human rule, 2026-08-27)*

The setting rule above (1a) makes the **picture** read as Sri Lanka. This makes the **words** do the same. Every line of in-frame copy is written for **Sri Lankan families at home and in the diaspora (AU · USA · CA · UAE)** and the wider desi audience — **never for an American consumer.**

- **Basic, everyday English.** Short words a non-native speaker reads at a glance — no dictionary, no re-read. If a simpler word exists, it wins. Simple is respect for the reader, not laziness.
- **No US idioms or American cultural furniture** — “holiday season,” “Mom,” “y’all,” “treat yourself,” “date night,” “back-to-school,” sports metaphors. **And no fussy formal British register** — “delightful,” “indulgence,” “kindly note.”
- **Family-first address.** Speak the way a desi family speaks: *Amma, Thaththa, Akka, Malli, Aiya, Nangi, uncle, aunty, the whole house.* Family, distance, duty, showing up, feeding people — those are the emotional coordinates, not individual self-expression.
- **One light authentic detail, never a costume.** No heavy Singlish, no phonetic slang, no village-coded reference that only reads in one town — the diaspora reader must get it just as fast.
- **⚠️ Recreating a reference means borrowing its CONSTRUCTION, not its voice.** Most references are Western-made; their copy register is exactly the thing to throw away.

**Two tests, both must pass:** **(1) The aunty test** — would an aunty in Kandy *and* a cousin in Melbourne both get this line on the first read, with nobody explaining it? **(2) The origin test** — could an American brand have written this exact line about an American family? If yes, it isn’t ours; push it onto a Sri Lankan/desi truth (the distance, the family duty, the person waiting at home).

*Full rule: [`../../../2-roles/04-copywriter.md`](../../../2-roles/04-copywriter.md) § LANGUAGE REGISTER. Paste-ready form: the COPY REGISTER block in `_STANDING-SPEC.md`.*

## 1e. ⛔ NO ALCOHOL, EVER *(human rule, 2026-08-28)*

**No alcohol appears in any Kapruka creative, in any track.** No beer, wine, arrack, spirits or cocktails; no bottles, cans, wine glasses, beer glasses or tumblers of drink; no bar, pub, brewery or drinks-trolley setting; nobody drinking or toasting with alcohol.

- **If a reference image contains alcohol, swap it out and keep everything else** — the drink becomes **tea, coffee, king coconut, a fresh juice or a soft drink**, and the rest of the composition is unchanged. Alcohol is never a reason to abandon an otherwise good reference.
- **Put it in the negative list of every prompt** that shows a table, a celebration, a party, a dinner, a gift hamper or any social scene — those are where a model reaches for a bottle unprompted.
- **Why:** it does not fit the brand or the families being sold to, and alcohol imagery is restricted under Meta's ad policies — an ad carrying it risks rejection regardless of how good the creative is.

## 1f. ⛔ COUNT THE LIMBS, AND MAKE THE SENTENCE PARSE *(human rules, 2026-08-28)*

Two failures caught live on the same render, both of which read as "cheap AI" instantly:

**⛔ EXACTLY TWO ARMS, TWO HANDS.** *(Caught 2026-08-28: a cartoon character holding two bouquets AND touching her cheek — three hands, each one drawn perfectly.)* The usual failure is not a *malformed* hand, it is an **extra** one, added because the character is holding more objects than two hands allow. **State the count in the prompt, not just the quality:** *"exactly two arms, two hands, five fingers per hand; no third hand anywhere, including behind or beside an object being held."* If a character must hold more than two things, **share them between the two hands or drop one** — never let the model solve it by growing a limb.

**⛔ THE HEADLINE MUST BE A SENTENCE THAT PARSES.** *(Caught 2026-08-28: “how I look at my husband when the flowers come to me too” — every word English, the sentence meaningless.)* A meme caption is still a sentence. Read it aloud before it ships. **Test: could you text this line to a friend without them replying “what?”** If it needs a second read to work out what it even means, it is not clever — it is broken. Rewrite it. This sits underneath the [Three-Yes Test](../../../CLAUDE.md#the-three-yes-test-copy-standard--every-line): a line cannot be visualised, falsified or owned if it cannot first be *understood*.

## 1g. ⭐ VIBRANT & POSITIVE — gift-giving reads as fun, energetic, alive, never dull or sad *(human rule, 2026-09-17)*

**Gift-giving is a joyful act, not a duty — every frame must feel fun, vibrant and active, never dull, flat or sad.**

- **The mood, every time (unless declared otherwise):** bright, energetic, alive — genuine joy on faces, movement/energy in pose and light. A gift moment should look like something the recipient is *thrilled* about, not a solemn handover.
- **Ban as the unconsidered default:** downcast or melancholic expressions, static/lifeless poses, flat/muted "sad" lighting, funereal colour choices. None of these belong in ordinary gifting work.
- **The one legitimate exception: a declared quiet/sympathy piece.** A memorial, condolence or genuinely quiet-intimate concept may deliberately run a calmer, softer register — but it must be **stated explicitly in the prompt** as a considered choice (*"deliberately quiet/sympathy tone — not the default gifting mood"*), never the fallback a prompt drifts into by default.
- **Test:** if the mood description could just as easily sit under a funeral-home ad, it's the wrong default for a gifting frame — rewrite it toward energy and warmth.

**⛔ VIBRANT ≠ OVERSATURATED — PREMIUM GRADE STAYS THE FLOOR** *(added 2026-09-26, human rule — caught live: after the vibrant/happy rule above, renders came back punchy, glossy and stock-photo-bright, closer to an app-onboarding graphic than a campaign photograph; the human's reference for the correct look was the muted, film-graded florist-portrait and "You remembered late" renders from before this rule existed).* "Vibrant and happy" describes the **mood and expression**, never a licence to blow out saturation, contrast or exposure. Every photoreal frame (B1/B2/B3) still carries a **premium, editorial colour grade**: natural light falloff, true-to-life (not candy-bright) colour, real skin tones, restrained contrast, genuine film-like tonal range — the same grade this spec has always required under §1 SUPER-REAL. A joyful person under a *graded, believable* light is correct; a joyful person under flat, oversaturated, stock-photo lighting is a reject, same as a sad/dull one. **The test:** if the image looks like it could be a phone-app onboarding screen or a generic stock-photo library shot, the grade is wrong even if the mood is right — pull it back toward a real campaign photograph. Add "no oversaturated/candy-bright colour, no blown-out exposure, no glossy stock-photo lighting, no app-onboarding-graphic look" to that prompt's NEGATIVE line whenever a happy/vibrant mood is in play.

**⛔ ONE HEADLINE, AT MOST ONE SUPPORT LINE** *(sharpened 2026-09-26, human rule).* This hardens the existing minimal-copy discipline (§0 Ogilvy headline discipline, Rule 5c item 5): a headline (1-3 short lines) plus **at most one** short support line (one sentence, never a paragraph or several stacked clauses) — no third text block, no caption-length copy. If a subtext line runs long, **cut the line** — never shrink the type to force it to fit (that also breaks the font-size floor, §8). If the idea needs more text than that to land, rework the idea, not the type size.

**⛔ DIRECT COPY — NO WORDPLAY, NO TWIST** *(added 2026-09-26, human rule — "the copywriting should be very direct, no word play, clarity over clever").* Every headline/support line says the claim straight: no puns, no double meanings, no impossible/deliberately-"wrong" verb to decode. This retires the old text-hero "twist" standard project-wide — clarity beats clever, in every prompt, not just functional/offer work.

**⛔ No guilt-tripping copy — ever.** Headlines and support lines must never lean on guilt, obligation-shaming or fear of letting someone down to make their point — no *"she'll never forgive you if…,"* *"don't be the one who forgot,"* *"he'll notice if you didn't try."* The emotional pull is **love, care and excitement — never shame or anxiety.** Keep the register **fun, warm, happy, positive.** This sits on top of Core Rule 9 (honesty) and the [Three-Yes Test](../../../CLAUDE.md#the-three-yes-test-copy-standard--every-line): a line can be true and ownable and still fail this rule if the lever it pulls is guilt rather than joy.

## 2. Real products, pulled from www.kapruka.com
Where a prompt shows a **specific Kapruka product**, **search www.kapruka.com, pick a real currently-listed product, and pull its actual product image to ATTACH** to the generation tool (same attach-don't-redraw discipline as the brand assets). Describe *that* product (real name, look, packaging, contents). Every prompt carries a **`Product (kapruka.com):`** line — the exact listing to pull, or `none` for type/UI-only concepts. If the site is unreachable or nothing fits, **say so in the prompt** and fall back to a category-accurate description — never silently invent a fake product/name/price.
- **Boundary:** *Ecom "Global Shop"* concepts show **imported goods from the world's stores** (Amazon/eBay etc.) — these stay **generic and brand-free**, NOT pulled from kapruka.com, and never counterfeit a real retailer's logo/packaging.

### 2a. ATTACH vs DESCRIBE — two different disciplines *(clarified 2026-07-25)*
Operators frequently cannot attach a dozen files per batch, and a prompt that *requires* an attachment simply stalls. Split the rule by what's at stake:

| | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Kapruka brand assets** — logo, flower box, cake box, gift packaging, delivery van | **ATTACH — strongly preferred.** If genuinely unavailable, fall back to the exact written spec in the asset's `.md` file, and flag it. | Branding must stay pixel-consistent; a redrawn logo or smile is off-brand. |
| **Third-party retail products** — jewellery, cosmetics, fragrance, handbags, footwear, watches, appliances | **DESCRIBE — attachment optional.** Open the real listing, **look at the actual product photo**, and write a precise description into the prompt (form, colour, material, hardware, label, finish). | These carry no Kapruka branding, so a faithful description renders acceptably and keeps the prompt self-contained. |

**The non-negotiable part: the description must come from the real product photo, never from the product name.** Guessing what a listing looks like is how a prompt ends up describing a product that doesn't exist. *(Caught live 2026-07-25: a prompt described "a dome of roses rising from the Kapruka flower box" for a listing that is actually a flat **black** presentation box with a printed poem — the name alone had been trusted. Another described a "chocolate sponge bento" that is in reality a pink buttercream cake with butterflies and a crown.)* **Open the image. Look at it. Then write.**

### 2b. Verify the listing is IN STOCK, and vary the product across a batch *(added 2026-07-21)*
- **Check availability before naming a listing.** Kapruka pages stay up when an item is out of stock ("OUT OF STOCK / Remind me when in Stock"). A prompt that names an unavailable product sends the operator to a dead end. *(Caught live: Budget ELC Electric Kettle BT 6006 and Lipton Ceylonta 450g were both out of stock and had to be swapped.)*
- **One distinct product per prompt — no repeats across a batch.** Verifying one listing and then reusing it as the hero of most prompts is a research shortcut, not a creative choice, and it actively contradicts any set arguing **category breadth**. Each prompt names its own product from its own category; state the category in the batch's product table so the spread is visible at a glance. *(Caught live: a 5-prompt Ecom app set shipped with the same kettle in four of five.)*
- **Never silently substitute.** If nothing in the category is available, say so in the prompt and fall back to a category-accurate description — flagged, not hidden.

### 2c. ⛔ Never put Kapruka branding on a third-party product *(added 2026-07-21)*
Kapruka **resells** appliances, groceries, household goods and electronics — it does not manufacture them. The attached listing photo rightly carries its **own maker's** branding (Sanford, Innovex, Sunlight, Qualitea…), and that is honest. **Never print "Kapruka", the Kapruka logo, or any invented mark onto a kettle, rice cooker, detergent pack, tea carton or bottle** — it manufactures a false claim about who makes the product and fails Core Rule 9. Add it to the NEGATIVE list of every prompt carrying a retail product. *(Caught live: a render came back with "kapruka" printed on a Sanford kettle body — rejected.)* The exception is the genuine Kapruka-branded assets (cake box, gift packaging, delivery van, corporate branded merch), which are **attached**, never redrawn.

### 2d. ⛔ CATEGORY BREADTH — Kapruka is not a florist and not a bakery *(human rule, 2026-07-25)*
Cakes, flowers, chocolates, hampers and combos are the **reflex** categories — the first thing every prompt reaches for, and the reason a batch ends up looking like a florist's window. Kapruka's real catalogue is far wider, and **that range is the brand promise itself ("A world of choices")**. Verified live on kapruka.com:

| Group | Real categories |
|---|---|
| **Gifting classics** *(the reflex — use sparingly)* | cakes · flowers · chocolates · hampers · combo gift packs |
| **Beauty & personal care** | cosmetics · skincare · perfume & fragrance · spa/wellness sets · grooming |
| **Fashion** | handbags & travel bags *(300+ items)* · ladies/gents/kids shoes · slippers · eyewear · wallets · belts · umbrellas · fashion gift sets |
| **Jewellery & watches** | gold, gemstone & silver jewellery · crystal sets · gents/ladies/couple watches |
| **Electronics** | mobile phones · gaming *(230+)* · audio & home entertainment · wearables *(320+)* · computers & accessories · cameras |
| **Home & living** | kitchen appliances *(600+)* · home appliances · smart home |
| **Also** | clothing · grocery · toys · personalised/customised gifts · car accessories |

> **⭐ The principle, stated positively (added 2026-07-26 — read this before the numbered rules).** This rule is **not** "avoid cakes and flowers." It is **"the range is the product."** *"A world of choices"* is not decoration under the logo — it is the claim the brand is making, and **every multi-product frame either proves that claim or contradicts it.** Read the rule as a positive requirement to *span*, never as a negative list of things to dodge. Dodging the classics while landing somewhere equally narrow satisfies the letter and breaks the point.

**The rule:**
1. **Never let a batch collapse into cakes-and-flowers.** Across any multi-prompt set the classics may take **at most about half** the prompts; the rest come from the wider catalogue.
2. **⛔ ANY frame showing more than one product must span more than one department — not just grids.** *(Added 2026-07-26.)* A **PRODUCT GRID (`G`)** must obviously span categories: a grid of a bouquet, a cake, chocolates and macarons argues **nothing** about range — those are near-neighbours, and the grid format exists to *prove* breadth. **But the same requirement binds every multi-product frame**, grid or not: a lifestyle scene, a flat-lay, a still life, a haul, a tabletop. **A themed single-department set argues DEPTH IN ONE CATEGORY, which is the precise opposite of the brand promise** — and it reads to the viewer as a *specialist retailer of that category*, not as Kapruka.
   > **Caught live 2026-07-26:** a "fun gifting" ad placed four automobile accessories (helmet, headrest cushions, car charger, key cover) in a car interior. It broke no existing rule — no cakes, no flowers, four real products, all in stock — and came back reading unmistakably as **a car-accessories ad**, with *"A world of choices"* sitting directly beneath a frame that showed exactly one. The set had been optimised for *"not the classics"* rather than for *span*.
   >
   > **The one legitimate exception: a declared category ad.** A single-department frame is fine when the ad is *deliberately* a category push to a category-interested audience (car accessories to car people, gaming to gamers). **Declare it in the prompt header** — `Category ad: [department] — single-department by intent` — and accept that it is **not** a brand ad and must not carry a breadth claim in its copy. Undeclared, single-department is a failed brief.
3. **A multi-product set spans DEPARTMENTS, not sub-categories.** Four kinds of car accessory is one department. Four kinds of cake is one department. Bouquet + cake + chocolates + macarons is one department. **Count departments, not objects** — and name them in the batch's product table so the spread is visible at a glance and a collapse is impossible to miss.
4. **Match the category to the recipient, not to habit.** Gifting **to her** runs far past flowers — cosmetics, perfume, handbags, jewellery, footwear, spa sets. Gifting **to him** is served *badly* by the classics: electronics, phones, gaming, audio, watches, wallets, grooming, car accessories, toys/hobby gifts. **A "girlfriend → boyfriend" set built on cakes and flowers is a failed brief.**
5. **Browse before you write.** Open kapruka.com and look at what is actually listed for that recipient — the catalogue is consistently bigger than memory suggests. Rules 2 and 2b still apply: real listing, verified in stock, no repeats.

## 5. TREATMENT declared UPFRONT — TWO AXES on every prompt *(restructured 2026-07-25, human rule)*
Every prompt tags **both axes** before "The idea":
**`Treatment: [A | B1 | B2 | B3] + [T0 | T1 | T2 | T3]`** — the **visual treatment** (what the image *is*) **×** the **text treatment** (how the copy sits in the frame). **In-frame copy is not mandatory — `T0` means no headline at all** (see 5.2).

### 5.1 · VISUAL TREATMENT — what the image is

**A — GRAPHIC / STYLE-BOARD.** The **metaphorical / visually-exaggerated / conceptual** creative ad. A flat premium poster on a solid Kapruka-colour ground: bold flat shapes, flat gold shorthand, clean type, product as a clean cut-out, **no photographic environment.** This is where the Cannes-grade metaphor and the visual exaggeration live (type-led posters + UI-card concepts). **No camera line** — it isn't a photograph.
  - **Variant — FLAT BACKDROP + PHOTOREAL STAGING** (named 2026-07-18, from the "a table away" winner). A **flat/illustrated backdrop** (impossible geography, flat gold shorthand, flat skylines) combined with a **photoreal foreground** — the product *plus* real props and surface, with true texture, reflections and contact shadows. **Use when the idea needs the impossible but the emotion needs real texture.** Example: the Sri Lanka↔Dubai table — the flat backdrop carries geography no camera could shoot, while the real brass tumbler, banana leaf and woven mat carry cultural warmth a flat illustration never could. Pure flat would read cold; pure photoreal would read as a fake composite. **Still tagged A (GRAPHIC), not B.** Guardrail: the **backdrop must stay flat** — don't let the photoreal foreground creep outward into a full photographic scene, or it stops being this variant. *(The photoreal foreground does carry a camera/lens line — see 5A-i.)*

**B — PHOTOREALISTIC.** **The image itself sells the story** — no graphic scaffolding, no metaphor device; the moment carries it. Three sub-types, and they are genuinely different looks — pick deliberately.

> ### ⛔ 5.1-M · THE MATERIAL REALISM FLOOR — EVERY treatment, including A and C *(human rule, 2026-09-29: "I want realism in all treatments")*
> **The distinction that makes this work: a scene may be impossible, but a surface may never be fake.** Treatments A (flat graphic) and C (surreal composite) are *deliberately* not photographs — they are exempt from the **photographic** floor below (§5.1-R), and only from that. **Every rendered element in every treatment, A and C included, obeys this material floor:**
> - **Real material behaviour.** Fur reads as fur (individual strands, direction, sheen breaking over form), fabric as fabric (weave, drape, real wrinkles), skin as skin (pores, fine lines, subsurface warmth), metal/glass/ceramic each behaving correctly. **No plastic-looking, waxy, over-smoothed or CG-putty surfaces on anything** — the most common failure and the fastest tell.
> - **Light behaves consistently across the whole frame.** One coherent light logic: **every composited element lit from the same direction, with shadows falling the same way** and matching softness. Correct **light wrap** where a subject meets its ground, and **real contact shadows** wherever an object touches a surface — a floating object with no contact shadow is the single clearest composite failure.
> - **Correct anatomy and construction.** Right number of limbs, fingers, legs; joints that bend the way they actually bend; objects built the way they are really built. **No melted, warped, fused or duplicated detail anywhere.**
> - **Real edges.** No cut-out halo, no eroded fringe, no obvious masking line where an element was composited in.
> - **Type is rendered, never faked.** Correct spelling, real letterforms, no garbled or invented sub-text on labels and packaging.
> - **The test:** *the idea may be impossible; nothing in the frame may look cheaply made.* A badly-made composite is **broken, not stylised** — regenerate it. This is §1 SUPER-REAL applied to materials rather than to the scene, and it binds even where the scene itself is openly unreal.
>
> ### ⛔ 5.1-R · THE PHOTOGRAPHIC REALISM FLOOR — every B prompt, no exceptions *(human rule, 2026-09-27 — "increase the realism, right now it looks fake, it gives an ad feeling and I don't want that")*
> *(Scope: **B1/B2/B3 only.** A and C are exempt from this block — they are not pretending to be photographs — but both still obey the **material** floor above, which is not optional for anything.)*
> **The failure being fixed:** B1 and B2 were defined by a camera name and little else, so renders came back glossy, evenly lit, perfectly clean — recognisable as advertising in a fraction of a second. B3 already carried the full realism discipline (and produced the renders the human has approved); **that discipline is now the floor for B1 and B2 as well.** Every clause below is stated *in the prompt itself* — dropping one is a rewrite, not a style choice:
> - **Light: ONE large soft directional source, side-on** — a window or practical light wrapping the subject, low contrast, gentle falloff into shadow, warm-neutral never orange. **No flash, no studio rig, no flat frontal fill, no rim-light kicker.** Flat/frontal/three-point lighting is the single fastest "this is an ad" tell; soft side light reads as a memory. This is the biggest lever here.
> - **Skin, texture and imperfection, stated outright:** *"visible skin pores and fine lines, no airbrushing, no skin smoothing,"* real fabric wrinkles, worn/used surfaces, fingerprints and small marks where an object has actually been handled. Perfect = fake.
> - **Fine natural film grain + real lens character** — mild vignetting, gentle highlight roll-off, slight corner softness. Sterile digital-perfect rendering is an AI tell.
> - **Shallow, genuine depth of field from a named aperture** (f/1.8–f/2.8 typical), focal plane sharp on the eyes (or hands on a detail shot), everything else genuinely falling off. Not software-faked bokeh with cut-out edges.
> - **Camera height: eye-level**, stated explicitly — generators drift high by default.
> - **Candid, in-between moment.** Subject absorbed in something real, caught mid-action, natural micro-expression, **not posing, not looking at the lens, not smiling on cue.** A held, presented, camera-facing smile is the second-fastest ad tell.
> - **Muted, cohesive palette** — earthy slightly-desaturated naturals, one or two accents only. Ties directly to §1g's VIBRANT ≠ OVERSATURATED: joy lives in the face and the moment, never in the saturation slider.
> - **Environment: layered, lived-in, curated-not-cluttered** — authentic depth front and back. A spotless, propped, styled-for-camera set reads as a catalogue.
> - **State the anti-ad intent explicitly in the prompt:** *"looks like a real photograph someone took, not an advertisement, not a stock photo, not a rendered or AI image."*
> - **Negatives to carry on every B prompt:** no studio/flat frontal lighting · no glossy or waxy skin, no airbrushing or skin smoothing · no posed camera-facing smiles · no HDR glow or cinematic grading · no oversaturated or candy-bright colour · no perfectly clean/propped set · no stock-photo or advertising look · no CG/3D-render look.
>
> **What still separates the three:** B1 is *composed and intentional* (an art director chose the frame), B2 is *a phone snap* (handheld, phone-lens character), B3 is *documentary* (real camera, photojournalist's eye). They differ in **who is holding the camera and why** — never in how real the result looks. All three sit on this same floor.

  - **B1 — CANON / COMPOSED REALISM.** *(Renamed from "Commercial" 2026-09-27 — the word "commercial/campaign-grade" was actively inviting the glossy ad look.)* A deliberately composed, art-directed frame — but it must still read as **a real photograph of a real moment**, not a campaign shot. The art direction lives in the *choice of frame, light and moment*, not in polish. **Carries the full Realism Floor above.** Use the opening line, choosing the lens by what the frame needs:
    - **50mm — intimate / product-and-person, natural perspective:**
      > *"Photorealistic lifestyle photograph, **8K**, vertical 4:5 format (1080×1350). Shot on a professional camera (Canon 5D Mark IV, 50mm lens, f/2.0), one large soft directional window light from the side, low contrast with gentle falloff into shadow, eye-level camera height, shallow depth of field with the focal plane sharp on the eyes, fine natural film grain, mild vignetting, visible skin pores and real fabric texture, no airbrushing, candid in-between moment with the subject absorbed and not posing — looks like a real photograph someone took, not an advertisement, not a stock photo, not a rendered or AI image."*
    - **35mm f/2.8 — wider, more scene and context around the subject:**
      > *"Photorealistic lifestyle photograph, **8K**, vertical 4:5 format (1080×1350). Shot on a professional camera (Canon 5D Mark IV, 35mm lens, f/2.8), one large soft directional window light from the side, low contrast with gentle falloff into shadow, eye-level camera height, shallow depth of field, fine natural film grain, mild vignetting, visible skin pores and real fabric texture, no airbrushing, candid in-between moment with the subject absorbed and not posing — looks like a real photograph someone took, not an advertisement, not a stock photo, not a rendered or AI image."*

  - **B2 — iPHONE / CANDID.** Unposed, real, native-to-feed — reads as a photo someone actually took, not a campaign. Use when authenticity beats polish (UGC-feel, real-moment, "this happened" ideas). **Carries the full Realism Floor above**, with the phone's own character in place of the DSLR's. Full form:
    > *"Photorealistic candid photo, **8K**, vertical 4:5 format (1080×1350), shot on an iPhone 15 Pro rear camera, one soft directional natural window light from the side, slight handheld imperfection and a slightly off-level horizon, true-to-life colors, minor phone-lens distortion, natural sensor noise in the shadows, visible skin pores and real fabric texture, no airbrushing, no cinematic grading, no HDR glow, subject caught mid-action and not looking at the lens — looks like an unposed photo taken by a coworker, not an advertisement, not a stock photo, not a rendered or AI image."*

    Shorthand to append for the most casual register: *"Shot on an iPhone rear camera, casually, one-handed, without thinking about it."*

    ⚠️ **B2 does not mean sloppy.** Handheld imperfection and phone-lens distortion are *specified* imperfections — the composition, the product and the light are still deliberate. What's banned is the *look* of a campaign: no cinematic grading, no HDR glow, no studio rig, no perfect symmetry.

  - **B3 — EDITORIAL / DOCUMENTARY (the "Maker Portrait" method)** *(added 2026-08-16, derived from a Meta-for-Business ad reference set — see swipe file).* A **real camera, not a phone** — full-frame warmth and control — but shot **candid and unposed like photojournalism**, not art-directed like B1. Sits between B1 and B2: B1 is polished/campaign-grade, B2 is a deliberate phone snap; B3 is a *real camera held by someone documenting a genuine moment.* Use when the subject is a real person absorbed in real work (a florist arranging stems, a maker at the bench, a recipient lost in the moment with the gift) and the target feeling is **warmth and trust**, not premium gloss. Full opening line:
    > *"Photorealistic editorial documentary photograph, **8K**, vertical 4:5 format (1080×1350) for social media. Shot on a full-frame DSLR/mirrorless camera, 50mm lens (or 35mm when the environment needs to stay readable), aperture f/2.0–f/2.8, shallow depth of field, focal plane sharp on the subject's eyes falling into soft bokeh, eye-level camera height, one large soft directional natural window light from the side, low contrast, gentle falloff into shadow, no flash, no studio lighting, fine natural film grain, mild vignetting, gentle highlight roll-off, realistic lens character, candid in-between moment — the subject absorbed in their task, not posing, caught mid-action, natural micro-expression, hands in natural motion."*

    **The discipline behind each clause (don't drop any of these):**
    - **Lens: 50mm default.** Matches roughly what the human eye sees — no distortion, faces read natural, proportions read true. It sits in the honest, intimate middle: wider (24–35mm alone) stretches and reads as "phone selfie" or "real-estate photo"; longer (85mm+) compresses and reads as "formal studio portrait." **Use 35mm instead when the environment itself needs to stay legible** (a workshop floor, a shop interior) — it shows more of the space while staying natural.
    - **Aperture: f/1.8–f/2.8, wide open.** Creates the soft "melty" background separation that pulls the eye to the subject and signals *real camera*, not a phone faking bokeh with software. **f/2.8 is the sweet spot when the environment still needs to read** (a workroom, a floor) — go wider only when the environment can safely dissolve; f/1.2 turns a working scene to mush and kills the storytelling.
    - **Focal plane: sharp on the eyes (or hands, for a detail shot), everything else falls off.** This is the core "editorial" tell — it mimics how human attention actually works.
    - **Camera height: eye-level, always.** Sensor at the subject's own eye height, shot straight across — not looking down (small/vulnerable) and not up (imposing). **State this explicitly** — generators drift to a slightly-high angle by default.
    - **Full-frame sensor, named explicitly.** Nudges the model away from the deep-focus, everything-sharp "phone camera / GoPro" look and toward genuine shallow-DOF rendering.
    - **Grain and lens character.** A touch of fine natural film grain plus realistic lens rendering (mild vignetting, gentle highlight roll-off) breaks the sterile digital-perfect look — this is what reads as "shot by a real person," not generated.
    - **Motion, implied not specified.** Don't give an AI a shutter speed — get the effect through phrasing: *"hands in natural motion, caught mid-task."*
    - **Light: ONE large soft directional source, side-on.** A window, wrapping the subject, low contrast, gentle falloff into shadow — warm in temperature but neutral-creamy, never orange. **No flash, no studio light, no flat frontal light** — flat/frontal reads instantly as an ad; soft side light reads as a memory. This is the single biggest warmth lever in the whole method.
    - **The moment is candid, not posed.** The subject is absorbed in genuine work — eyes down, mid-action (arranging, smelling, glazing, adjusting) — never looking at or performing for the camera. Real vs. posed is detected in a fraction of a second and real wins on trust every time. This is the documentary "decisive moment" applied to advertising.
    - **The person is the hero; the product is context.** The maker or recipient is centre-frame, fully human; the goods are set dressing, not the subject. This sells a *feeling* (pride, quiet joy, the moment itself), never an object — which is what makes it feel aspirational rather than salesy.
    - **Environment: layered but tidy.** Authentic detail front-and-back creates real depth — but the mess is *curated*, not cluttered. Lived-in signals real craft; tidy keeps it premium.
    - **Palette: muted and cohesive.** Earthy, slightly desaturated naturals with **one or two accent colours only** (never more) — reads as editorial/lifestyle-magazine, never a sale flyer or a garish product shot.
    - **Texture and imperfection, stated explicitly.** Visible skin pores, real fabric wrinkles, worn surfaces — this is what the eye reads as authentic and therefore warm. **State "visible pores, no airbrushing" outright** — this is the exact opposite of the glossy, plastic stock-photo look and the single strongest lever against the AI-render tell (alongside Rule 1).
    - **Generous negative space + rule of thirds.** Subject off-centre or lower in the frame, with clean breathing room (usually the top) — both a calm, confident read and a clean zone left for the headline if the text treatment needs it.
    - **⚠️ Still carries the Four Output Invariants (Rule 5.1b)** — 4:5, 8K, Inter type, one logo — and the mandatory camera/lens spec (Rule 5.1a). B3 is a warmth register, not an exemption from the brand system.

#### 5.1a · ⛔ CAMERA / LENS / DEVICE SPEC IS MANDATORY *(human rule, 2026-07-25)*
**Every prompt with a photographic element names the device, the lens, and the shooting conditions — no exceptions.** That means all of **B1**, all of **B2**, all of **B3**, and the photoreal foreground of the **A-variant**. A photoreal prompt that opens with a vague *"a premium photograph of…"* and no camera/lens is **incomplete and must be rewritten** — the device spec is what pins the look and is the single strongest lever against the AI-render tell (Rule 1). Name it in the **Format line** (Rule 5c item 1), first thing, before anything else.

### 5.1b · ⛔ THE FOUR OUTPUT INVARIANTS *(human rules, 2026-07-26)*

Four things are **fixed in every prompt, every treatment, every batch.** They are not creative choices and never vary by concept. Check all four before shipping a prompt.

| # | Invariant | Where it goes in the prompt |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **4:5 — always.** Every image is **1080×1350 (4:5) vertical**. No square, no 1:1, no 9:16 story crop, no landscape. | The **Format line** (item 1), stated as `1080×1350 (4:5)`. |
| **2** | **8K — on everything photographic.** The word **`8K`** appears in the Format line of every **B1**, every **B2**, every **B3**, and the photoreal foreground of the **A-variant**. | Immediately after *"Photorealistic …photograph/photo,"* before the format and camera spec. |
| **3** | **Inter — the only typeface.** No serif, no script, no slab, no display or decorative face, no handwriting, in any element — headline, support, label, numeral or footer. | The **Headline** and **Support** items, and the **NEGATIVE** list. |
| **4** | **ONE logo per picture.** Exactly one composited Kapruka logo lockup in the frame. Never two, never a repeat in a corner "for balance," never a logo inside a grid cell as well as on the ground. | The **Logo** item (item 7) + the **NEGATIVE** list. |

**Notes that stop each one being misread:**

- **`8K` is a detail cue, not a file spec.** It pushes the model toward fine texture, micro-detail and sharpness; **the delivered image is still 1080×1350.** Don't ask for an 8K file, and don't drop the 1080×1350 because 8K is present — both appear together.
- **`8K` does NOT apply to Treatment A.** A flat graphic style-board is not a photograph; adding 8K there invites photographic texture into a deliberately flat poster. **A carries no camera line and no 8K** — *except* the A-variant's photoreal foreground, which carries both.
- **⚠️ `8K` vs B2's deliberate imperfection.** B2 is meant to look like a phone snap; 8K pulls toward clinical sharpness and can fight the *"slight handheld imperfection, no cinematic grading"* clause. Keep **both** — 8K raises detail, the imperfection clause keeps the register — but if a B2 render comes back looking like a studio shot, the fix is to strengthen the candid clause, **not** to drop 8K.
- **Inter in practice:** image models don't reliably know a font by name, so write it as a **description plus the name** — *"Inter (a clean geometric grotesque sans-serif), ExtraBold"* — and put the alternatives in the negatives. Naming it alone is not enough.
- **"One logo" vs branded packaging.** Kapruka branding printed on a **real attached asset** (van, bag, flower box, cake box, gift packaging) is **part of the product, not a second logo** — it doesn't breach this rule. But it does make the composited logo redundant: **when a brand asset already carries the mark prominently in frame, consider dropping the composited lockup entirely rather than having both.** What is banned is two *placed* logos.

### 5.2 · TEXT TREATMENT — how the in-frame copy sits

**T0 — NO IN-FRAME TEXT.** *(added 2026-07-25 — in-frame copy is NOT mandatory.)* The image carries the whole idea; **no headline, no support line.** The **logo still appears** — that is brand attribution, not copy; no tagline beneath it by default (§7). Meta's own primary-text and headline fields do the talking.
  - **Use it when:** the product *is* the message (pure premium still life) · the metaphor is strong enough that a headline would explain the joke and kill it · a carousel frame that only has to be beautiful · brand/emotional work where silence reads as confidence.
  - **⛔ Do NOT use it when the ad makes a claim.** Anything leaning on the moat — *dated delivery, from anywhere in the world, 23+ years, island-wide reach* — must state it **in frame.** You cannot offload an honest-claims obligation (Core Rule 9) into the caption: captions are truncated, skipped, and stripped when the image is reshared. Broad-brand rows almost always need type, because with no persona named the line is doing the positioning.
  - **The test:** cover the caption. If a stranger cannot tell **what is being sold and by whom** in one second, T0 is the wrong call — go to T1/T2/T3.
  - Pairs with any visual treatment; most natural with **B1** (product-only still life) and **A**.

**T1 — GRAPHIC-INTEGRATED.** Type is **part of the flat composition** — set on the solid Kapruka ground with intentional negative space, the way the style-boards already do it. Headline, support and logo are compositional elements, not an overlay. *(Native partner to Visual A.)*

**T2 — SPLIT / THIRDS BAND.** The frame is **divided**, photo above, solid colour below:
  - **TOP TWO-THIRDS — THE PHOTO.** The photoreal image, full-bleed to the left, right and top edges. Nothing overlaps it — **no type on the photo at all.**
  - **BOTTOM THIRD — SOLID PURPLE BAND.** A flat **Retail Purple `#340A5C`** band carrying the headline (Inter ExtraBold, one `#FFC107` highlight word), the support line (soft white/warm grey) and the attached logo.
  - **The edge between them is HARD and STRAIGHT** at exactly ~2/3 frame height — **no fade, no gradient, no feather, no drop shadow, the photo does not bleed into the band.** State this explicitly; models love to soften it.
  - *(Partners with Visual B1 or B2. This is the cleanest way to run heavy copy on a photoreal image without touching the photo.)*

**T3 — TYPE DIRECT ON PHOTO, NO OVERLAY.** Full-bleed photoreal image, type set **straight onto the photograph** with **no scrim, no gradient wash, no dark box, no blur panel behind it.** Legibility is earned by **composing real negative space into the shot** — a plain wall, a blurred background, an empty table surface, sky — and putting the type there. **Premium feel; the most restrained option.**
  - This means the *prompt must specify the empty area* the type will sit in ("upper-left third is an evenly-lit plain cream wall, unbroken, no props"), or the render won't leave room.
  - If the concept can't hold clean negative space, **use T2 instead** — don't reach for a scrim. A scrim/overlay is a **reject** under this treatment.
  - *(Partners with Visual B1 or B2.)*

### 5.3 · LAYOUT — the optional third tag *(added 2026-07-25, human rule)*

Most prompts need no layout tag. One pattern is common and specific enough to be named:

**G · PRODUCT GRID** — a multi-product grid showing the **range** of gift options in a single frame.
- **When to use it:** the job is **breadth** — *"look how much choice there is"* · answering *"I don't know what to get her/him"* · occasion round-ups · category showcases · "gifts under X." It is the **only format that argues choice visually**, which makes it the literal expression of the brand tagline, **"A world of choices."**
- **Cell count: 4–9. Six is the sweet spot.** Fewer than 4 isn't a grid (it's a group shot); 10+ becomes a catalogue page and loses one-second comprehension.
- **Geometry:** 2×2 (4) · 3×2 (6) · 2×4 (8) · 3×3 (9). Equal cells, even gutters, exact alignment. **Name the exact grid in the prompt** — never leave it to chance.
- **Consistency is the entire craft.** Identical lighting direction, identical camera angle, identical scale logic and identical ground in **every** cell. Mixed angles or mixed lighting = catalogue mess = instant reject. Say this explicitly in the prompt; it is the #1 failure mode of the format.
- **Distinct products, every one a real verified listing** (Rule 2b). Every cell a different category — a grid of near-identical products destroys the very argument the format exists to make.
- **Composes with the visual treatment:** `A + G` = clean product cut-outs on flat colour cells (premium poster grid) · `B1 + G` = photoreal products shot in one consistent studio setup.
- **⚠️ Palette guardrail.** Many products = many colours, which fights the 70/15/10/5 ratio hard. Hold it by keeping the **ground and gutters dominantly purple or cream** and scaling each product modestly inside its cell — the grid's *structure* carries the frame, not the products.
- **Text pairing:** **T1** (headline above/below the grid) or **T2** (band beneath). **T3 rarely works** — a grid leaves no clean negative space to set type into.
- **⛔ Anti-slop:** it must read as a **premium editorial grid**, never an e-commerce search-results page. No price tags, no "SHOP NOW" buttons, no star ratings, no discount badges, no borders/boxes around each cell, no drop-shadow collage, no app UI chrome.

### 5.4 · Batch mix
Each batch is a deliberate **mix across both axes** — not all A, not all T2 — plus a mix of **cast** (with-people / no-people / elements-only / product-only). Tag every prompt so the range is visible at a glance, and state the batch's spread in a small table at the top of the document.

The A/B1/B2/B3 visual treatments, the T0–T3 text treatments, the **Realism Floor (§5.1-R)**, the one-headline-plus-one-support limit (Core Rule 16), the no-stroke-on-yellow ban (§6d) and every honesty rule all still bind. **Where a reference contradicts a rule, the rule wins** — borrow the device, not the violation. Each entry below names the device, when it earns its place, and what to watch.

### Text/layout devices

**L1 · INSET PHOTO CARD.** The photo sits as a **rounded-corner card inset on a flat page** (cream/solid ground) rather than bleeding to the frame edge; the headline lives on the page *above* the card, not on the photo. The subject may **break out of the card** past its edge, which is what stops it reading as a plain box. *Use when:* you want photo warmth plus poster cleanliness, or the subject's silhouette is strong enough to break the frame. *Watch:* it adds a text zone outside the photo — still only **one headline + one support line total** (Core Rule 16).

**L2 · FEATURE STRIP.** A row of **3–5 small outline icons with a one-or-two-word label under each**, set in a band beneath the main image. *Use when:* an ad genuinely has several parallel, factual points that resist being said in one line (a service's real attributes). *Watch:* icon labels count as copy — this is the one device that legitimately exceeds the two-block limit, so use it **only for factual attribute rows**, never to smuggle in extra selling lines. Every label must be true and confirmable (Core Rule 9).

**L3 · OVERSIZED NUMERAL.** A very large number anchors the layout, with the rest of the headline set small and tight beside or around it. *Use when:* a real count carries the idea (5 stages, 7 departments, 23 years). *Watch:* the number must be **true and approved** — this device makes a claim visually louder than any sentence, and it fails the One-Second Test the moment the count needs verifying.

**L4 · BOXED / HIGHLIGHTED WORD.** One word inside a solid filled rectangle within the headline, carrying the emphasis instead of a colour change. *Use when:* the ground can't hold a yellow highlight word. **This is the approved alternative to a yellow highlight on a light ground** — and unlike the retired stroke fix (§6d), a clean filled box is a legitimate typographic device, not a sticker effect.

**L5 · TYPE CENTRED OVER A FULL-BLEED PHOTO.** Bold sans headline set straight across the middle of the image, unapologetically centred. *Use when:* the photo has enough motion, blur or tonal calm in the middle band to carry type without a scrim. *Watch:* this is **T3 with a centred axis** — the no-scrim rule still binds absolutely. If it needs a dark box behind it, it's T2 instead.

**L7 · WHITE STACK + PRICE CHIP (the "retail corner" lockup).** *(Human-supplied reference, 2026-10-03.)* A complete corner lockup, all four parts in one vertical column, flush-left in the upper-left (or upper-right) of a **full-bleed photo with no scrim**: **(1)** the **logo**, small and white, at the top · **(2)** the **headline in HEAVY WHITE Inter ExtraBold**, broken into **2–3 very short stacked lines of 2–3 words each**, tight leading (~1.1×), **each line ending in a full stop** (*"Same Denim. / New Angles."*) · **(3)** a **product descriptor** in light white Inter Medium at roughly a third of the headline size, naming the actual product (*"White Stripe Denim"*) · **(4)** a **price chip** — the real price in **dark Retail Purple `#340A5C` Inter Bold inside a solid Kapruka Yellow `#FFC107` rounded rectangle** (~12px radius), sized tight to the text.
- **⭐ What makes it work — and the rule it changes:** the headline is **white, not purple**, which only holds because the photo is deliberately shot into a **darker, warmer, busier environment** (a lamp-lit interior, a dusk exterior, a shaded wall). **The prompt must COMPOSE that dark area, not hope for it** — name the exact zone the lockup sits in and its value. This is the mirror image of the existing light-ground guidance: same no-scrim principle, opposite colour answer.
- **Use when:** a specific, named, real product with a real price is the point — the retail/offer register. Pairs naturally with a **two-up or series layout** (the reference runs two frames of one product side by side, same lockup position, different pose and setting).
- **⛔ SCOPE NOTE — this device carries a PRICE, which is product/offer advertising.** The project default excludes SKUs, prices and deals unless the human explicitly flags one (root `CLAUDE.md`). **The human flagged it for this device on 2026-10-03, so the price chip is IN for L7** — but the price must be the **real, current, verified price of the named listing**, and a price that can't be verified means the chip is dropped, not guessed (Core Rule 9).
- **⛔ Copy-block accounting:** headline + descriptor + price chip reads as **three elements**, so L7 is a **declared, narrow exception to the two-block ceiling** — exactly like L2's feature strip, and permitted for the same reason: the extra elements are **factual product identifiers (what it is, what it costs)**, never additional selling lines. **No support sentence may be added on top of an L7 lockup.** If a concept wants a support line, it is not an L7 frame.
- **⛔ Watch:** the logo in this lockup is **the frame's one logo** (the Four Invariants still bind — never add a second in another corner). Yellow appears **only** in the chip, nowhere else in the frame. The headline stays **direct, no wordplay** (Core Rule 15) — the reference's own *"Good Jeans Bring Good People"* is a pun and **must not be copied**; L7 takes the reference's *construction*, never its wordplay.

**L8 · LABELLED CAST GRID (L7 miniaturised and repeated).** *(Added 2026-10-03, from the human's brief "like for PT master this gift, likewise, but creative.")* **L7's corner lockup shrunk to roughly a third scale and repeated once per grid cell**, so the type stops being a headline and becomes **a cast list**. Each cell carries **one 1–3 word ALL-CAPS label naming a role or recipient** (`PT MASTER` · `CLASS TEACHER` · `ART MISS`), set in an **L4 solid plate** at an **identical position inside every cell**, with the object beside it answering that specific label. *Use when:* the job is range but a plain product grid would read as inventory — the labels recast the grid as **people instead of products**, which is the one grid structure that argues breadth without looking like a search-results page.
- **⭐ Why it works:** the label pre-loads the meaning, so the object only has to deliver the payoff. A six-cell grid needs six good product ideas; a labelled grid needs six good *pairings*, which is a much richer brief. **It also makes Core Rule 12 self-enforcing** — once the cells are named for six different people, a cake in every cell becomes visibly absurd, so the department spread stops being a quota to remember and becomes a consequence of the device.
- **⛔ Copy accounting — a declared narrow exception**, on the same grounds as L2's feature strip and L7's descriptor+chip: **each label is a factual identifier naming who the cell is for, never an additional selling line.** Bounded by four hard conditions: every label is **1–3 words, all caps, naming a role only** · **all labels typographically identical** (same size, weight, case, plate, position — a label that differs has become a second headline) · **no support sentence anywhere in the frame** · **no label may carry a claim, price, benefit, product name or a joke in words.** The object carries the humour; if a label is trying to be funny, delete the words and fix the object.
- **⛔ Exactly ONE yellow label per frame** — the deliberate first eye-stop and the entire yellow budget (per the single-accent discipline of swipe IMG-0020). Yellow appears nowhere else. No stroke on it, ever.
- **⛔ Watch — the labels are TYPE, not logos.** The Four Invariants still bind: one Kapruka logo per frame, on the ground, **never inside a cell.**
- **⛔ Watch — authenticity lives in the label wording.** Use the words local students actually use (*PT MASTER*, *MATHS SIR*, *ART MISS*) — anglicising them to "Gym Teacher" or "Form Tutor" throws away the device's single biggest asset.
- **⛔ Watch — G's consistency rule binds harder than usual.** Identical viewing angle, light direction, shadow softness and scale logic in every cell; mixed angles or lighting is an instant reject. In the near-identical-object variant it is not merely craft — any variation destroys the idea outright.
- **First run:** `3-output/reference-recreations/teachers-day-labelled-cast-grid-2026-10-03.md`.

**L6 · SCRIPT + SWOOSH OVERLAY.** A script/handwritten-feel word with a thin drawn circle or ellipse looping around it. **⛔ NOT AVAILABLE TO KAPRUKA** — §8 bans every non-Inter face, including script, in **every** element. Listed here only so it is explicitly recognised and rejected rather than quietly copied from a reference.

### Visual devices

**V1 · EXTREME CROP + REFLECTION.** A face cropped very tight, with the product appearing **reflected in a lens, mirror or glass surface** rather than held. *Use when:* the idea is about seeing/noticing/point of view. *Watch:* the reflection must be physically plausible — a pasted-in product that ignores the surface curve is an instant AI tell (§1).

**V2 · SPLIT BEFORE/AFTER.** One frame divided down the middle, same subject, two states. Already in the device menu (§0a) — the references confirm it reads instantly at thumbnail. *Watch:* **honesty (Core Rule 9)** — a before/after implies a claim about what the product does. Only run it where the transformation is literally true.

**V3 · ARMFUL / ABUNDANCE.** A person holding an **armful of products fanned toward camera** — abundance as the visual argument. *Use when:* the job is range or generosity. *Watch:* Core Rule 12 — more than one product means **more than one department**, or it argues the opposite of "a world of choices."

**V4 · SUBJECT BREAKING THE FRAME.** The subject deliberately crosses a card edge, band edge or panel line (pairs naturally with L1). *Use when:* a layout needs depth and energy without extra elements.

### The declared exception

**C — SURREAL / CG COMPOSITE.** *(Human decision, 2026-09-29 — approved as its own tagged treatment.)* Deliberately impossible, digitally-composited imagery: an animal in human dress, a physically impossible object, a staged visual conceit that is **openly not a photograph.**

- **This treatment sits outside the PHOTOGRAPHIC floor (§5.1-R) — by declaration, not by accident — but it is fully bound by the MATERIAL floor (§5.1-M).** §5.1-R exists to stop photoreal work drifting into fake-looking ad imagery; **C is not pretending to be photoreal**, so that block does not apply. **§5.1-M does apply, in full**: the scene may be impossible, but fur, fabric, skin, metal and glass must all behave like the real thing, light and shadow must be consistent across every composited element, contact shadows must be present, anatomy must be correct, and nothing may look plastic, waxy or melted. **Impossible scene, real surfaces.** C must still be **tagged explicitly in the prompt** — an untagged CG-looking render is still a §5.1-R failure and still a reject.
- **Choose it deliberately, never by drift.** If a concept is a real moment with real people, it is B — and the Floor binds in full. C is for concepts whose whole point is the impossible image.
- **Craft bar still applies:** the composite must be *excellently* made — correct light wrap, consistent shadow direction, real fur/fabric/material rendering, no melted or warped detail. A badly-made composite is not "stylised," it is broken (§1's anti-AI-tell rules still bind on materials and anatomy).
- **Everything else still binds:** 4:5, Inter only, one logo, honesty, no third-party brands, Core Rule 12 breadth, the copy rules, and §1g's colour-grade discipline.
- **⚠️ Use sparingly.** The human's standing direction (2026-09-27) is *more* realism, not less. C is a deliberate tool for a specific kind of idea — not a licence to return to glossy composite work across a batch.

## 5b. EXECUTION VARIANCE — judge the CONCEPT, not one render (best-of-N)
Image models are stochastic: the **same prompt** yields strong *and* weak renders (proven — one Kapruka prompt gave both a winning layout and a broken one: box too big/floating, skyline squashed, logo boxed). **A weak render does NOT mean a weak concept.** So:
- **Generate at least 3–4 variations per prompt and judge on the BEST one.** The pick gate is on the best render of the concept — never the first, never the average.
- **Never kill a concept on a single generation.** Only reconsider the *idea* if it fails across multiple good-faith renders — and even then, **tighten the prompt first** and re-run before abandoning it.
### ⭐ EXPLORE or EXPLOIT — declare ONE per prompt, never both *(added 2026-07-25, human decision)*

> **The contradiction this fixes.** The old rule said *"generate 3–4 variations and keep the best"* **and** *"every prompt should carry a LOCK LAYOUT pinning placement, scale and grounding."* Those fight each other: a fully locked prompt returns four near-identical renders, so you pay for best-of-N and get none of its benefit. Locking is how you *reduce* variance; best-of-N is how you *exploit* variance. Pick the one the concept needs.

**`Mode: EXPLORE`** — the composition is genuinely open and you want the model to find it.
- Describe the **elements, the mood and the idea**; do **not** pin placement, scale or arrangement.
- **Generate 4–6 variations and pick the best.** Expect several to fail — that is the method working.
- **Use when:** a new or device-led concept, a metaphor whose staging isn't obvious, or any first attempt at an idea. **This is the right default for anything from the §0a device menu.**

**`Mode: EXPLOIT`** — you already know the composition you want.
- Carry the full **🔒 LOCK LAYOUT** block: exact vertical position, subject **scale as a % of frame width**, how it sits ("on the ground line with a soft contact shadow, not floating"), logo treatment.
- **Generate 1–2 renders.** More is waste — a locked prompt has little left to vary.
- **Use when:** re-running a layout that already worked, producing a variant of a proven winner, or a piece whose geometry is fixed anyway.

**The healthy loop is EXPLORE → then EXPLOIT:** explore an idea loose, pick the render whose composition works, then write that composition into a locked prompt for production and variants.

**⚠️ Brand invariants are NOT part of this choice.** EXPLORE frees *composition* only. These stay pinned in every prompt regardless of mode: the **T2 hard straight split edge**, the **T3 no-scrim rule**, **safe margins**, **logo treatment (plain, no panel)**, correct spelling, and every honesty and third-party-branding constraint. Exploration is about where things sit, never about the brand system.

## 5c. PROMPT DEPTH — every prompt is FULLY detailed (no shortcuts)
Every prompt block is written to the full depth of the reference exemplar (`3-output/reference-recreations/cannes-01…` **Prompt 3 "The Moat, Reversed"**). **Never abbreviate.** Required, in order, every time:
0a. **`Comedy: yes` or `Comedy: no` — declared as the absolute first line, before Visual Idea (Rule 0's Visual Comedy principle).** When `yes`, the joke must be grounded in something specifically Sri Lankan (§1a) — not a generic joke relocated to a Sri Lankan setting.
0b. **⭐ VISUAL IDEA — declared next (Rule 0a).** One line naming the **device** the picture uses (seam · substitution · absence · scale · impossible object · transformation · before/after · **remix/fusion** — see below), plus a **`Blank-headline read:`** line stating what a viewer takes from the image with the copy covered. If the piece is deliberately a product hero, write **`Visual idea: none — product hero (functional/offer piece)`** and say why. **A prompt that skips this item defaults to a product poster — which is exactly the failure this item exists to prevent.**
1. **Format line — carrying the MANDATORY camera spec for anything photographic (Rule 5.1a) and the output invariants (Rule 5.1b).** Full descriptor + **`1080×1350 (4:5)`** + **`8K` if photographic** + **device + lens + shooting conditions**:
   - **A (graphic):** *"A flat, premium GRAPHIC advertising poster (style-board look, NOT a photographic environment), 1080×1350 (4:5)"* — no camera line and **no 8K**, it isn't a photograph.
   - **B1 (Canon):** *"Photorealistic commercial lifestyle photograph, **8K**, vertical 4:5 format (1080×1350) for social media ad. Shot on a professional camera (Canon 5D Mark IV, 50mm lens)"* — or the 35mm f/2.8 form for a wider frame.
   - **B2 (iPhone):** *"Photorealistic candid photo, **8K**, vertical 4:5 format (1080×1350), shot on an iPhone 15 Pro rear camera, natural morning light, slight handheld imperfection, true-to-life colors, minor phone-lens distortion, no cinematic grading, no HDR glow — looks like an unposed photo taken by a coworker, not a rendered or AI image."*
   - **B3 (Editorial/Documentary — "Maker Portrait"):** *"Photorealistic editorial documentary photograph, **8K**, vertical 4:5 format (1080×1350) for social media. Shot on a full-frame DSLR/mirrorless camera, 50mm lens (or 35mm when the environment needs to stay readable), aperture f/2.0–f/2.8, shallow depth of field, focal plane sharp on the subject's eyes falling into soft bokeh, eye-level camera height, one large soft directional natural window light from the side, low contrast, gentle falloff into shadow, no flash, no studio lighting, fine natural film grain, mild vignetting, gentle highlight roll-off, realistic lens character, candid in-between moment, subject absorbed in their task and not posing, natural micro-expression, hands in natural motion."*
2. **Background / frame structure** — hex + treatment + negative space (*"Solid flat Retail Purple #340A5C, even, no gradient, vast intentional negative space"*). **If the text treatment is T2 (split/thirds), specify the split here:** where the hard edge falls (~2/3 height), that the top two-thirds is the photo full-bleed with **no type on it**, that the bottom third is a solid `#340A5C` band, and that the edge is **hard and straight — no fade, gradient, feather or bleed.** **If T3 (type direct on photo), name the clean negative-space area** the type will occupy (*"upper-left third is an evenly-lit plain cream wall, unbroken, no props"*) and state **no scrim/gradient/box behind the type.**
3. **Composition** — element-by-element placement; the flat shorthand / scene described concretely (what, where, colour, scale).
4. **Headline** — line-by-line: **typeface named as `Inter (clean geometric grotesque sans-serif)`** + weight + px size + colour + placement, and the `#FFC107` highlight word called out if one is used.
5. **Support** — the exact copy + **`Inter`** + weight + px + colour + placement.
6. **Hero / product** — described in detail + the FULL attach instruction where a brand asset is used.
7. **Logo — the FULL instruction, never shorthand, and exactly ONE per frame (Rule 5.1b):** *"Composite the ATTACHED Kapruka logo exactly as provided, top-centre ~200px — do not redraw, distort or recolour, and place it PLAIN with NO background box/panel behind it. **Exactly one Kapruka logo in the entire image — no second logo anywhere, no repeat in a corner, none inside a grid cell.**"* + tagline line.
8. **Safe margins** — state them (≥80–90px).
9. **🔒 LOCK LAYOUT** — numbered points pinning placement/scale/grounding + *"Generate 3–4 variations and keep the one that matches this layout."*
10. **NEGATIVE** — the FULL detailed list (not a 3–4 item abbreviation).

> ❌ Not acceptable: *"Logo attached, top-centre ~200px, plain."* · a 4-word negative list · a headline with no per-line sizes. ✅ The exemplar is the **minimum** bar — match its granularity on every prompt, in every document.

## 5d. COMPOSITION & LABEL LEGIBILITY (every product placement, every scenario)
- **Tidy, deliberate placement.** Every product/hero is placed with intent — **grounded** on the surface/ground line with a soft contact shadow, and **composed in relation to nearby elements** (nestled at the base of a prop, aligned to the ground line, tucked beside — not overlapping awkwardly, never floating, never randomly dropped, never oversized). Name the exact anchor in the 🔒 LOCK LAYOUT line (e.g. *"parcel nestled at the base of the gate post, sitting on the ground line"*).
- **Legible labels — always.** Any on-image label — a product/pack label (e.g. "Kapruka Global Shop"), a badge, stamp, price chip, or UI text — must be sized and rendered for **clear readability**: large enough to read at a glance, sharp-edged, correctly spelled, high-contrast on its background. **Never shrink a brand/product label into illegibility.** Spell out the label's exact text, size and placement in the prompt.
- **⛔ THE PRODUCT MUST SEPARATE FROM ITS GROUND** *(added 2026-07-26).* **Every product named in a prompt has to be findable in the frame at ~250px.** Before writing the scene, check the **tonal and colour contrast between each product and the surface it sits on** — a dark product on a dark surface disappears, however accurately it is rendered. **If a product's real colour matches its setting, change the setting, not the product** (put it on a lighter surface, a cloth, a tray, a light-coloured seat, a windowsill) or move it into direct light. **State the contrast explicitly in the prompt**, e.g. *"the black key cover rests on a folded cream cloth so it separates clearly from the dark console."*
  > **Caught live 2026-07-26:** a car-interior ad named four products — black headrest cushions on black seats, a black key cover in a black tray, a black charger in a black console, and a white helmet. Only the helmet read. Three of the four products were technically present and effectively invisible, so a viewer saw *a photo of a car interior*, not an ad for four things. **A product the viewer can't find is a product that isn't in the ad** — this is the same failure as illegible labels, one level up.
  >
  > **The check:** name each product, name the surface under it, and ask *"light on dark, or dark on light?"* If the answer is "dark on dark" or "light on light" for any of them, fix the staging before writing another line.

## 6. Palette — a BRAND SYSTEM, not a per-ad quota *(revised 2026-07-25, human decision)*

> **What changed and why.** The ratio **70% purple · 15% white-neutral · 10% yellow-gold · 5% accent** used to be enforced on **every single ad**, together with "exactly ONE `#FFC107` highlight word." The arithmetic guaranteed a purple ground every time, so every graphic piece came out as *product-cut-out-on-purple* and the yellow word became a tic the viewer learns to skip. **The colours are the brand; the ratio is not.** *(Caught live 2026-07-25 — five graphic ads back-to-back with no visual variety.)*

**The locked part (unchanged — this IS the brand):**
- **Real attached assets keep their true colours:** Kapruka Purple **`#4B2386`**, Kapruka Yellow **`#FFD21E`** — never recolour the van, bag, flower box, cake box or packaging.
- **The brand colours themselves are fixed:** Retail Purple **`#340A5C`** · highlight yellow **`#FFC107`** · Soft Cream **`#F5F4EB`** · Luxury Gold **`#D9A441`** · Deep Purple **`#241044`**. Do not invent new brand colours or drift the hexes.

**The freed part (now a choice per ad):**
- **The ground is a creative decision.** Retail Purple is the **default and the anchor**, not a requirement. Also legitimate: **Soft Cream**, **editorial white**, **near-black / Deep Purple**, a **real photographic environment**, or a **product-colour environment** where the category earns it. Red grounds are no longer banned outright — but red must be *chosen for a reason* (a Valentine's or romance piece), never a default, and never so saturated it reads as another brand.
- **⛔ The `#FFC107` highlight — the STRONG DEFAULT, reversed 2026-08-16.** *(Human rule — caught live after 3 of 5 flower prompts in a batch shipped with no yellow at all, which read as under-branded.)* **Whenever on-image copy is present, one word or two should be set in Kapruka Yellow `#FFC107`.** This reverses the 2026-07-25 "fully optional" softening — that change let too many frames drop yellow entirely by default. Only skip it with an **explicit, stated reason** (a genuine legibility conflict, or a deliberate tone call like a sympathy/grief piece) — and when legibility is the blocker, **try to solve it before dropping yellow**: change the ground the type sits on, add a small yellow accent element elsewhere in frame (an icon, a ribbon, a sliver of the logo's own yellow), or move the highlight word to a line that *does* sit on a workable ground — don't default straight to "no yellow" because the first-choice wall happens to be pale. **What remains banned either way is yellow sprawl** (see 6b): yellow stays a small, deliberate accent — one or two words, never a field.
- **The ratio is now a guide across the BODY OF WORK, not within one frame.** Judge it at the level of a batch or a month: does the output as a whole still read unmistakably Kapruka? A single ad may be 90% cream or 90% black. **A batch where every ad is 70% purple is now the failure**, not the target.

**The one thing to hold:** whatever the ground, the piece must still be **identifiably Kapruka in one second** — via the logo, the real packaging assets, the type discipline, or the purple appearing somewhere with intent. Freedom in ground, not in identity.

### 6b. ⚠️ GOLD ≠ YELLOW, and the ratio is arithmetic *(added 2026-07-21 — renders keep breaking this)*
- **Say it in the prompt, every time gold shapes appear:** *"Luxury Gold `#D9A441` — a MUTED, DESATURATED, ANTIQUE gold, NOT bright yellow. Do not let it collapse into `#FFC107`/`#FFD21E`."* Without that sentence the model merges the two golds, and any highlight stops highlighting anything.
- **Yellow stays a small, deliberate accent — never a field.** *(Revised 2026-07-25: the old rule was "exactly ONCE in the frame — the highlight word." The highlight word is now **optional** (§6), but the anti-sprawl discipline stands.)* Where a highlight word is used, keep it to **one, occasionally two** — and keep yellow off the support line, rules, dots, icons, numerals, badges and footers unless it is doing real work. **Support lines are soft white or warm grey.** A frame that comes back substantially bright yellow is still a reject.
- **Check the coverage arithmetic BEFORE writing the device.** A 4×5 grid of gold icons, or a gold shape at 78% frame width, cannot sit inside a 10% gold budget however it renders — the brief itself is incompatible with the ratio. Shrink the device (fewer icons, narrower shape) so the canvas still reads ~70% purple. *(Caught live: an Ecom app poster came back at roughly 35–40% bright yellow.)*
- **A render that comes back bright-yellow is a reject** — tighten the prompt and regenerate; don't ship it.

### 6c. ⭐ SCROLL-STOPPING CONTRAST — colours must POP, not just comply *(added 2026-08-20, human rule)*
> **The failure this exists to stop:** a prompt can obey every hex, every ratio, and every negative in this rulebook and still generate a flat, low-energy image that a thumb scrolls past without registering. Brand-compliant is not the same as arresting. **Every prompt must be written for stopping power, not just correctness.**

- **State the contrast explicitly in every prompt.** Name the specific pairing doing the work — e.g. *"Retail Purple `#340A5C` ground against Soft Cream `#F5F4EB` subject — high value contrast, the eye lands on the product first"* or *"Kapruka Yellow `#FFC107` highlight word popping hard off the dark purple ground."* Don't leave contrast to the model's default — spell out which element is the darkest, which is the brightest, and that the gap between them must be large, not muted or washed-out.
- **The hero subject must separate cleanly from its ground** — via colour contrast, not just a drop shadow. If the product/subject's real colours are close in value to the chosen ground (e.g. a cream cake on a cream background), either change the ground or add a deliberate contrast element (a purple ribbon, a yellow accent, a darker shadow plane) so the subject reads instantly, not just correctly.
- **Saturation and clarity over muted/washed-out rendering.** Within the locked brand hexes (§6), push for clean, vivid colour reproduction — not a hazy, desaturated, or overly soft render that technically matches the palette but reads flat on a feed. This is about rendering fidelity, not licence to drift off-brand: the hexes stay locked, the *execution* of them must not go muddy.
- **This works alongside the ratio and the highlight-word rule (§6/§6b), never against them.** Contrast is a call for *punch within the brand system* — it does not license oversized yellow fields, off-brand colours, or abandoning the 70/15/10/5 body-of-work guide. A prompt can be high-contrast and still yellow-sprawl-free.
- **Run the test:** shrink the described frame to thumbnail size in your head (per the One-Second Test, §0a) — does it still grab the eye, or does it disappear into the feed? If it disappears, the prompt needs a stronger contrast statement, not just a compliant one.

### 6d. ⚠️ YELLOW NEEDS A DARK GROUND — the T3/light-wall trap *(added 2026-08-20, caught live on the "Nobody saw this coming" doorstep prompt)*
> **The failure:** the `#FFC107` highlight-word default (§6) assumes the word will land somewhere dark enough to hold it. On **T2** that's guaranteed — the highlight always sits in the solid Retail Purple band. On **T3** (type direct on photo, no scrim) there is no such guarantee — if the composed negative space is a light/cream/white wall or surface, the yellow highlight sits light-on-light and the contrast collapses exactly the way §6c warns against, even though the rest of the headline (in dark purple) reads fine.

- **Before placing a yellow highlight word in a T3 prompt, name the ground value it's landing on.** If that ground is light (cream, white, pale wood, bright sky), the yellow highlight is a **legibility conflict** under §6's existing exception — handle it with one of, in order of preference:
  1. **Move the highlight word to fall on the darkest patch of the composed negative space** (a shadow, a doorway, a darker material) — same word, same colour, just repositioned.
  2. **Drop the yellow highlight for that prompt and keep the full headline in dark Retail Purple** — the stated, legitimate exception per §6; don't force yellow onto a ground that can't hold it.
- **⛔ NO OUTLINE OR STROKE OF ANY COLOUR ON YELLOW TYPE, EVER** *(added 2026-09-27, human rule — caught live, a purple outline stroke behind a yellow headline word read as a cheap sticker/decal effect, not premium type).* This retires the earlier "add a thin dark-purple stroke/shadow behind the yellow word" fix — it is **no longer an option.** Yellow type is either solid `#FFC107` with no outline, or it is not used on that ground at all (fall back to option 2 above). Applies everywhere yellow type appears, on any ground, any Treatment — not just T3.
- **T2 is unaffected** — the purple band is always dark, so the highlight word never needs this check there. This rule is scoped to **T3, and any T1/A composition where the negative space the type sits in is genuinely light-toned.**

## 7. Tagline + invented sub-text (revised 2026-08-21; hardened to a ban 2026-09-29)
**⛔ The tagline is NOT placed beneath the composited logo by default.** *(Reversed 2026-08-21 — earlier this rulebook required "A world of choices" under every logo lockup; that requirement is dropped.)* The logo stands alone — mark + wordmark only, no tagline line beneath it, unless a specific concept deliberately calls for one as part of its own copy (e.g. a broad-brand piece where the tagline itself is the headline's point). **"Every Bite. Pure Happiness."** remains printed on the real Kapruka cake box packaging asset — that's part of the physical product, not a composited tagline, and is unaffected by this change.

> **⛔ HARDENED TO A BAN — NO TAGLINE UNDER THE LOGO, AND NO INVENTED SUB-TEXT ANYWHERE** *(human rule, 2026-09-29: "avoid subtext" — caught live on three renders at once).* The 2026-08-21 wording above was a **soft default** ("unless a concept calls for one"), carried no entry in the global negatives, and was therefore ignored for five weeks. It is now a hard ban with two halves:
>
> **(a) The logo lockup is mark + wordmark ONLY.** No tagline line beneath it, **no yellow divider rule beneath it**, no descriptor, no strapline — nothing under the logo at all. *(Caught live 2026-09-29: two renders placed "A world of choices" under the composited logo, one of them with a yellow rule under that as well — making a **fourth** text block in a frame already budgeted for a headline and one support line under §1g.)* **The tagline counts as a copy block.** If a concept genuinely wants "A world of choices" in the frame, it goes in **as the headline or the support line, spending that budget** — never as a free extra hanging under the mark.
>
> **(b) No invented sub-text printed on any object in the frame** — packaging, boxes, bags, labels, tags, signage, vehicle panels, aprons, cups. *(Caught live 2026-09-29: a redrawn cake box came back carrying "Every Bite, Pure Happiness" on the lid **and** "A World of Happiness" beneath the wordmark on the front — neither is a Kapruka line; "A World of Happiness" is not even the real tagline.)* This is a **stronger rule than the material floor's "no garbled sub-text"**: garbled text is obviously broken and gets rejected on sight, whereas **cleanly-rendered invented brand copy looks legitimate and ships** — which makes it worse. An object carries text in only two cases: it is **the attached real asset** (whatever is genuinely printed on it is part of the physical product), or the prompt **names the exact string** to render. Anything else: the surface stays blank. **⛔ Never let the model decide what a package says.**
>
> **Why this keeps happening:** the asset descriptions in `tools/ig2img/lib/prompt.mjs` tell the model each package carries a "wordmark + tagline." Read as a *description* that's accurate; read as an *instruction to draw*, it licenses exactly this. Those descriptions exist to help the model **recognise and place an attached file**, never to reconstruct one — see the attach-don't-redraw discipline in §3.

## 8. Typography + logo *(hardened 2026-07-26 — see Rule 5.1b)*
**Inter, and nothing else** (ExtraBold/Bold headlines, Medium/SemiBold support) — crisp, correctly spelled. **No serif, no script, no slab, no display or decorative face, no handwriting — in any element**, including labels, numerals, badges and footers. Because image models don't reliably know a font by name, write it as **description + name**: *"Inter (a clean geometric grotesque sans-serif), ExtraBold"* — and list the banned alternatives in the negatives. Naming Inter alone is not enough.

**⛔ Inter identity check — a generic default sans is a reject** *(added 2026-08-16, caught live).* Naming "Inter (a clean geometric grotesque sans-serif)" is not always enough to stop a model drifting to its own default UI sans (a generic Arial/Helvetica/system-font look). Strengthen the description with what actually distinguishes Inter — **tall x-height, open apertures on the c/e/a, a straight-cut single-story "a," near-square dots on the i/j** — and treat any render that comes back reading as a stock system font as a **reject**, not a close-enough pass.

**⛔ Font-size ranges are a FLOOR, not a suggestion** *(added 2026-08-16, caught live — a footer support line rendered visibly smaller than spec).* Every headline and support-line px value stated in a prompt (Rule 5c items 4-5: headline ~76-92px, support ~32-46px) is the **minimum** size for legibility at thumbnail scale, not an upper bound to drift under. If a render comes back with any on-image text smaller than the size stated in its own prompt, it is a **reject** — regenerate, don't ship it.

**⛔ No em dash in any on-image copy** *(added 2026-08-16).* Headline, support, proof, CTA, label, badge — no line of rendered copy uses an em dash (—). Rewrite the line as two clauses, a comma, a period, or a colon instead. This governs the actual text the image model renders on the picture; it does not apply to this rulebook's own prose.

**Exactly ONE Kapruka logo per picture** — the **attached asset**, composited not redrawn, top-centre ~200px, plain with no panel. Never a second placement, never a repeat "for balance," never one inside a grid cell as well as on the ground. Branding printed on a real attached packaging asset (van, bag, boxes) is part of the product and doesn't count as a second logo — but when an asset already carries the mark prominently, **prefer dropping the composited lockup to having both.** One highlight word max in yellow (and the highlight word itself is optional — §6).

## 9. Honesty (Core Rule 9) + not-AI-slop
Every claim ties to confirmed proof (23+ yrs, island-wide delivery, millions delivered, AU/USA/CA/UAE; Global Shop = 50M+ products, customs handled, ~8–11 days — never overnight). **⛔ The same-day-Colombo / next-day-island-wide timing claim is no longer a standard copy element (removed 2026-08-21)** — don't default to it as a support-line filler; if a specific concept's insight genuinely depends on delivery speed, state only what's separately confirmed for that concept, not this stock phrasing. Reject the ordinary-art-director first idea; the concept lives in a real object/seam/landmark, not a decoration.
