# Kapruka — Common Online-Shopping Fears & Objections

> **Purpose:** These are the fears and objections that stop people from buying online. They power the **pain-led angle** — name the fear, disarm it with proof. The Copywriter checks that any fear-disarming claim answers a *real* fear here, not an imagined one.
>
> **Pain-led is ONE angle type, not the rule.** Don't force a fear into every ad. An ad's job can equally be **desire/positive-led** (joy, love, pride, surprise), **broad-brand**, or **occasion-led** — those ads needn't name a fear at all. Reach for this file when the ad's chosen job *is* to disarm a doubt; skip it when the job is to make someone feel something good. Every ad needs **one clear job** — pain is one option among several.
>
> **How to use (when the ad IS pain-led):** Find the persona's segment below, pick the 1-2 fears most relevant to the angle, and make the ad's proof/authority directly neutralize them. A pain-led ad that names the buyer's specific fear and then disarms it converts far better than one that names a fear and ignores it.
>
> **Status:** v1 — written as hypotheses. `[CONFIRM]` against real Kapruka support tickets, reviews, cart-abandonment data, and customer complaints, which are the truest source of real objections.

## A — UNIVERSAL ONLINE-SHOPPING FEARS (everyone, all segments)

These apply to almost any online buyer in Sri Lanka and shape baseline trust:

1. **"Will it actually arrive?"** — fear the order vanishes after payment.
2. **"Will my money/card be safe?"** — payment security, fear of fraud or card misuse.
3. **"Is this site legit?"** — fear the platform itself is a scam or fly-by-night.
4. **"Will it look like the photo?"** — fear of misrepresentation (especially products and food/cakes).
5. **"What if something goes wrong — can I reach a human?"** — fear of no support, no recourse.
6. **"What if I need to return it?"** — fear of being stuck with the wrong/damaged item.
7. **"Hidden charges"** — fear the final price balloons with delivery/fees at checkout.

**Kapruka's counter (the authority that disarms most of these):** 20+ years operating, island-wide reliable delivery, established local trust. `[CONFIRM exact stats / guarantee / returns policy]` — the ad's proof element should pull from whatever of these is true and approved.

## B — DIASPORA GIFTER FEARS (International → Local, A1)

The highest-stakes segment. The sender **cannot verify anything themselves** — they're thousands of miles away, can't check the shop, can't see the delivery, can't fix a problem in person. Trust fear is at its absolute peak here. These are the fears that lose this customer:

1. **"Will it actually reach my family in Sri Lanka?"** — the core fear. Sending across borders to a place they can't physically check.
2. **"Will it arrive ON the day?"** — for a birthday/anniversary there is no second chance. Late = the whole gift failed. This is the most acute diaspora fear.
3. **"Will it reach the right address / right person?"** — fear of wrong delivery to a relative they can't easily redirect.
4. **"Am I being scammed because I'm overseas?"** — fear that distance makes them an easy target; no way to chase it down from abroad.
5. **"Will my foreign card even work / will I get charged weirdly?"** — international payment friction, currency confusion, card declines.
6. **"Will it look cheap / will my family think I didn't care?"** — the gift represents *them* in their absence. A poor gift reflects badly on them with the people they love most.
7. **"Will the cake/flowers be fresh / not melted/wilted?"** — perishable gift anxiety, amplified because they can't inspect it.
8. **"If it goes wrong, can I even fix it from another country / time zone?"** — fear of being helpless across distance.
9. **"Can I trust this more than just sending money to a relative to handle it?"** — Kapruka's real competitor here is "give cash to an uncle." Fear that the platform is *less* reliable than family.

**What dissolves these (proof to lean on):**
- Dated delivery guarantee ("delivered on the date you choose")
- Proof of successful overseas-to-SL deliveries (volume, years, reviews from diaspora customers)
- Real delivery confirmation / photo / tracking
- Island-wide coverage (reaches even non-urban family addresses) `[CONFIRM]`
- International payment acceptance, shown clearly
- "Better than sending money" framing — done properly, on time, looking good, without burdening family

## C — LOCAL GIFTER FEARS (Local → Local, A2)

The sender *could* do it in person, so their fears are about **whether outsourcing it is safe and worth it**, plus occasion pressure:

1. **"Will it arrive on time?"** — especially last-minute gifters; the occasion has a hard deadline.
2. **"Will it look as good as if I picked it myself?"** — fear of losing control over quality/presentation.
3. **"Will it reach the right place?"** — sending to another town/address they don't control.
4. **"Will the recipient think it's impersonal because I didn't bring it?"** — fear that delivery feels less thoughtful than in-person.
5. **"Is it worth paying delivery vs just doing it myself?"** — convenience-vs-cost hesitation.
6. **"Will the cake/flowers be fresh on arrival?"** — perishable anxiety.
7. **"What if I picked the wrong thing?"** — gift-choice anxiety (strong for romantic gifters: "I never know what to get them").

**What dissolves these:**
- Speed + dated/same-day/next-day reliability `[CONFIRM]`
- Quality/presentation proof (photos of actual delivered gifts, reviews)
- "Looks like you spent hours / they'll never know you didn't hand-pick it" framing
- Curated/guided selection to kill choice anxiety (gift guides, "perfect for him/her")

## D — LOCAL SELF-PURCHASE SHOPPER FEARS (Self-Purchase, B1)

Practical buyer. Emotion is minimal; fears are about **value, authenticity, and hassle**:

1. **"Am I overpaying? Is it cheaper elsewhere?"** — price/comparison fear (value shopper).
2. **"Is the product genuine / not fake?"** — authenticity fear, esp. electronics, branded goods.
3. **"Is it actually in stock or will they cancel after I pay?"** — fear of phantom inventory.
4. **"How long will delivery take?"** — impatience; fear of long/uncertain waits.
5. **"What if it's damaged or the wrong item?"** — fear of defective/incorrect delivery.
6. **"Can I return it / get a refund / is there warranty?"** — after-sales fear.
7. **"Will my payment be secure?"** — card/payment safety.
8. **"Will I get any support if it goes wrong?"** — fear of being ignored post-purchase.

**What dissolves these:**
- Clear price / value framing, comparison where honest
- Authenticity/warranty signals `[CONFIRM]`
- In-stock + fast-delivery messaging
- Ratings, reviews, return/refund policy `[CONFIRM]`
- Established-platform trust (20+ years, scale)

## CROSS-SEGMENT NOTE FOR THE STRATEGIST

When an ad's job *is* to build trust, the single most powerful move is to **name the fear out loud and then disarm it in the same ad.** People expect ads to ignore their doubts. An ad that says the quiet fear ("Worried it won't reach them in time?") and immediately answers it ("Delivered on the date you choose, island-wide — for 20+ years") builds disproportionate trust. (But this is the *pain-led* play — a positive, broad, or occasion-led ad does a different job and shouldn't be forced into a fear frame.)

- **Diaspora:** lead with the *reach + on-the-day + trust* fears. These are emotional and acute — they are your best message-first angles.
- **Local gifter:** lead with *on-time + quality/presentation + choice anxiety*.
- **Self-purchase:** lead with *value + authenticity + delivery speed*.

Match the proof element in every concept to the specific fear the angle raises. No floating proof — proof must answer a named doubt.
