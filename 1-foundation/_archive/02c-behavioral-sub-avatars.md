# Kapruka — Behavioral Sub-Avatars

> **Purpose:** `02-personas.md` cuts the audience by **emotion** (who they are sending to, what they fear, what they desire). This file cuts the *same* audience by **behaviour** — how they shop: what triggers the purchase, how far ahead they plan, how they treat price, whether they've bought before, how much they need to verify before they trust. The two layers are meant to be **combined**, not chosen between.
>
> **Why behaviour matters in the Andromeda era:** creative is the targeting. We don't pick the behavioural cohort in Meta's settings — we put a behavioural *cue* in the creative (urgency, a deal frame, a "no reason needed" trigger, a first-order trust stack) and the right behaviour sorts itself in. A behavioral sub-avatar tells you **which cue to pull**, the emotional persona tells you **which pain to name.** Together they write a sharper ad than either alone.
>
> **The unit:** `Emotional persona (A1.2 — diaspora parent → own child) × Behavioral sub-avatar (BH2 — Last-Second Scrambler)` = "a guilt-loaded diaspora parent who left the birthday gift to the last minute." That intersection writes a *completely* different ad than the same persona who is a BH1 Calendar Planner.
>
> **Status:** v1 — written as hypotheses. Every "Observable signal" is `[CONFIRM]` until validated against real Kapruka analytics (session behaviour, cart data, repeat-purchase cadence, payment origin, support tickets). Do not treat these as measured facts yet.

---

## THE BEHAVIORAL AXES (the dimensions every sub-avatar is built from)

A behavioral sub-avatar is a recognisable *position* on these axes. Read a real buyer's behaviour against them to place them.

| Axis | Runs from … | … to |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Occasion-locked (a date forces it) | Self-initiated (impulse / habit / a need) |
| **Planning horizon** | Plans well ahead | Acts at the last second |
| **Price posture** | Deal-led, compares, waits for offers | Value-neutral / willing to pay for certainty |
| **Purchase history** | First-time buyer | Repeat / loyal · or lapsed |
| **Decision speed** | Impulsive, buys in one session | Deliberative, multi-visit, research-heavy |
| **Verification need** | High anxiety, must be reassured to act | Confident, low friction needed |
| **Payment context** | Overseas card / different time zone | Local, frictionless |

## THE BEHAVIORAL SUB-AVATARS

Each is coded **BH#**. Each lists: the behavioural signature, the observable signals that identify it, the buying pattern, which emotional personas it most overlaps, the **creative cue** (the behavioural lever the ad pulls so the creative sorts this behaviour in), and the **lifecycle move** (the next-best action for this behaviour).

---

### BH1 — The Calendar Planner
- **Signature:** Buys ahead of a known date. Wants certainty more than speed; the win is "I can stop worrying about it."
- **Observable signals `[CONFIRM]`:** Early sessions relative to occasion; browses gift guides; sets/asks about delivery date; often returns seasonally on the same dates.
- **Buying pattern:** Plans ahead · price-aware but certainty > cheap · often repeat-seasonal.
- **Overlaps:** A1.1, A1.4, A2.2 (the conscientious ones).
- **Creative cue:** Lead with **dated, guaranteed delivery** and "choose the date." Calm, in-control tone — not panic. Plan-ahead framing ("sorted, weeks early").
- **Lifecycle move:** Capture the date; reminder before the *next* recurring occasion. High retention potential.

### BH2 — The Last-Second Scrambler
- **Signature:** Remembered late. Panic is the emotion; speed is the only thing that matters.
- **Observable signals `[CONFIRM]`:** High-intent late searches ("fastest delivery", "today/tomorrow"); mobile; fast, single-session checkout; sorts/filters by soonest delivery.
- **Buying pattern:** No planning · price-insensitive in the moment · decisive once reassured it'll arrive in time.
- **Overlaps:** A2.3 (its emotional twin), A1.5, any persona under deadline.
- **Creative cue:** **Speed + reliability as hero.** "Forgot? Still arrives tomorrow." Urgency, a hard time-promise, one-tap simplicity. `[CONFIRM delivery window]`
- **Lifecycle move:** Convert fast, then re-market as a BH1 Planner for next time ("beat the panic — plan the next one early").

### BH3 — The Impulse "Just Because" Sender
- **Signature:** No occasion, no deadline. Scrolls, feels something, sends. Emotionally triggered, not calendar-triggered.
- **Observable signals `[CONFIRM]`:** Social-referred entry; low-intent landing; short path to a low-consideration item; no occasion in the order.
- **Buying pattern:** Self-initiated · low deliberation · moderate price ceiling · often repeatable if delighted.
- **Overlaps:** General gifting (Kelly's W25-16/17 territory), A1.3, A2.1.
- **Creative cue:** **Emotional hook + low friction + permission.** "You don't need a reason." Scroll-stopping feeling, minimal thinking required.
- **Lifecycle move:** This is the buyer you *create* demand in. Nurture into a habit (BH5).

### BH4 — The Deal Hunter / Comparison Shopper
- **Signature:** Price is the decision. Compares, waits for offers, distrusts the "first price," abandons and returns.
- **Observable signals `[CONFIRM]`:** Multiple visits before buying; sorts by price; coupon/discount searches; cart abandonment then return on a promo.
- **Buying pattern:** Deal-led · deliberative · multi-visit · low loyalty unless value is proven.
- **Overlaps:** B1.1 (its emotional twin), value-conscious gifters.
- **Creative cue:** **Honest value clarity** — real price, honest comparison, "no buyer's remorse." NOT fake-scarcity "SALE!!!"; this buyer is allergic to it. Trust the price *and* the platform.
- **Lifecycle move:** Win on value once, then convert distrust into loyalty with consistent fair pricing.

### BH5 — The Loyal Repeat Sender (high LTV)
- **Signature:** Sends regularly, often to the same recipient(s). Kapruka is already a habit. The most valuable behaviour in the system.
- **Observable signals `[CONFIRM]`:** Repeat orders; saved addresses/recipients; predictable cadence; account/login behaviour.
- **Buying pattern:** Habitual · low price-sensitivity · high trust already earned.
- **Overlaps:** A1.1, A1.2, A1.4 (ongoing relationships).
- **Creative cue:** **Ease + reorder + "keep showing up."** Don't re-sell trust they already have; reduce friction and celebrate the habit. Reorder, saved recipients, "their next moment is coming."
- **Lifecycle move:** Protect and expand — more occasions, more recipients. Do NOT waste prospecting spend re-acquiring them; this is retention, not acquisition.

### BH6 — The Anxious First-Timer
- **Signature:** Never bought. High doubt, especially diaspora. The order is a leap of faith. Trust must be *built* before they'll act.
- **Observable signals `[CONFIRM]`:** Long sessions; reads reviews / FAQ / returns / "is it legit"; cart abandonment at the payment step; first-time visitor.
- **Buying pattern:** Deliberative · verification-hungry · one cautious first order, then either churns or becomes BH5.
- **Overlaps:** Any persona's first purchase; most acute for A1 diaspora (can't verify from abroad).
- **Creative cue:** **Proof stack — the full trust load.** Reviews from people like them, "20+ years," delivery proof, secure/known payment, "better than sending money." Name the fear, disarm it. (Kelly's W25-15 is built for exactly this behaviour.)
- **Lifecycle move:** The whole job is the *first* successful order. Nail it → onboard toward BH5.

### BH7 — The Category Specialist (specific-need)
- **Signature:** Came for one thing in one category and knows roughly what they want. Goal-directed, not browsing.
- **Observable signals `[CONFIRM]`:** Direct category entry; search-led; spec/brand filters; little cross-category browsing.
- **Buying pattern:** Need-based · moderate deliberation · wants selection + availability + authenticity.
- **Overlaps:** B1.3 (its emotional twin); also occasion gifters who've decided on a SKU type (e.g. "a cake").
- **Creative cue:** **Product-first: selection, in-stock, genuine, fast.** Show the category depth and the specific item clearly; authenticity/warranty signals. Less emotion, more "the right one, in stock, delivered."
- **Lifecycle move:** Cross-sell adjacent needs; convert single-category into multi-category trust.

### BH8 — The Obligation Discharger
- **Signature:** *Has* to send something appropriate (wedding, funeral, boss, in-laws). Low emotional investment, high "don't get it wrong" pressure. Wants it handled tastefully and *done*.
- **Observable signals `[CONFIRM]`:** "What's appropriate" searches; reliance on curated/occasion guides; one-off, occasion-locked orders; little browsing beyond the guide.
- **Buying pattern:** Occasion-locked · wants guidance · will pay to remove the decision burden.
- **Overlaps:** A1.5, A2.4 (its emotional twins).
- **Creative cue:** **Curated, safe, "the right thing — handled."** Remove choice anxiety with a vetted pick per occasion; tasteful, appropriate, no risk of embarrassment. (Kelly's W25-12 serves this behaviour.)
- **Lifecycle move:** Be the default safe answer; they return for the *next* obligation if the first felt effortless.

### BH9 — The Overseas Payer (diaspora context layer)
- **Signature:** A behavioural *context* more than a personality — paying from abroad on a foreign card, in another time zone, unable to physically verify anything. Sits *under* most A1 personas and amplifies their verification need.
- **Observable signals `[CONFIRM]`:** International payment origin / IP; overseas-to-SL address split; off-hours (SL time) activity; foreign-card friction or declines.
- **Buying pattern:** Variable trigger · certainty > price · payment friction is a real drop-off point.
- **Overlaps:** All of A1; stacks with BH1/BH2/BH6 especially.
- **Creative cue:** **International payment ease + reach proof + delivery confirmation.** "Pay from anywhere, delivered on the day, see it land." Show the from-anywhere-to-anywhere moat explicitly.
- **Lifecycle move:** Remove payment friction (the most fixable diaspora drop-off); once they clear it successfully, retention is strong.

### BH10 — The Lapsed / Win-Back
- **Signature:** Bought before, then went quiet. Trust was earned once but the habit didn't stick (or a bad experience cooled it).
- **Observable signals `[CONFIRM]`:** Prior order(s) + long inactivity; returns via re-engagement, not cold.
- **Buying pattern:** Was a buyer · dormant · needs a reason and a reminder, not a trust rebuild from zero.
- **Overlaps:** Any prior buyer; most valuable to win back from the BH5 pool.
- **Creative cue:** **Reminder + what's new + a reason to return.** "It's been a while — here's the occasion you're about to miss." Warm, not cold-acquisition; assume some trust remains.
- **Lifecycle move:** Re-activate toward BH5; diagnose *why* they lapsed if the pattern is common (feeds the Analyzer/human).

## HOW TO COMBINE THE TWO LAYERS (the operating method)

1. Pick the **emotional persona** from `02-personas.md` (the pain/desire/fear — *what to say*).
2. Pick or infer the **behavioral sub-avatar** from this file (the shopping behaviour — *which cue to pull*).
3. The ad **names the persona's pain** AND **pulls the sub-avatar's creative cue.** The cue is what makes the creative self-select the right behaviour (creative is the targeting).
4. Keep packs clean: one persona × one dominant behavioural cue per pack. Don't build an ad that tries to be urgent (BH2) *and* plan-ahead-calm (BH1) at once — they sort opposite behaviours and the signal muddies.

**Worked example:**
> Persona **A1.2** (diaspora parent → own child, terrified the birthday gift won't arrive on the day) × **BH2** (Last-Second Scrambler) → HOOK names the panic ("Her birthday's tomorrow and you're 4,000 km away"), the cue is **speed + dated guarantee** ("Order tonight — on her table by morning"). Same persona × **BH1** (Planner) → calmer hook ("Her birthday's in two weeks. Sort it tonight, stop worrying"), cue is **plan-ahead certainty.** One persona, two behaviours, two different ads.

## HOW EACH ROLE USES THIS FILE

- **Strategist** — after picking the persona, names the **target behavioral sub-avatar(s)** for each concept and pulls that cue. The behavioural cue is now part of the concept, not an afterthought. (One concept = one persona × one dominant behavioural cue.)
- **Copywriter** — checks the hook/support actually carries the sub-avatar's cue (urgency reads urgent; value reads honest-not-shouty; planner reads calm). Wrong cue = right words, wrong behaviour sorted.
- **Evaluator** — fidelity check: does the creative name a specific behavioral sub-avatar's cue, or is it behaviourally vague? A behaviourally generic ad sorts no one cleanly.
- **Kelly (content)** — ties each content idea to a behavioral sub-avatar as well as a persona, so organic experiments map cleanly onto the same behavioural language the paid track validates against.
- **Analyzer / Memory (when built)** — record results by **persona × behavioral sub-avatar**, so the system learns *which behaviour* a winning angle actually converted, not just which persona.
