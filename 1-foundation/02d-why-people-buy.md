# WHY PEOPLE BUY — Buying-Psychology Reference

> **What this is.** An external reference digest of the core psychological reasons people buy, synthesised from five marketing-psychology sources. It is *not* Kapruka source-of-truth (that lives in `01`, `02`, `02b`, `02c`) — it is a **lens** the Strategist (and downstream the Creative Director) applies when finding the insight: which universal buying driver a persona's pain maps onto, and which doubt to disarm.
>
> **How to use it.** Personas (`02`) tell you *what* the buyer feels. This file tells you *why anyone buys at all* — the motivational engine underneath. Pair them: take a persona's dominant emotion and name the buying driver(s) it pulls (e.g. the overseas gifter → **Fear/Reassurance + Aspirational identity**, disarmed by **trust/risk-reduction**). Every Kapruka angle should ride at least one driver below and disarm at least one barrier.
>

## THE BIG TRUTH (the one to internalise)

**People buy on emotion, then justify with logic.** An estimated **~95% of purchase decisions are subconscious** (Zaltman, Harvard). The buyer feels first — desire, fear, pride, relief, belonging — and *afterward* reaches for the rational reason ("it was a good deal," "I needed it"). So the creative's job is to trigger the feeling; the features exist to give permission, not to persuade.

> **Strategist takeaway:** Brief the *feeling and the identity* first. Features and proof are the rationalisation scaffold — name them, but never lead with them.

## THE CORE BUYING DRIVERS (what to *say*)

### 1. Necessity / Solve a real problem
People buy genuine needs with little hesitation. The strongest position is "must-have," not "nice-to-have." Connect the offer to a concrete, daily problem and show it solved.
- *Use:* Frame the product as the answer to a problem the persona already feels.

### 2. Save time & money
Buyers reward things that make life cheaper or faster. Efficiency and cost-saving are evergreen, low-friction reasons to act.
- *Use:* Make the saving conspicuous (and honest). Quantify when you can.

### 3. Reduce hassle / remove friction
People pay to make a frustration *go away*. Naming the everyday pain and showing exactly how it's eliminated converts — especially for anything logistically stressful.
- *Use (high relevance for Kapruka):* The whole point of reliable island-wide delivery is removing the hassle and helplessness of "I can't be there." Lead with the relief.

### 4. Emotional gratification / joy
Many purchases simply *feel good* — they deliver joy, fulfilment, or a moment of pleasure. Gifting lives here almost entirely.
- *Use:* Sell the moment, the reaction, the warmth — not the SKU.

### 5. Look & feel better (self-improvement)
People buy to fix a perceived shortcoming and feel better about themselves. Purchases attach to identity and self-image.
- *Use:* Position as an investment in a better self / a better version of the moment.

### 6. Desire to transform
A close cousin of self-improvement: buyers want a meaningful *change* — a before→after, an upgrade in their life or status.
- *Use:* Tell the transformation story; show the gap closed.

### 7. Social status & prestige / belonging
People buy to signal who they are and to belong to a group they admire. ~72% of premium buyers cite emotional satisfaction (pride/status) over function.
- *Use:* Let the product be a statement about the buyer's identity and standing.

### 8. Aspirational identity
Buying bridges the gap between the current self and the idealised self — the person, life, or values the buyer aspires to.
- *Use:* Associate the product with who the buyer *wants to be* (the thoughtful family member, the reliable provider abroad).

### 9. Curiosity & exploration
Novelty and the thrill of discovery pull people in. Intrigue and "what's this?" can open a sale that a flat benefit can't.
- *Use:* Tease; create an open loop; make the offer feel like a discovery.

## THE PERSUASION LEVERS (what makes them *act now*)

### 10. Social proof
People trust other people far more than they trust the seller. ~92% trust peer recommendations over branded content; ~93% read reviews before buying.
- *Use:* Real testimonials, recipient reactions, "thousands trust us," visible track record.

### 11. Trust & security (the conversion floor)
For high-value or long-distance purchases, the buyer must feel safe *before* anything else converts. ~83% need emotional validation (reviews/reputation) before a significant purchase.
- *Use (Kapruka's moat):* 20+ years, dated reliable delivery, "it will arrive, on time, as promised." Trust is the asset — protect it with honest claims only.

### 12. Perceived value over price
People want a *good deal*, not necessarily the cheapest. Anchoring, bundles, and free add-ons make a purchase feel like a win (buyers spend up to ~67% more when they feel they're getting an exclusive deal).
- *Use:* Frame value, not just price. Anchor against the alternative (or the cost of getting it wrong).

### 13. FOMO / scarcity / urgency
Fear of missing out drives a large share of impulse buys (~60%). Limited time, limited stock, "before it's gone" compels immediate action.
- *Use:* Honest deadlines and genuine scarcity (occasion cut-offs, seasonal windows) — never manufactured.

### 14. Lower risk / increase access (disarm the barrier)
Uncertainty kills purchases. Guarantees, easy returns, clear pricing, and visible accessibility remove the "what if it goes wrong?" objection.
- *Use:* Pre-empt the specific doubt (see `02b-fears-objections.md`) and neutralise it on the creative.

### 15. Personalisation & connection
Buyers bond with brands that feel authentic, human, and made-for-them. ~80% are more likely to buy from a brand offering a personalised experience.
- *Use:* Speak to the specific person and occasion; warmth and a human voice over generic polish.

## FORBES (YEC) — THE FOUR MACRO-CATEGORIES *(reconstructed)*

A useful top-level sort that every driver above falls under:

1. **Need-driven** — practical, utilitarian, problem-solving purchases (maps to #1–#3).
2. **Impulse / emotional** — spontaneous, feeling-led, minimal deliberation (maps to #4, #9, #13).
3. **Fear-based** — FOMO, risk-avoidance, reassurance-seeking (maps to #11, #13, #14).
4. **Aspirational / identity** — self-image, status, who-I-want-to-be (maps to #5–#8).

> *Note: Forbes & PandoraFMS were not directly fetchable; these macro-categories and several stats are corroborated across the other sources and flagged as reconstructed.*
